Part 10 - Breeds of Animals (in Post-Purāṇic Sanskrit Works)

[Full title: Animal Husbandry in Post-Purāṇic Sanskrit Works and its Relevance with the Purāṇas (1) Breeds of Animals]

The Arthaśāstra mentions different kinds of domesticated animals such as horse, donkey, camel, buffalo, goat, sheep and pig etc.[1] The horses belonging to the breed of Kamboja, Sindhu, Araṭṭa and Vanāyu countries were the best horses for use in battle etc; those of Vāhlīka, Pāpeya, Sauvīra and Taitala region were of middle quantity. The rest of the breeds were considered ordinary.[2] Regarding the quality of horses it is mentioned in the Śukranītisāra that those horses are good which run with legs thrown from a height, whose movements are like those of tigers, peacocks, ducks, parrots, pigeons, deer, camels, monkeys and bulls.[3]

In case of bulls it is stated by Śukra that the bull which is neither idle nor wicked but good for carrying load with a well-formed body and a good back is the best of all.[4] Reference of camels is also met with in the Śukranītisāra. It is mentioned there that the camel which is strong-built, has a good face, carries burden and goes thirty yojanas (1 yojana=4 miles[5]) a day is appreciated.[6] The bull and the camels should be governed by strings with which the nose can be pulled.[7]

The Śukranītisāra mentions four classes of elephants and their characteristics. The four classes according to Śukranītisāra are—

Bhadra,

Manda,

Mṛga and

Miśra.[8]

The elephants whose tusks are like the colour of honey, which are strong and well-formed, round and fat in body, have good face and excellent limbs are called as Bhadra class of elephants.[9] The elephant which has fat belly, lion-like eyes, thick skin, throat and trunk, medium limbs and a long body is known as Manda elephant.[10] The Mṛga elephant is that which has small or short throat, tusks, ears and trunk, big eyes and very short lips.[11] The Miśra elephant has the above said characteristics in mixture.[12]

The Arthaśāstra classifies elephants into four groups according to the training they are given. They are:

1) the elephants which are tameable (damya);
2) the elephants which are trained for war (sānnāhya);
3) the elephants which are trained for riding (aupavāhya); and
4) the rogue elephants (vyāla).[13]

According to the Arthaśāstra, the elephant which is seven aratnis in height, nine aratnis in length, ten aratnis in circumference, and is 40 years old is considered the best.[14] The Bṛhatsaṃhitā mentions the signs of cows[15], dogs[16], goats[17], horses[18] and elephants[19] in details. In the Purāṇas too we find the references of breeds of animals such as horses, cows, bulls, elephants etc.

In case of horses four varieties are mentioned. They are—

bhadra,

manda,

mṛga and

saṅkīrṇa.[20]

In case of elephants also there are four varieties. They are mentioned in the Matsya Purāṇa as—

Kumuda,

Añjana,

Vāmana and

Airāvata.[21]

Various types of bulls are mentioned. They are—

Nandīmukha,

Samudra and

Nīla Vṛṣabha.[22]

Moreover, mention of goats, sheep, buffaloes, camels, dogs is met with in the Purāṇas.

Animal Breeding:

The people fully knew the necessities of number of males of different animal species per herd of one hundred females of different types of domestic animals for the purpose of breeding. Kauṭilya in his Arthaśāstra mentions that in a herd of one hundred asses and mules there shall be five male animals and in the case of herds of goats and sheep there shall be ten males per hundred. For a herd of ten heads of cows or buffaloes there shall be four male animals each.[23]
