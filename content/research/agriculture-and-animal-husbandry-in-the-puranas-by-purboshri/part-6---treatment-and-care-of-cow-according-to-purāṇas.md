Part 6 - Treatment and Care of Cow (according to Purāṇas)

The Agni Purāṇa discusses the diseases of cow and their remedies in detail.They are as follows:

Swelling of the stomach (udarabṛṃhaṇa):

The remedy for it is turmeric (haridrā), the bark of the rājavṛkṣa, lavaṇa and the lodhraka. If this is drunk by the cow, the cure is certain.[1]
Diseases of horn (śṛṅgāmaya): Oil prepared with a decoction of śṛṅgavera, balā, powdered māṃsa and makṣikā, would be favourable in this case.[2] Infection of the ears (karṇaśūla): In such diseases, oil boiled with the essence of mañjiṣṭhā, hiṅgu (Asafetida) and saindhava salt would be the best remedy.[3]

Toothache (dantaśūla):

A plaster composed of roots of bilva, apāmārga, dhātakī, pāṭala and kuṭaja, applied over the gums would be favourable in toothache.[4]

Infection of cavity of the mouth (mukharoga):

Above mentioned drugs boiled with clarified butter should be used when cavity of the mouth gets affected.[5] Diseases related to tongue (jihvāroga): Same above mentioned drugs mixed with saindhava salt would be a good remedy in case of this disease.[6] Inflammation of the throats (galagraha), cardites and pain in the lower back(hṛcchūle bastiśūle ca) and rheumatic complaints(vātaroga): In such type of diseases, the remedy should be the essence of śṛṅgavera, haridrā and triphalā.[7] Dysentery (atīsāra): The juice of trifalā and ghṛta should be mixed with the drink of a cow.[8]

All diseases of digestive organs, cough (kāsa) and asthma (śvāsa): The juice of śṛṅgavera and bhārṅgī should be given when the cow suffers from these diseases.[9]

Feeding and Nourishment:

A calf should be given a food which consists of māṣa, tila, godhūma, payaḥ, kṣīra and ghṛta (clarified butter), mixed with lavaṇa, to develope well.[10] To increase the milk of a cow small amount of food prepared from aśvagandhā and tila would be beneficial.[11] The Agni Purāṇa states that to give a morsel of grass to the cows(go-grāsa) was considered the daily duties of a householder. After go-grāsa and performing the ritual svastyayana and after worshipping the guests, the householder should take his food.[12] It is said that one who gives a small amount of food everyday to a cow, is sure to go to heaven after death. A man, who does anything for the welfare of this animal, goes to the region of Brahmā, after death.[13]

Preservation of Cow-Life:

Precautionary and curative measures have been elaborated in the Purāṇas for the treatment of diseased animals.These included

1) Fumigation of the cow house with the vapours of viṣā plants to make the cattle strong.[14]

2) Fumigation of the cow-house with the vapours of devadāru, vacā, māṃsī, guggula, hiṅgu and sarṣapa mixed together to keep safe from the evil effects of malignant planets.[15]
