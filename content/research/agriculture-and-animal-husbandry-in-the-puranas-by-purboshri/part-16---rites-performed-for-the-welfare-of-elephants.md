Part 16 - Rites Performed for the Welfare of Elephants

As the elephants are great glory of the army; therefore, it was necessary to perform the pacificatory rites for them. The pacificatory ceremony for the elephants (gaja-śānti) is described in details in the Agni Purāṇa. The gaja-śānti is for the health and welfare of the elephants. Describing the rite of bringing peace on elephants, Śālihotra says that the god Viṣṇu, goddess Lakṣmī, the elephant Airāvāta, Brahmā, Śaṃkara, Indra, Vaiśravaṇa, Yama, Candra, Arka, Varuṇa, Vāyu, Agni, Pṛthivī and the elephants of the heaven, such as Kumuda, Airāvāta, Padma, Puṣpadanta, Vāmana, Supratīka and Añjana should be propitiated with oblations of clarified butter and then dakṣiṇā should be paid to the Brāhmaṇas.[1] The elephants are beneficial for prosperity if thus sprinkled over by purificatory water. In the Naimittika form of the worship, Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī should be worshipped at the centre of the lotus shaped diagram which should be traced outside the city.The Gods Brahmā, Bhāskara, Pṛthivī, Skanda, Ananta, Heaven, Śiva, Soma and Indra should be worshipped at the petals together with their weapons.On the edge of the outer circle of the diagram the celestial weapons such as vajra, śakti, daṇḍa, tomara, pāśa, gadā, śūla and padma should be worshipped.At the center the Sun and Aśvins should be worshipped. At the petals the eight Vasus and the Sādhyas, Aṅgirasa, Bhṛgu, Maruts, Viśvedeva and Rudra should be worshipped.[2]

The gods should be invoked and worshipped on the edge of the outer surface of the circle.The sages, the streams and the mountains, the great elements should be worshipped at the corner of the diagram.Padma, cakra, gadā and śaṅkha should also be invoked in order to protect the four gateway of the sacrificial shed. Pitcher full of water should be stored tidily in each of the doorway.[3] The elephants of heaven such as Airāvata etc. should be invoked to protect the four gateway of the maṇḍala.They should be worshipped and libations of clarified butter should be poured hundred times on the sacrificial fire, in honour of each of the deities who were invoked.Then the performer of the rite should circumbulate the heavenly elephants and Agni.At last Dakṣiṇās should be paid to the Brāhmaṇas and the physicians of elephants(Hastivaidya).[4]

Then the priest who is familiar with the influences of stars and the significance of the time (kālavit), should ride on a female elephant and addressing her as the queen of elephant speak to her that he has performed this pacificatory rite.The king has made her the chief of his stable and he will worship her with flower-garlands and perfumes. Obeying the order of the king the people will also worship her.The elephant may protect the king in battle and home. Giving up her animal nature she may remember her divine origin.The gods created the heavenly elephants on the occasion of the war between them and the demons.Again, Ariṣṭa, who was the son of Airāvata derived the strength and the quality of showing good judgement from his divine father. Developing all these qualities the elephant may protect the king in battle.[5]
Consecrating the elephant thus, the king should ride on its back and the warriors with their weapons should follow him. Again a mystical diagram should be placed on the ground of the elephant shed and the Dikpālas should be worshipped at its centre. Moreover, Godess Earth and Sarasvatī, Bala, Nāga should also be worshipped at its petals. The hand-drum should be worshipped and flowers, perfumes and garlands should also be offered to it.Libations in honour of the gods, should be poured on the fire. Pitchers full of juice should be given to a Brāhmaṇa.The superintendent of the elephants, the groom and the astrologer should be worshipped. Then the hand-drum should be given to the superintendent of the elephants who by beating it would greet his king with the continuous deep sound.[6]
