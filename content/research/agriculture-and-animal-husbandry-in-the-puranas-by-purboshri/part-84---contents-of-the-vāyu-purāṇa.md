## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Contents of the Vayu Purana’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 8.4 - Contents of the Vāyu Purāṇa

The Vāyu Purāṇa obtains its name from Vāyu who is its promulgator. M.Winternitz in his A History of Indian Literature says that this Purāṇa appears in some lists, under the name of Śaiva or Śiva Purāṇa, a title which is given to the work because it is dedicated to the worship of god Śiva. As proclaimed by the Wind-god, Vāyu Purāṇa is quoted in the Mahābhārata as well as by the Harivaṃśa. It has been mentioned that the poet Bāṇa (about 625 A.D.) had read the Vāyu Purāṇa and that in this Purāṇa, the rule of the Guptas is described as it was in the 4th century A.D., making the work not later than 5th century A.D.[1]

The Vāyu Purāṇa itself declares that it was illustrated by Vāyu (Mātariśvā).[2] It was recited to the Brāhmaṇas by Vāyu.[3] It was expanded by Vāyu himself, who was employed in the beneficence of the people. He described it to the sages in the Naimiṣa forest.[4] In another verse, the Vāyu Purāṇa states that this Purāṇa was spoken by Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, who was the expounder of Brahman.[5] Again it is stated that previously this Purāṇa had been stated by Brahmā.[6] This sacred text was presented by Brahmā to Mātariśvā(Vāyu). From him it was acquired by Uśanas, from Uśanas it was acquired by Bṛhaspati, Bṛhaspati then spoke it to Savitṛ. Savitṛ passed it to Mṛtyu(Yama), Mṛtyu passed it to Indra, Indra passed it to Vasiṣṭha and Vasiṣṭha passed it to Sārasvata. Sārasvata passed it to Tridhāmā, Tridhāmā passed it to Śaradvata, Śaradvata passed it to Triviṣṭa and Triviṣṭa passed it to Antarikṣa.

Antarikṣa passed it to Varṣiṇ, Varṣiṇ passed it to Trayyāruṇa, Trayyāruṇa passed it to Dhanañjaya and he gave it to Kṛtañjaya. From Kṛtañjaya it was received by Tṛṇañjaya, he passed it to Bharadvāja, Bharadvāja passed it on to Gautama and Gautama gave it to Niryantara. Niryantara handed it over to Vājaśravas, Vājaśravas passed it on to Somaśuṣma, who gave it to Tṛṇabindu. Tṛṇabindu gave it to Dakṣa, Dakṣa passed it to Śakti, from Śakti it was heard by Parāśara, who was even in embryo then. From Parāśara it was received by Jātukarṇa, he passed it to Dvaipāyana and from Dvaipāyana, it was expanded to Śāṃśapāyana.[7]

The Vāyu Purāṇa elaborates the spirits of manes (Pitṛs) and their cults (Śrāddhas).[8] It’s one chapter is devoted to the art of song.[9] Although it is a Śaiva Purāṇa, but we find two chapters in this Purāṇa, even on Viṣṇu too.[10] Moreover, in the Gayā Māhātmya section, a great tribute is paid to Viṣṇu.[11]

The whole Vāyu Purāṇa is divided into two parts(Kāṇḍa).The second Kāṇḍa begins with the 62nd chapter.[12] There are one hundred and twelve chapters in this Purāṇa. It has twenty-three thousand Ślokas.[13]

This Purāṇa is divided into four Pādas—

Prakriyā Pāda,

Ānuṣaṅga Pāda,

Upodghāta Pāda, and

Upasaṃhāra Pāda.[14]

Leaving out the two opening chapters, the first section (Prakriyā Pāda) occupies only four chapters, with the general title of sṛṣṭiprakaraṇaṃ or the creation of world.[15]

The second section (Ānusaṅga Pāda) comprises 58 chapters of the book, beginning with the 7th chapter and ending with the 64th chapter.[16] It is the longest among the four sections. Creation of gods and men, cycles of Manus, practice of Yoga, descriptions of thirty-three Kalpas of the duration of the world(the last one of these kalpas i.e., Śveta Kalpa, in which we are now said to have), incarnations of Mahādeva, and the curse of Dakṣa, descendants of Ṛṣis, of Agni and other Devas, of Svāyambhuva Manu, of Prajāpati, of Pṛthu, the descriptions of Jambudvīpa, the four yugas, the sacred tīrthas, the continents of Ketumāla, Bhāratavarṣa, Kiṃpuruṣa and Plakṣa are also met with in this section.

The third section (Upodghāta Pāda) contains 34 chapters (65-99).[17] This section gives the description of the races of Prajāpati, Kāśyapa, Varuṇa, Vaivasvata Manu, Viṣṇu, and the Gandharvas. Moreover, there are accounts of Śrāddhas to which more than ten chapters are devoted. Descendants of Soma and Yadu race and the dynasties of kali age are also met with there.

The last section (Upasaṃhāra Pāda) consists of chapters 100 to the end.[18] Periods of seven Manus[19] are described here. Moreover, descriptions of periodical destructions and regenerations[20] have also been elaborated. The concluding chapters on the greatness of Gayā seem to be out of place and indicate a definite interpolation.[21] The Vāyu Purāṇa states that, a Brāhmaṇa, who may understand the four Vedas along with the six Vedāṅgas and Upaniṣads, but if he does not understand this Purāṇa then he may be called as an inexpert.[22]
