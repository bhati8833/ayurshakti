Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

Part 18 - Rainfall, the Support of Agriculture that Depends upon the Sun

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa speaks out that due to rainfall the crops grow and by crops the whole world survives. During the eight months of the year the Sun sucks the waters, and during the remaining four months, he pours them on earth in the form of rain.[1] The water which the clouds shed upon earth gives fertility to the plants and by the support of plants the living beings exist. By this way, from rain, all the vegetables grow.[2] Men, through these products give sustenance to the gods during the performance of daily sacrifices.[3] And thus, the whole world including the sacrifices, the Vedas, the four castes, the Brāhmaṇas, and all the animals are supported by the rains by which food is produced.[4] The Sun is the source of subsistence for all.Because, the moisture of the earth, which the Sun attracts by his rays, is again handed over by him for the fertilization of the grain and by grains the world survives.[5] The Viṣṇu Purāṇa enjoins that the world depends upon the Sun for rainfall.The Sun with his burning rays sucks up the moisture of the earth, and with them nourishes the Moon.[6]

The Vāyu Purāṇa speaks that the water swallowed by the Sun gets transmitted to the Moon from the fire of the Sun.Then by the Nāḍīs (tubular veins), full of wind, the action of sustaining the world begins.[7] The Viṣṇu Purāṇa further adds that the Moon converses, its dews to the clouds, which are composed of smoke, fire, and wind or vapour, through tubes of air, and can, keep hold of the waters, with which the clouds are charged.[8] The Matsya Purāṇa attests that the rays of the Sun drag out water from the ocean with the help of air.But by means of white rays he gets hold of rain from the clouds in appropriate seasons.The waters from the vapours of the clouds when get in touch with the wind, fall down in the form of rain.[9] The Viṣṇu Purāṇa also adjoins that when and however the clouds are broken to pieces by the wind, and then watery stores fall down in process of time.[10] The Vāyu Purāṇa speaks that the clouds by the impact of wind release waters on the ground.[11] As the contents of the clouds are not dissolved, therefore, they are called Abhra.[12] According to the Vāyu Purāṇa and the Matsya Purāṇa, the root mih means ‘to exude’ or ‘to sprinkle’. Since the clouds exude water, so they are called Megha.[13]

The Vāyu Purāṇa enumerates three types of clouds that are associated with rainfall. They are—

Āgneya,

Brahmaja and

Pakṣaja.[14]

The same Purāṇa points out that the universe is supported by the Moon as the water of the universe in whole filters down from the Moon in the sky.[15]

The heat generates from the Sun and the coldness gets into being, from the Moon. Thus, these two have the power to sustain the universe.[16] The Viṣṇu Purāṇa too adds that the Moon with its cooling rays nourishes the vegetables with the cool nectary atoms (containing water), it sheds upon them and through their development it sustains living beings.[17] The Matsya Purāṇa discloses that the Sun always pleases men by producing herbs.[18] It imparts strength in the herbs and plants.[19] During spring and hot weather the Sun passes on heat by his three hundred rays, during the rainy and autumn season causes rain by his four hundred rays and during the dewy and winter season communicates cold by his three hundred rays.[20]
