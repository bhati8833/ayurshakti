Part 15 - Major crops and Staple food

[Full title: Agriculture in the Modern Age of 20-21st Century (1) Major Crops]

Rice is the staple food of nearly three-fourth of the population in India. Its cultivation goes back to the unrecorded ages extending beyond 3,000 B. C. The customary use of rice in the religious ceremonies of the Hindus, which are associated with birth, marriage and death, is evidence to its great antiquity.[1] In India, generally three crops of paddy are raised in a year: autumn, winter and summer. Of these, the winter crop is the most important, reporting for about 90 percent of the total; the autumn crop accounts for less than 10 percent, and the summer crop constitutes the negligible balance.

The sowing and harvesting seasons of the three crops are as follows:[2]

Other food crops grown in India are wheat, millet, barley, maize, pulses, potato, etc. Various kind of oilseeds such as ground nut, mustard, sesamum, castor, linseed, safflower, etc. and fibre crops such as cotton, jute etc. are also grown in India. In Assam, among the cereals, rice is the single most important crop followed very remotely by wheat and maize.[3]

Agriculture in Assam depends upon rainfall. For this reason and for the agro-climatic difference, in Assam, there are three distinct rice-growing seasons. They are:

1) Śāli or winter rice(June/July to November/December);
2) Āhu or autumn rice(March/April to June/July) and
3) Boro or summer rice(November/December to May/June).[4]

Major pulses grown in Assam and adjoining north-eastern states are black gram (Vigna mungo) and green gram (Vigna radiata) during kharif and summer seasons and field pea (Pisum sativum), lentil(Lens culinaris) and lathyrus(Lathyrus sativus) during rabi seasons.[5]

In recent years the following plant species were introduced into India[6] :

1) Sunflower (Helianthus annuus); 
2) Tree-tomato (Cyphomandra betacea); 
3) Spearmint (Mentha arvensis); 
4) Kiwifruit (Actindia chinensis); 
5) Gum-arabic (Acacia senegal);

In the Purāṇas also various kinds of grains are mentioned. People in the days of the Purāṇas could recognize the crops. Mention of seventeen kinds of grāmya oṣadhis and fourteen kinds of yajñīya oṣadhis is met with in the Purāṇas.[7] Some of the agricultural crops mentioned in the Purāṇas are-tila[8] dhānya[9] (eighteen sorts of grain), vrīhi[10] (a kind of paddy), śyāmāka[11] saktu[12] (the flour of grain), nīvāra[13] (a kind of corn for śrāddha), māṣa[14] (a kind of grain), masūra, yava and godhūma[15] etc.
