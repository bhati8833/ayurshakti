Part 5 - Successful Production Depends on the Fertility of the Soil

Kāśyapa points out the importance of good soil stating thus-a good quality land yields good results to everyone, confers good health on the family and causes growth of money, cattle and grain.[1]

He further mentions that a good soil—

(i) should be devoid of bones and stones,[2]  
(ii) should be a plastic clay, with reddish and black colour, full of potency and glossy with water,[3]
(iii) should not be too deep or shallow,[4]
(iv) should be conducive to speedy seedling emergence,[5]
(v) should be easy for ploughing and other operations,[6]
(vi) should easily absorb moisture and should be inhabited with beneficial living creatures;[7] and
(vii) should have a substantial mass.[8]

With regard to the fertility of the soil, it is mentioned in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa that if the seeds are sown in the same plot of land, time and again without manuring, the land would lose its fertility and consequently the seeds would get destroyed[9].

Cakrapāṇi Miśra in his Viśvavallabha mentions three types of soil-arid, wet and ordinary. It is differentiated on the basis of six types of taste.[10]

According to him soil is of six colours—

dark gray,

pallid,

black,

white,

red and

yellow.

Their tastes are-sweet, sour, saline, bitter, pungent and astringent respectively.[11] The land which is soft and water is available nearby is good for plantation. Therefore, gardens should be planned on such locations.[12]

Śārṅgadhara in the Upavana-Vinoda too mentions three main types of soil, viz.

Jāṅgala (barren),

Anūpa (moist) and

Sāmānya (ordinary).

They are divided into six on the basis of colour and taste.[13] Soils having black, pale, blue, red, yellow and white colour are correlated with sweet, acid, salty, sour, bitter and pungent tastes respectively.[14] All kinds of trees flourish in the soil, near which there is water and where the sprouts of trees put on green appearance.[15]

The Agni Purāṇa also mention various types of grounds. They are—

white coloured,

blood coloured,

blood smelling ground and

yellow coloured ground.[16]

Again [in the Agni Purāṇa, the following] are also mentioned.—

golden soil,

sandy soil,

blue soil,

yellow soil,

white soil and

yellowish red soil.[17]
