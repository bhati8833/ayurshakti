Part 24 - Agriculture and its Related Concepts in the Proposed Purāṇas

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa mentions the four branches of knowledge-logical, scriptural, practical and political. Agriculture, commerce and tending of cattle; the knowledge of these three professions constitutes practical Science. Agriculture is the subsistence of farmers.[1] In the Tretā age, there grew the plants in agricultural produce.[2]

In the Agni Purāṇa the Goddess Earth is prayed as the possessor of all seeds, all gems and all cereals.[3] The Agni Purāṇa has described the synonymous words of agricultural products and its related terms in the chapter 366. The term kaṇiśa and śasyamañjarī are used in the sense of the ear of paddy etc. cereals[4], dhānya, vrīhi and stambakari in the sense of common cereals,[5] māṣa etc. (mudga, rājamāṣa, kulattha, canaka, tila, kākaṇḍa and cīvara) are used in the sense of śamī cereal (dhānya), barley (cereal bearing tūṇḍa) etc. in the sense of śūka cereal, and nīvāra is used in the sense of tṛṇadhānya.[6] Yugya and prāsaṃgya are used in the sense of bull being yoked, śākaṭa in the sense of bull pulling the cart.[7] Mṛt and mṛttikā are used in the sense of clay and mṛtsā and mṛtsnā are used in the sense of fertile soil.[8]

Among the duties of a king the foremost is that, a good king should maintain or protect agriculture.[9] The provinces should be largely inhabited by the Śūdras.[10] The inhabitants of the provinces should live by agriculture[11] and abound in cattle.[12] The country should be safe against all foreign invasions abounding in grains.[13] In reference to the ways of protecting the fort and the city of a king, Matsya said in the Matsya Purāṇa that within the fortress, agricultural products such as all kinds of grain, barley, wheat, all kinds of pulse such as aconite leaved kidney been, green grain, gram, sesamum and grain-dust,etc. should be kept in stock in abundance.[14]

Auspiciousness of cereals and paddies are mentioned in the Agni Purāṇa. It is stated that cereals and paddies, except those of the black species are auspicious and ensure the success of one’s mission, when looked at by a man at the time of leaving house.[15] Other grains such as barley, wheat, sesamum, green gram and black gram, when they are consecrated by the offerings, they are dear to Viṣṇu.[16] While enumerating the sixty different Samvatsaras, the Agni Purāṇa states that a good harvest is usually reaped and gathered in and the earth overflows in joy in the year named as Śukra.[17] Corn grows abundant in the year named as Vikrama.[18] The year Pramāthī is characterized by a moderate rain-fall.[19] The year Pārthiva is marked by the abundance of grain and food-stuff, while the year named Jaya is characterized by excessive rainfall.[20] God Indra (rain-god) is considered the presiding deity of all the cereals.[21] Various sorts of paddy grow in a year belonging to the class of Ananam.[22] Again the Dundubhi class is marked by abundance of paddy.[23] Rainfall becomes abundant in a year of the Piṅgala class. Again there is a moderate rainfall in a year of the Durmati class.[24]

The Purāṇas describe the quantity of food grains and articles that are to be collected when natural phenomena such as the showers of meteors, earthquakes, falls or thunderbolts, hurricanes, eclipses and the appearance of the meteoric light take place in the different months of the year.[25] The food-stuff such as rice, wheat and barley stocked in the month of Jyeṣṭha under the mysterious influence of the above mentioned phenomena would bring in six times their value in the course of eight months.[26] Rice purchased in the month of Aśvina and rice bought and stocked in the month of Kārttika would sell at an advantage in the month of Māgha.[27]

Reference of the use of forced labour in agriculture is met with in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa. Bharata, being made to engage in work by the instance of others, by viṣṭi(forced labour) or by wages, he ate whatever was obtained by alms or by chance, much or little, sweet or foul, but never for satisfaction of senses.[28] When he wished to have food(for his livelihood) in return(as wages) for work for others, he was asked even by his brothers to work in the fields and he did it, but he knew not whether it was level or uneven, whether he did more or less(in proportion to the wages).He swallowed, as if it were nectar, whether it was broken rice or other grain;oil-cake, husk, worm-eaten grains and charred rice sticking to the bottom of pot.[29]
