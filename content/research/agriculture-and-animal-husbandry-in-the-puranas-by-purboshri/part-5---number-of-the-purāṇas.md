## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Number of the Puranas’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 5 - Number of the Purāṇas

The Mahāpurāṇas are held to be eighteen in number. Almost in all the Purāṇas, the list of eighteen Mahāpurāṇas, is found.[1]

In the Matsya Purāṇa[2], the Agni Purāṇa,[3] and the Nārada Purāṇa[4] the list of the eighteen Purāṇas is as follows:

Brahma-purāṇa;

Padma-purāṇa;

Viṣṇu-purāṇa;

Vāyu-purāṇa;

Bhāgavata-purāṇa;

Nāradīya-purāṇa;

Mārkaṇḍeya-purāṇa;

Agni-purāṇa;

Bhaviṣya-purāṇa;

Brahmavaivarta-purāṇa;

Liṅga-purāṇa;

Varāha-purāṇa;

Skanda-purāṇa;

Vāmana-purāṇa;

Kūrma-purāṇa;

Matsya-purāṇa;

Garuḍa-purāṇa and;

Brahmāṇḍa-purāṇa.;

In the Matsya Purāṇa,the Agni Purāṇa, the Nārada Purāṇa and in the Skanda Purāṇa,[5] Vāyavīya occurs fourth in the serial order whereas the Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa[6] the Viṣṇu Purāṇa[7] and the Bhāgavata Purāṇa[8] mentions the Śiva Purāṇa instead of the Vāyu Purāṇa. The Śaiva of the lists cannot be other than the extant Vāyu, which claims to be a Śaivite Purāṇa. The name Vāyavīya may have commentary into vogue much earlier because even the Mahābhārata mentions a Purāṇa  proclaimed by Vāyu, and the name Śaiva may have been originated at a later stage in view of its Śaivite tendencies.[9] The Skanda Purāṇa mentions that as the fourth Purāṇa was originally proclaimed by Vāyu, so this Purāṇa is remembered as Vāyu Purāṇa. And since devotion to Śiva has been propounded in it, so the other name for this Purāṇa is Śiva Purāṇa.[10]

In the Bhāgavata Purāṇa the names of the eighteen Purāṇas is serialized differently—

brāhmaṃ pādmaṃ vaiṣṇavaṃca śaivaṃ laiṅgaṃ sagāruḍam/
nāradīyaṃ bhāgavatamāgneyaṃ skāndasaṃjñitam//
bhaviṣyaṃ brahmavaivarttaṃ mārkaṇḍeyaṃ savāmanam/
vārāhaṃ mātsyaṃ kaurmaṃca brahmāṇḍākhyamiti triṣaṭ//[11]

brāhmaṃ pādmaṃ vaiṣṇavaṃca śaivaṃ laiṅgaṃ sagāruḍam/
nāradīyaṃ bhāgavatamāgneyaṃ skāndasaṃjñitam//
bhaviṣyaṃ brahmavaivarttaṃ mārkaṇḍeyaṃ savāmanam/
vārāhaṃ mātsyaṃ kaurmaṃca brahmāṇḍākhyamiti triṣaṭ//[11]

The Vāyu Purāṇa[12] too gives the list of the Purāṇas in a different order as follows—

Matsya-purāṇa;

Bhaviṣya-purāṇa;

Mārkaṇḍeya-purāṇa;

Brahmavaivarta-purāṇa;

Brahmāṇḍa-purāṇa;

Bhāgavata-purāṇa;

Brahma-purāṇa;

Vāmana-purāṇa,;

Ādika-purāṇa;

Anila-purāṇa (Vāyu-purāṇa);

Nāradīya-purāṇa;

Vainateya-purāṇa;

Padma-purāṇa;

Kūrma-purāṇa;

Saukara-purāṇa and;

Skanda-purāṇa.

In the Vāyu Purāṇa, the names of only sixteen Purāṇas are given although ‘eighteen Purāṇas’ are spoken with reference to.[13] And instead of the terms Garuḍa and Varāha, Vainateya and Saukara are used.

The Devi Bhāgavata Purāṇa has stated the following sonnet for memorizing the names of the Purāṇas easily—

madvayaṃ bhadvayaṃ caiva bratrayaṃ vacatuṣṭayam/
anāpaliṃ gakūskāni purāṇāni pṛthakpṛthak//[14]

madvayaṃ bhadvayaṃ caiva bratrayaṃ vacatuṣṭayam/
anāpaliṃ gakūskāni purāṇāni pṛthakpṛthak//[14]

Here, madvayam refers to the two Purāṇas the names of which begin with ma. They are: Mārkaṇḍeya and Matsya. Similarly the word bhadvayaṃ signifies the two Purāṇas, the names of which begin with bha. They are: Bhāgavata and Bhaviṣya. The names of the other Purāṇas are to be memorized similarly.

The numbers of verses of the eighteen Purāṇas are also mentioned in the Purāṇas.[15] According to the Skanda Purāṇa[16] the number of verses in the various Purāṇas are—

Brahma: 10000 verses;

Padma: 55000 verses;

Viṣṇu: 23000 verses;

Vāyu: 24000 verses;

Bhaviṣya: 14500 verses;

Mārkaṇḍeya: 9000 verses;

Agni: 16000 verses;

Nāradīya: 25000 verses;

Bhāgavata: 18000 verses;

Brahmavaivarta: 18000 verses;

Liṅga: 11000 verses;

Varāha: 24000 verses;

Skanda: 81000 verses;

Vāmana: 10000 verses;

Kūrma: 17000 verses;

Matsya: 14000 verses;

Garuḍa: 19000 verses and

Brahmāṇḍa: 12800 verses.
