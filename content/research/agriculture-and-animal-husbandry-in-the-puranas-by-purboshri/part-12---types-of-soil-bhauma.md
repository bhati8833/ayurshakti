## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Types of Soil (Bhauma)’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Part 12 - Types of Soil (Bhauma)

Various types of grounds are also mentioned in the Purāṇas. They are—

White coloured ground, secreting the fragrant of clarified butter (śuklājyagandhā),

Blood coloured ground, possessing the smell of blood (raktā ca raktagandhā),

Yellow coloured ground, having sweet fragrant (sugandhinī pītā) and

Black clay, producing the smell of wine (kṛṣṇā surāgandhā).[1]

Again the Agni Purāṇa mentions different colours of soil. They are-

Golden soil (rukmabhauma),

Rocky soil (śilābhauma),

Blue soil (nīlamūrttika),

Yellowish red soil (pītarakta) and

White soil (śvetabhauma).[2]

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa too mentions different types of soil. They are as follows—

white (śuklā),

black (kṛṣṇā),

purple (aruṇā),

yellow (pītā),

sandy (śarkarā),

stony (śailā), and

of gold (kāñcanā).[3]

The Matsya Purāṇa refers to fertile (urvarā) and barren or sterile (uṣara) types of soil.It states that plants do not grow in a barren land.[4] The Agni Purāṇa mentions various types of soils, such as, mṛt, mṛttikā, mṛtsā and mṛtsnā etc. Among these the mṛtsā and mṛtsnā type of soil are considered the best.[5]

The Agni Purāṇa[6] refers to the following three kinds of regions—

1 Anūpa—A country flourishing in trees and having plenty of water is known as anūpa (marshy).It produces excess wind and phlegm.
2 Jāṅgala—A country that is devoid of the above said characteristics is known as jāṅgala (rural or picturesque).The country known as jāṅgala produces excessive bile.
3 Sādhāraṇa—A country that has trees and water in moderate quantity is known as sādhāraṇa (ordinary) country The sādhāraṇa type is known as moderate.
