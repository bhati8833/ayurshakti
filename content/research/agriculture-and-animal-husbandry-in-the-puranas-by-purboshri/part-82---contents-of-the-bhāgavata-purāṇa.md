## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

Part 8.2 - Contents of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa

According to the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Brahmā described the Bhāgavata Purāṇa to his son Nārada, which had been transmitted to him by the glorious God. Nārada, who then illustrated it to Vyāsa.[1] The glorification of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa has been stated elaborately in the Bhāgavata-Māhātmya[2] section of the Padma Purāṇa. It is stated there that in the Kali-age, the holy text of Śrīmad Bhāgavata, would bring safety of the world. There is nothing higher to this Purāṇa that would cleanse the mind. It is acquired by good men through religious merit earned in many (earlier) existences.[3] Knowing the salvation of king Parīkṣit, the lotus-born one (kamalasaṃbhavaḥ) compared the holy texts and the great Purāṇas.

Among them Śrīmad Bhāgavata came to be superior on the earth.[4] By reciting and listening to it, a man goes to Viṣṇu's position. Listening to it for a year gives great happiness, dedication to it for a month brings luxury, and listening to it for a week gives salvation at all times.[5] The Śrīmadbhāgavata Purāṇa takes pleasure in itself.

It is manifested for the happiness of Bhakti, Jñāna and Vairāgya to Brahmā, by Kṛṣṇa. The four-versed (summary) of it appears to be the entire Brahman. That account was narrated to Nārada by Brahmā. Nārada narrated it to Vyāsadeva, to take away torment. By remembering it Bādarāyaṇa (Vyāsa) instantly became depressed; and in a manner delighting the soul, composed the work of the great name.[6] According to the Padma Purāṇa, the treatise called Śrīmad Bhāgavata comprises eighteen thousand verses and twelve skandhas(sections), and is in the form of a dialogue between Parīkṣit and Śuka.[7]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa itself too mentions that it contains eighteen thousands ślokas.[8] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa was revealed by Viṣṇu to god Brahmā at first.[9] It contains discussion on the value of detachment at the beginning, in the middle and at the end. Moreover, it contains several episodes exalting the sportive activities of Lord Hari.[10] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa is said to be the essence of all the Upaniṣads which is distinguished by uniqueness between the supreme Brahman and the Paramātman. Its only purpose is to secure Mokṣa.[11] One, who drinks the sweet nectar of the teachings of this Purāṇa, does not find pleasure in any other thing.[12] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa occupies the foremost position among all the other Purāṇas.The position is compared with that of the holiest river Gaṅgā among the other rivers, Lord Viṣṇu among the gods or god Śiva among the devotees of Viṣṇu.[13] Among the Purāṇas there is no Purāṇa which can be equal to the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.[14] It is a Purāṇa without any fault. It is dear to the devotees of Śrī Viṣṇu.[15] He who, on the full moon day of Bhādrapada, offers the Bhāgavata Purāṇa as a gift, attains to the highest abode.[16]

In the Bhāgavata Purāṇa the god has been glorified at every step, who abides in the entire universe.[17] Lord Hari who is the destroyer of all sins, who is Nārāyaṇa, the regulator of sense-organs and who is the defender of his devotees, has been glorified here, in this Purāṇa.[18] It has described the Supreme Brahma, who is the cause of creation. The topic of spiritual knowledge has also been discussed.[19] In its twelfth Skandha, the Bhāgavata Purāṇa itself mentions the merits of studying it.

According to statement mentioned therein, a Brāhmaṇa acquires spiritual knowledge, a Kṣatriya conquers sovereignty over the whole world, a Vaiśya becomes the lord of possessions and a Śūdra is freed of sins, on studying this Purāṇa.[20] He who narrates this Purāṇa every day or for a time of three hours or for a minute and he who listens this Purāṇa with devotion, purifies his inner soul.[21] Moreover, it added that one, who hears it on the twelfth or eleventh day of every fortnight of the lunar month, will be blessed with long life. One who reads it with dedication frees himself of all sins.[22] Reading this Purāṇa at the holy places like Puṣkara, Mathurā and Dvārakā, is said to be freed from the fear of Saṃsāra.[23] One who, with a pure and controlled mind, learns this Purāṇa, attains the supreme abode i.e. Mokṣa.[24]

In this regard Professor Wilson’s remark is worthy to mention. He thus views: The Bhāgavata is a work of great celebrity in India and exercises a more direct and powerful influence upon the opinions and feelings of the people than perhaps any other of the Purāṇas. It is placed in the fifth position in all the lists but the Padma-Purāṇa ranks it as the eighteenth, as the extracted substance of all the rest. According to the usual specification, it consists of eighteen thousand ślokas, distributed amongst three hundred and thirty-two chapters divided into twelve Skandhas or books.[25]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa[26] has ten topics in it. They are as follows—

1. Sarga (subtle creation)—It consists of the creation of the Bhūtas, Tanmātras, Indriyas, Ahaṃkāra and Mahat, and the appearance of Virāṭ Puruṣa.[27]
2 Visarga (gross creation)—the individual creation by Brahmā is called Visarga.[28]
3 Sthāna [law and order (ensured by God)]—it is the triumph of the lord in the maintenance of (the divine) law and order(in everything).[29]
4 Poṣaṇa (protection-welfare of all)—the grace of God is known as Poṣaṇa.[30]
5 Manvantara (the periods of Manu)—the duties of the rulers of Manvantaras.[31]
6 Ūti (material lust from Karmas)—the desire for karma(action) is called Ūti.[32]
7 Īśānukathā (accounts of God’s deeds)—the stories of the various incarnations and of the followers of Hari are known as Īśānukathā.[33]
8 Nirodha (physical annihilation)—the sleep of Hari and all individual souls in Pralaya.[34]
9 Mukti (liberation)—the continued perception of the identity of self and of Brahman.[35]
10 Āśraya (the last resort of the universe, the ultimate reality)—The Final Resort, Para-Brahman or Paramātman from creation or dissolution both proceed.[36]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa contains twelve Skandhas. The first Skandha presents a discourse between Vyāsa and Nārada,[37] as well as accounts of Nārada.[38] The second Skandha describes what men are to do and what they are forbidden to do,[39] about the creation of the Universe-the Bhāgavata Purāṇa as related by Brahmā to Nārada,[40] description of the Virāṭ Puruṣa,[41] preservation by Līlā-Avatāras[42].The third Skandha describes about the creation of Bhāgavata as related by Maitreya to Vidura,[43] the ten-fold creation by Brahmā,[44] divisions of Kāla,[45] the descriptions of the various creations of Brahmā,[46] the first or the Svāyambhuva–Manvantara,[47] Deva and Deva-yoni creation.[48] Kapila, the founder of Sāṃkhya school is mentioned in the third Skandha as an incarnation of Viṣṇu, who instructs his mother Devāhuti about the yoga philosophy of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.[49] The Fourth Skandha describes about the genealogy of Manu and the Ṛṣis,[50] the story of Dhruva,[51] the story of Pṛthu, etc.[52] The fifth and the sixth Skandha describes about the Bhuvanakoṣa, Earth-chain,[53] story of Ajāmila,[54] the progeny of Dakṣa,[55] the story of Vṛtra,[56] etc. The seventh Skandha includes the account of Hiraṇyakaśipu and Prahlāda,[57] description of Varṇa and Āśrama,[58] etc. The eight Skandha gives description about the second to fourteenth Manvantaras. The ninth Skandha narrates about the Vaivasvata-Manvantara,[59] the dynasty of Ikṣvāku,[60] the Lunar dynasty,[61] etc. The tenth Skandha includes the description of the life of Kṛṣṇa. The love-scenes with the Gopīs have been described elaborately. Referring to the tenth Skandha Winternitz says that the tenth book is the most popular and most widely read by all. This book is translated into almost all popular Indian languages of India and is a favourite book of all classes of the Indian folk.[62] The eleventh Skandha describes about the destruction of the Yādavas and the death of Kṛṣṇa, and the twelfth Skandha encloses the usual predictions on the Kaliyuga and the destruction of the world.
