## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Agricultural Products Used as Medicines’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 27 - Agricultural Products Used as Medicines

The Garuḍa Purāṇa[1] states the medicinal value of various agricultural products. Rakta śāli pacifies thirst and reduces fat. Moreover, it removes the three faults i.e. vāta, pitta and kapha.[2] The mahāśāli increases sexual desire (vṛṣyaṃ).[3] The kalabha cures bile-troubles and phlegmatic humour.[4] The ṣaṣṭika (variety of rice) cures from the tridoṣa (vāta-pitta-kapha).[5] The śyāmāka is the cure for bile and phlegmatic disorder.[6] Priyaṅgu, nīvāra and koradūṣa cure the same.[7] Yava is also the remover of bile and phlegm.[8] Godhūma is promoter of sexual desire and destroyer of vāta (gastric disorder).[9] The mudga cures cough, bile and dripping eyes (asra).[10] The māṣa gives great strength, promotes sexual desire and also removes bile and phlegm.[11] Rājamāṣa does not promote sexual desire, but it removes gas trouble.[12] The kulattha cures from restrained breathing, hiccups, phlegm (kapha) and gases.[13] The makuṣṭhaka drives away fever and raktapitta (derangement of blood produced by bile).[14] Caṇaka and āḍhakī destroys the pitta

and kapha.[15] Atasī generates pitta and destroys kapha and vāyu.[16] The masūra is destroyer of bile and phlegm.[17] Besides, the soup from yava, pepper and kulattha is stated as good for throat and it also cures the gas-trouble.[18] The mudga and āmalaka is a cure for phlegm and bile.[19]

To remove the weakness of a person suffering from fever, a gruel (maṇḍa) of roasted puffed rice (lājaḥ) and boiled water is recommended.[20] For a convalescent patient, a diet of old and matured grains of ṣaṣṭika, nīvāra, raktaśāli, pramodakā should be given.[21] Another diet chart prescribed by the Agni Purāṇa for a patient with fever includes preparations of yava in any shape as well as the pulses mudga, masūra, caṇakā, kulattha cooked with kuṣṭha and fruits and vegetables such as pomegranates, paṭolas, nimbas, parpaṭas, karkoṭakas, kaṭolakas may also be used safely.[22] In diarrhoea (atisāra), the Agni Purāṇa recommends a diet composed of saktu, godhūma, lāja (roasted puffed rice), yava, śāli, masūra, caṇaka and mudga treated with the drug kuṣṭha.[23] Again old śāli rice is also recommended in diarrhoea.[24] For the patient of chronic dysentery, grains of old and matured śāli rice should form the staple food.[25] Wheat, barley, śāli rice, mudga pulse and brahmarkṣa are the various diets given in the Agni Purāṇa for a man suffering from abdominal dropsy.[26] Another diet chart consists of a soup of masūra, mudga, old śāli rice, nimba and parpaṭa along with the soup of animals.[27]

Apūpa, kuṣṭha, kulmāṣa and yava are favourable to a man suffering from an attack of Gonorrhea, during which his diet should consist of mudga pulse, kulattha, preparations of barley and matured śāli rice.[28] A leper should largely drink of a potion containing the washings of mudga or masūra pulse.[29]

A man suffering from Erysipelas(bacterial infection which affects the skin) should live on a diet composed of mudga, āḍhaka, masūra, prepared with lemon juice, saindhava salt, clarified butter, juice of grapes, old wheat, barley and śāli rice.[30] For śotha or swelling, dry radish and roots of kulattha boiled in the juice of jāṅgala are prescribed. And the patient should live on a diet composed of preparations of barley, wheat and matured śāli rice; treated with uśīra.[31] For a patient with breathing trouble or bronchitis, kulattha and mudga pulse prepared with bird’s flesh(viṣkiras), cooked with curd, pomegranate juice, juice of matuluṅga, honey, lime and grapes and wheat, barley or boiled śāli rice are prescribed.[32]

For a patient of rheumatism a diet of red ṣaṣṭika, barley, wheat, mudga, kākamācī (niteseed), vāstuka,honey and distilled water is prescribed.[33] Śāli rice, āmalaka, date fruits, grapes and jujubes are also favourable for rheumatic patient.[34] Besides, the diet for a patient suffering from piles should consist of preparations of barley, śāli rice, and suvarca salt.[35] Fried paddy, powdered barley, honey, śikhi and wine are good medicines for cold.[36] Boiled rice made of śāli paddy or a mixture of milk and water made lukewarm should be deemed as the best layer of thirst.[37]

In ūrustambha (rheumatism of the thigh),various preparations made of barley, dry radish, vegetables of paṭola and vetra are prescribed.[38] The diet of a consumptive patient, should consist of oil pressed out of sesamum seeds, mudga, barley, wheat, boiled rice made from grains matured in the granary, at least for a year and the juice of jāṅgala are prescribed.[39]
