Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Festivals and Rites Related to Agriculture:’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 25 - Festivals and Rites Related to Agriculture:

During the Puranic age, the basic occupation of the common people was found to be agriculture. Therefore, most of the festivals were usually celebrated around the pre-harvesting or post-harvesting period in the Purāṇic period. Various festivals and rites were performed in those periods which were closely related to agriculture.They are discussed below:

Āgneya-Utsava:

The Skanda Purāṇa[1] mentions about the festival Āgneya Utsava which is celebrated in the month of Pauṣa. No definite day is mentioned for this festival. This festival is in respect of Śiva. This was the festival of fresh corn.

Indra Mahotsava:

The Skanda Purāṇa[2] mentions the festival Indra Mahotsava to be held for five days. This festival is performed in honour of Indra in the month of Bhādrapada. Only a banner of Indra is worshipped, but not an idol, because of the sin of Indra in seducing Ahalyā.[3] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa also mentions about this festival.[4] Performance of this festival gives good harvest and timely rain.Rain is said to be Indra, and cloud his aspects.[5] Timely rain is very much necessary for the process of agriculture.In such a way it is connected with agriculture.

Madana Mahotsava:

The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa[6] mentions about the Madana Mahotsava festival which is performed for timely rain and prosperity, in honour of Kāma. It comes on the 13th bright of the Caitra. While Śiva was observing the Pāśupata vow, Madana, the god of love shot him with an arrow made of flowers. Śiva got angry and reduced him to ashes.When Madana’s wife Rati wept bitterly, Śiva assured that it was impossible to restore Madana with the same body, but he would stay in the mind of people. Especially on the 13th bright of the Caitra, he would regain his form symbolically.

In this rite one should draw the Aśoka tree with sindūra(Zink oxide) and other colours in wood or in gold.The picture of Madana along with the personified spring season vasanta must also be drawn near it.Vasanta should have a face of a horse and the banner of a fish.[7] In accompaniment nymphs should also be drawn.Madana should be worshipped.At the mid-day Brāhmaṇas should be fed with sweets.In connection with this festival dances with lights are also mentioned.

Saṃkrānti:

According to the Skanda Purāṇa,[8] this festival was observed in the month of Māgha. At Jagannātha Puri in accordance with custom, the newly reaped rice (śāli) was to be deposited, on the east side of the Viṣṇu temple, after pounding it. It was to be covered with new clothes and was to be worshipped. Viṣṇu was to be worshipped and the nirmālya wreath of Viṣṇu was to be placed on the śāli rice.Then the image of Viṣṇu was to be carried three times around the temple.This indicates that he was going three times around the world.In the next morning varieties of cooked rice were to be placed in gold plates. Mixing curd, sugar, ghee, jujube fruits etc. with the rice, these plates were then taken around the temple.This is the festival where new rice was to be offered to the God.
