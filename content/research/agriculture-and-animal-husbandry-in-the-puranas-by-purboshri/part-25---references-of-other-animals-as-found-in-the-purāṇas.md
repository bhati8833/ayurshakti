Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

Part 25 - References of other Animals as found in the Purāṇas

Buffalo:

We find in the Purāṇas, Mahiṣa i.e. buffalo as the riding animal of Yama, the god of death[1],as also of the Goddess Vārāhī.[2] According to the Matsya Purāṇa, Surabhi in union with the sage Kaśyapa, produced the buffaloes along with the cows.[3] The Vāyu Purāṇa speaks that Mṛgamandā (daughter of Krodhā and wife of Pulaha), gave birth to buffaloes, camels, pigs etc.[4] Offering of buffalo’s flesh satisfied the Pitṛs for ten months.[5] It was enjoined that in śrāddha rituals a she-buffalo should be given to Brāhmaṇa as she was the vehicle of Yama. This belief is vibrated in the Agni Purāṇa,[6] which adds that one’s desire is fulfilled and fortune is gained by giving the gift of a she-buffalo.

Camel:

Reference of Camels and mules are found in the Purāṇas.[7] Camel was used for chariot. Mahiṣāsura, an asura residing in Rasātala had a chariot drawn by camels.[8] The Matsya Purāṇa and the Viṣṇu Purāṇa states that Sugrīvī, the daughter of Tāmrā and Kaśyapa, was the mother of camels, sheep, goats, horses, and asses. They were the progenies of Tāmrā.[9]

Dog:

Śvānabali is an oblation to the two heavenly dogs Śyāma and Śabala. They were born in the family of Vaivasvata.Bali was offered to them in Yajña so that they may become non-violent.[10]

Donkey:

Reference of khara i.e. donkey[11] is also found. Donkey is shown, traditionally, to the left side of the idols of Kāma, the god of love.[12] Khara, donkey was produced from Sugrīvī.[13] Donkey or asses were also used for chariot. Kujambha, an asura, had a chariot drawn by donkeys.[14]

Goat:

Aja (goat) is born of the Creator’s mouth,[15] which is considered a useful animal in yajñas(as a victim)[16] along with cows, mahiṣas, aśvas, hastīs, and birds.

The goat, is the riding animal of Agni.[17] Its flesh pleases the Pitṛs for six months.[18] The mouth of the goat and horse was believed to be pure.[19] From the medicinal point of view the urine of a she goat is praised. According to the Agni Purāṇa the oil or the urine of a hornless goat is an excellent remedy for ear diseases.[20] The she goat’s milk is a remedy for bloody urine in case of a horse, if drunk on an empty stomach after getting boiled with sugar added and cooled.[21] The goat especially the she-goat is important in the ritual of the manes. The oblation to the manes may be given in fire or in the palm of a Brāhmaṇa, or in the ear of the she goat or that of the horse or in a cow-shed or near a water place,[22] but the milk of a she goat or a sheep was not to be offered to the manes.[23] According to the Kūrma Purāṇa the place where a śrāddha for the mane is performed has to be purified.For this purpose it is ordained that the place should be strewn with sesame seeds and goats should be tied all over the place; thereby everything polluted by the evils get purified.[24]

Sheep and Deer, as Riding Animal:

Śumbha, a commander of Tāraka’s force; had the gigantic black and white coloured sheep as his riding animal.[25] Deer (Mṛga) was the riding animal of the Goddess Patākinī.[26] Deer and other animals like hare were born of Mṛgī, who was the daughter of Krodhā and wife of Pulaha.[27] Again, Garuḍa was the riding animal of Goddess Vaiṣṇavī.[28]
