## 📜 1. Section Overview & Classical Context

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Characteristics of the Puranas’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 2 - Characteristics of the Purāṇas

The Matsya Purāṇa and the Vāyu Purāṇa speak of five characteristics to distinguish a Purāṇa.[1] They are as follows—

Creation (sarga),

Dissolution and re-creation (pratisarga),

The lineage line (vaṃśa),

Jurisdiction of the fourteen Manus, belonging to the solar and lunar races (manvantara), and

The genealogy of the kings of the solar and lunar races (vaṃśyānucarita).

The Purāṇas, with these five characteristics, sing the glory of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Arka and Rudra.They also describe the creation and dissolution of the Earth.[2] Moreover, the Purāṇas describe about the four Vargas i.e. Dharma, Artha, Kāma and Mokṣa.[3]

Amarasiṃha also describes the five characteristics of the Purāṇas in the Amarakoṣa.[4] The Agni Purāṇa too mentions the five characteristics.[5] The five fold characteristics of Purāṇas are also mentioned by some other Purāṇas such as the Śiva Purāṇa, Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Skanda Purāṇa, Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa and the Kūrma Purāṇa.[6]

But the Bhāgavata Purāṇa mentions the ten characteristics of Purāṇas as described by the Brāhmaṇa sages in conformity to Vedas and Śāstras.[7] It has also elaborated all the characteristics of the Purāṇas thoroughly.

They are as follows—

1. Sarga (subtle creation)

According to the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, sarga consists of the growth of mahat from the interruption in the balance of stability of the three guṇas of the prakṛti,leading to the development of three-fold ahaṃkāra (sāttvika, rājasa and tāmasa),the evolutes of which are subtle elements (bhūtas), the sense organs and their objects(gross elements and the deities presiding over the sense organs).[8]

2. Visarga (gross creation)

Visarga is the creation of the gross mobile and immobile nature.[9]

3. Vṛtti (sustenance)

The immobile creation(food-grains, fruits, roots) and some products of mobile creation too form the Vṛtti (sustenance) of the mobile creation. In case of human beings the Vṛtti is determined by their character, wish and scriptural commands.[10]

4 Rakṣā (protection of the universe)

Rakṣā consists of the incarnations and the sportive acts of Lord Viṣṇu, from age to age appearing among sub-human beings, human beings, sages and among gods, and the annihilation of the haters of the Vedas.[11]

5 Hetu (cause or the lust for Karmas)

Hetu of the universe is Jīva (individual soul). He is the doer of actions under the influence of ignorance.[12]

6 Antara (the periods of Manu)

Manvantara(Manu-period) is characterized by six special aspects. They are the period presided over by the particular Manu, the set of gods, the sons of Manu, Indra, seven sages and the incarnations of Hari.[13]

7 Vaṃśa (dynasties of kings)

Vaṃśa is the race of kings who were born of god Brahmā and continued throughout the three divisions of time–the past, present and future.[14]

8 Vaṃśānucarita(history of the kings)

Vaṃśānucarita is the history of the kings and their descendants.[15]

9 Saṃsthā (physical annihilation)

The destruction of the universe created by Māyā is called Saṃsthā. This destruction is of four types-Naimittika(Occasional or brought about by a particular cause), Prākṛtika(of the Prakṛti), Nitya(Constant) and Ātyantika(Absolute).[16]

10 Apāśraya (ultimate reality)

Apāśraya is the highest goal of the characteristics of the Purāṇas. Apāśraya (the last support or ultimate reality) is Brahman. It is depicted as a observer to what is caused by Māyā, as in the three phases of Jīva(viz. wakefulness, dreaming and dreamless deep sleep) and is also somewhat different from them as in Samādhi.[17]

The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa also describes the ten main characteristics of the Purāṇas—

sṛṣṭiścāpi visṛṣṭiśca sthitisteṣāṃ ca pālanam/ 
karmaṇāṃ vāsanā vārtā manūnāṃ cākrameṇa ca// 
varṇanaṃ pralayānāṃ ca mokṣasya ca nirūpaṇam/ 
tatkīrtanaṃ harereva vedānāṃ ca pṛthak pṛthak//

These are as follows—

sṛṣṭi (original creation),

visṛṣṭi (creation of other movable and immovable things from mūla prakṛti),

sthiti (period of life),

pālana (defense),

karma vāsanā (necessity which makes a man liable for his acts or the desire for work),

manuvārtā (information about different Manus),

7. pralaya-varṇanā (the account relating to the eternal dissolution of the world),

mokṣa-nirūpaṇa (the determination of the nature of redemption),

harikīrtana (the eulogies of Hari) and

vedakīrtana (reciting of the Vedas separately).[18]

This Purāṇa further mentions that the Mahāpurāṇas are to deal with these tenfold characteristic.[19]

The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa mentions the five-fold feature too. But according to this Purāṇa, these are appropriate for the Upapurāṇas only.[20]

The Padma Purāṇa[21] looks the various Purāṇas as the various limbs of Viṣṇu. the Padma Purāṇa is said to be Viṣṇu himself,—

the Brahma Purāṇa being the head,

the Padma Purāṇa the heart,

the Viṣṇu Purāṇa the right arm,

the Śiva Purāṇa being the left arm,

the Bhāgavata Purāṇa the two thighs,

the Nāradīya Purāṇa the navel,

the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa the right shank,

the Agni Purāṇa being the left shank,

the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa the right knee,

the Brahmavaivarta being the left one,

the Liṅga Purāṇa the right ankle,

the Varāha Purāṇa being the left,

the Skanda Purāṇa the hair in the body,

the Vāmana Purāṇa the skin,

the Kūrma Purāṇa the back,

the Matsya Purāṇa the fat,

the Garuḍa Purāṇa being the marrow and

the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa the bones.
