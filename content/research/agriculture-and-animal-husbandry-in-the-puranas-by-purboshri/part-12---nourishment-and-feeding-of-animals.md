Part 12 - Nourishment and Feeding of Animals

[Full title: Animal Husbandry in Post-Purāṇic Sanskrit Works and its Relevance with the Purāṇas (3) Nourishment and Feeding of Animals]

It is recommended in the Arthaśāstra, for horses, the diet of two droṇas of any one of the grains like rice (śālī, vrīhi), barley, panic seeds (priyaṅgu) soaked or cooked, cooked mudga (Phaseolus mango) or māṣa (Phaseolus Radiatus), and one prastha of oil would be best. Five palas of salt and fifty palas of flesh are to be added therewith. One āḍhaka of broth (rasa) or two āḍhakas of curd is needed to soften this food item. Five palas of sugar, one prastha of liquor (surā) and two prasthas of milk are needed to make the above diet savoring.[1]

For bulls which are equal to horses in speed and in carrying loads, [the following] will form the diet.[2]—

half a bhāra of meadow grass (yavasa),
twice bhāra of ordinary grass (tṛṇa),
one tulā (100 palas) of oil cakes,
10 āḍhakas of bran,
five palas of salt (mukhalavaṇam),
one kuḍuba of oil for rubbing over the nose,
1 prastha of drink (pāna),
one tulā of flesh,
1 āḍhaka of curds,
one droṇa of barley or cooked māṣa (Phaselous radiatus),
one droṇa of milk or half an āḍhaka of surā (liquor),
1 prastha of oil or ghee (sneha),
10 palas of sugar or jaggery,
1 pala of the fruit of śṛṅgivera (ginger),

The same commodities less by one quarter each will form the diet for mules, cows and asses; twice the quantity of the above things for buffaloes and camels. Draught oxen and cows, supplying milk, should be provided, twice the quantity as said above, according to the duration of time the oxen are kept at work and the quantity of milk which the cow supplies. All the cattle shall be supplied with abundance of fodder and water.[3]

The shares for an elephant (of seven aratnis in height) should be—

one droṇa of rice,
half an āḍhaka of oil,
3 prasthas of ghee,
10 palas of salt,
50 palas of flesh,
one āḍhaka of broth (rasa), or two āḍhakas of curd (in order to tender the dish tasteful),
10 palas of sugar,
one āḍhaka of liquor, or two āḍhakas of milk.

One prastha of oil is required for smearing over the body. Moreover, one-eight prastha of oil for applying on the head and for use in a lamp is to be kept in the stables.[4] The Śukranītisāra too mentions that the horse should be made to take milk, ghee, water and powdered grains.[5] The horse should be given peas or grains, māṣa, mudga, both dry and wet, as well as well-cooked meat.[6] The best foods for horses are yava and caṇaka; māṣa and makuṣṭhaka are considered good and masūra and mudga are considered inferior stuff.[7]

The Purāṇas too mention some diets for animals. It is recommended there that a calf should be given a food which consist of māṣa, tila, godhūma, payaḥ, kṣīra and ghṛta(clarified butter), mixed with lavaṇa, to develop well.[8] The Agni Purāṇa states that to give a morsel of grass to the cows(go-grāsa) was considered the daily duties of a householder. After go-grāsa and performing the ritual svastyayana and after worshipping the guests, the householder should take his food.[9] In case of diet of elephant it is said that the food which consists of ṣaṣṭi, vrīhi and śālī rice, is considered the best food for an elephant.[10] The food consists of wheat and barley should be ranked as second best, while the other sorts of food should be considered inferior sort.[11]
