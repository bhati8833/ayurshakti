Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

Part 11 - Sowing of Seeds (Bīja/Vīja)

The successful cultivation of crops depends on the viability of seeds, their good health, proper sowing time and good soil moisture conditions. The importance of good seed for better crop has been emphasized by Manu that good seed alone sown in good land is successfully raised.[1] The seeds sown in poor land are destroyed within that very land.[2]

The Nighaṇṭu opines that seeds (vīja) are the cause of crop growing—

vījiḥ syātpreraṇakriyā/preryate hi kāryakāraṇāya vā vījam/ yathā dhānyādivījamuttarottaraṃ svābhivṛddhaye bhavati evamapatyamapi pitṛṇāmabhivṛddhiheturiti vījamityucyate.[3]

vījiḥ syātpreraṇakriyā/preryate hi kāryakāraṇāya vā vījam/ yathā dhānyādivījamuttarottaraṃ svābhivṛddhaye bhavati evamapatyamapi pitṛṇāmabhivṛddhiheturiti vījamityucyate.[3]

In the Ṛgveda too, it is mentioned that seeds should be sown only in the properly prepared seed bed.[4] The Agni Purāṇa mentions that after the consecration and installation of the images of all gods and spiritual beings feasts are to be celebrated.

Describing the nature of the feast it is stated there that the feast should be celebrated with the sowing of auspicious seeds, which comprise—

yava,

śāli-rice,

tila,

mudga,

godhūma and

śvetasarṣapa (white mustard),

kulattha and

māṣa.

It is suggested that these should be well washed before sowing.[5] A śarāva (earthen saucer), a small water-pot and a small raised bank or embankments are the most convenient post or places for growing on the above seeds.[6] The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa also contains reference of the sowing of seeds in the field for the purpose of vegetation.[7] The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa mentions that before constructing a temple, preparing a garden and digging a well, the ground was to be ploughed.In reference to this context it is pointed out there that after the process of ploughing five types of grains of paddy are to be sowed. But, in the case of a temple, more varieties of grains are instructed to be sowed. These are called as ‘seven-grain’ formula (sapta-vrīhi-gaṇa).

The ‘five-grain’ (pañca-vrīhi) formula consists of—

mudga,

māṣa,

dhānya,

tila and

śyāmāka.

If masūra and kalāya are added there, that will be ‘seven-grain’ formula.[8] In the Agni Purāṇa, the god Janārdana is prayed to destroy all the dangers of corn-fields, such as fire, ashes etc.[9]
