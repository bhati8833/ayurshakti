Ayurveda cooking rules of Kanda-saka (Vegetables of rhizomes)

KANDA-SAKA (VEGETABLES OF RHIZOMES)—KADALIKANDA (Rhizome of banana tree): Cut into pieces and boil them in water. Cook in ghee with asafoetida, rock salt and black pepper. It is cold, delicious, appetiser, strength promoting and beneficial for hair. It is digestive stimulant. It cures burning sensation and acidity. SURANA (Amorphophallus campanulatus Blume): Boil the pieces in decoction of tamarind-leaves or in sour gruel. Cook in sesame oil with turmeric juice, water of asafoetida, salt, fragrant spices and black pepper. Dr Clean and wash it. Make it putapaka. Add rock salt, sesame Or oil, cumin seeds, lemon juice and wet ginger. Fry in mustard oil. Cook with sour spices. It stimulates digestion. It is light and un-unctuous. It cures piles and kasa.

The stalks of surana are also deseabed as vegetable? stalks are digestive stimulant, delicious and light. It cures 96 piles. MRUNALAKANDA : Cut into pieces and prepare Praleha. F h It is heavy, cold, astringent and sweet in taste, delicious and constipative. It causes flatulence. It alleviates pitta and aggravates vata. The Kshemakutuhala describes the following potherbs to be consumed in daily meal in Chapter IV (41, 42, 48 to 59): I (1) PATOLAPHALA (Trichosanthes dioica Roxb). (2) KARCARI (Cucumis melo L. Var.) (3) MULAKA (Radish) (4) ADRAKA (Ginger root). The Kshemakutuhala describes the various types of vegetables in the following numbers: Phalasaka Patras aka 1 30 25 Puspasaka Pallavasaka Dandasaka Kandasaka dasak } I 3 4 3 1 7

There are lot of potherbs to be used as vegetables. But all are not worth to be consumed (VII - 203). Number of vegetables to be consumed is 168 in the Caraka Samhita and 97 198 in the Sushruta-samhita While the Kshemakutuhala describes only 74 vegetables. Because later Nighantus have discouraged the use of vegetables by describing thier various harmful effects particularly on eye sight, sexual potency, muscular strength, blood and mental power. Patola, vastuka, kakamaci and panarnava have been said as exception to this (VIII-206) 98 According to the Kshemakutuhala, the following vegetables should not be consumed. " (1) Vegetables which are old, exceedingly un-unctuous, grown in improper land and eaten away by parasites. (2) All types of vegetables when dried (except the Mulaka). It is very difficult to identify all the vegetables descri bed by the Kshemakutuhala botanically. Because a vast change has occured in the usage of fruits and vegetables during the interval of four hundred years. However an attempt has been made for this in respect of most of the items, others have been left as they are in the text.
