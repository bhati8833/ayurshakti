Research Questions and Aims of the Study

Research Questions

Vinaya is the literature that contains not only the rules for the Buddhist monastic members (both monks and nuns) to follow, but also contains abundant details about ancient Indian culture.[1] Ancient Indian medicine forms a part of such particulars recorded in the Vinaya canon. The Chapter on Medicine is that part of the Vinaya Piṭaka preserving numerous medical data concerning cases of diseases and treatments as well as materia medica. Thus this text is, as mentioned above, frequently studied for its medical material. However, through the review of relevant works referred to in the preceding section, it has been revealed that the medical lore of the Chapter on Medicine in different VPs has not yet been thoroughly explored. Further research on this text is required for better understanding such lore.

Those people who have read this text would easily notice that the cases and remedies depicted in the Chapter on Medicine are very succinct and difficult to comprehend. For example, what is the autumnal disease that is presented as the first case in many versions of the Chapter on Medicine? What are those substances used for treating this disease, and why are they prescribed?[2]

Many such medical facts in the Chapter on Medicine are hard to understand. Several questions thus arise:

(i) What, in fact, is the medical information in the Chapter on Medicine referring to? That is, what could have happened for the cases of diseases and their prescribed treatments, and what could those medicinal substances be?

(ii) How can these medical details be understood? Why is such a method utilised for understanding them?

(iii) Can these facts be interpreted in terms of modern scientific or medical knowledge? If so, what could be the interpretations?

These are the major questions that this present study is attempting to answer.

Aims of the Study

Apart from attempting to provide answers for the above questions, certain aims are aspired to be achieved in this study.

Firstly, ancient Indian medicine, especially that at the Buddha’s time, can be explored through studying the Chapter on Medicine. The Chapter on Medicine records cases of diseases and treatment that occurred to the Buddha and his monastic followers, as well as drugs being utilised at that time. These data show the medical information preserved in this Buddhist text. By examining these medical facts through the method employed in this study (which will be elaborated in the following section on methodology in this chapter), ancient Indian medical knowledge documented in the Chapter on Medicine can be revealed. Secondly, the difficult and succinct medical cases and substances in the Chapter on Medicine will be studied and interpretations will be provided.

The medical material in the Chapter on Medicine lacks details or explanations. We are often informed that a monk or monks were sick with an illness in a case, and certain treatment was allowed for use by the Buddha. But there is no explanation for the illness/remedy or the prescribed drug(s) in the source. Interpretations of the drugs in the Chapter on Medicine will be given in the Chapter on Medicine in Chapter Four, as well as those of the diseases and treatments in Chapter Five. Thus readers, who are interested in apprehending the medical details contained in this Buddhist text, can better understand what information is being conveyed and may find such material meaningful, inspiring, and even applicable. Thirdly, English translation of these medical facts in the Chapter on Medicine is not yet available (except the Pāli Bhesajjhakkhandhaka which has been translated by Isaline Horner and published in The Book of Discipline by the Pali Text Society). By rendering the medical data into English from the primary sources–which are extant in Pāli, Sanskrit, classical Chinese, and classical Tibetan–the present thesis will furnish readers with English translation of these records. Readers can then be able to know what is preserved in the source materials.

Lastly, for studying the ancient medical information in the Chapter on Medicine, a suitable method is required. The method devised for the present research–a three-fold interpretative method–will be detailed in the next section. Such a method not only can be used for examining the medical cases and drugs in the Chapter on Medicine, but also can be applied for examining medical material found in other Buddhist texts (or even other historical documents).
