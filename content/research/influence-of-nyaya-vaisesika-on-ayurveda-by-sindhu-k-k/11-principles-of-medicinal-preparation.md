11. Principles of medicinal preparation

An important aspect of Indian medicine dealing with the identification, description, and classification of drugs. (pharmacology) and also the preparations, and administration of drugs, (viz., pharmacy, baisajya). This aspect has been recognized as the foremost among the eight limbs of the present day Ayurveda pharmacology, diagnostics, therapeutics, major surgery, minor surgery. psychotherapy, toxicology and 239

pediatrics, (Dravyaguna, Nidana, Keyacikitso, Salya, Salakya, Bhuta vidya, Agadatantra kaumara brtya) The foundation of this aspect is to be formed in the concept of substance (dravya), as crystalized in the Nyaya-Vaisesika system. The science of pharmacy aims at retaining the medicinal properties of the ingredients of drugs with necessary modification .This modification, Caraka declares, is brought about by dilution, application of heat, clarification, emulsification, storing, maturing, flavoring, impregnation, and preservation, as also by the material of the receptacle. As for as medicine is concerned, among the categories of existence, substance is the most important, for the other categories are dependent upon it. Substances are known by the properties they possess (guna) and the action they cause in the organism (karma) the science of dravya-guna, therefore, inquires into the nature of substances and the medicinal value they have in order to preserve 240

the health of the individual as well as to care the discusses that he may suffer from. Substances, from this point of view, comprehend both article, of food (ahara) and drugs (ausada). In a substance are to be found five categories of existence (pancapadartha): properties (guna), tastes (rasa), systemic changes (vipaka), potencies (virya) and specific effects (prabhava). Each of which discharges its own function. This aspect of Ayurveda deals with all these details. Brodly, however, three details are recognized drugs (dravya) their therapeutic properties (guna) and their pharmacological actions (karma). Drug: Substances which have medical property are broadly classified into three groups. ( 1) articles of food (ahara) (2) medicinal preparations or drugs (ausadha) and (3) poisons (visa) The basic idea is that besides the actual drugs, food as well as poison can be employed for therapeutic ends. 241

Drugs listed in the classical pharmacological works include animal products (pranijanya), substance from the plant kingdom (vanaspati janya), and minerals and metals (Khanija), Drugs of vegetable origin are classified according to the this kind of country they are grown in, and the soil where the drug grown is an important consideration. In accordance with the pancabhautika theory, the soil can be one of five types depending on the predominance of the primary element. (mahabhuta). 104
