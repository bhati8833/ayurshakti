12. Concept of mind, Indriyas and Indriyarthas

Life is defined as a combination of sarira (body) indriya (senses) manas (mind) and atma (soul) According to Ayurveda indriya and indriyarthas are considered as evolved from pancamahabhutas,. and also this is the theory postulated by Vaisesika. The five sense organs (pancendriyas) are formed mainly of one particular bhuta and the sense it perceives is the 242

specific quality of that particular bhuta. Susruta has accepted the theory of evolution as postulated by Samkhya Philosophy. "pancabhautikatindriyapyayarvede varnyante tathendriyarthih | "105 In Caraka samhita sarirasthana, Caraka deals the science relating to human body in respect of its cause, origin, maintenance, growth etc. A human body is regarded "as a modification of the five elements. Ether, air, fire, water and earth. 'Khadhi indicate them, which include five senses as well, cetana, is consciousness consisting of soul associated with mind. 106 The concept of purusa is accepted by the Vaisesika system and is utilized in medicine. The same saddhatuka purusa, is define by Susruta. The word purusa is derived as that who lies in the body. 108 Pure consciousness is also known as purusa but this is not so practical from the point by view of medicine which deals with the saddhatuka purusa. 109 243

Though the word purusa is applied to all living beings, mainly it is used for human being which is the most important all.11 Again from the division of constituents he is known as possessing twentyfour entities such as mind ten sense organs, five sense objects and prakrti ( matter) consisting of eight entities ( Avyakta, mahat Ahamkara and five tanmatras). Here manas (mind) is defined with its specific character and properties. Conjunction of mind with sense senses in the invariable factor in perception. If mind is there perception is there, or the other hand, if mind is absent, perception is also absent. 111 Thus by law of agreement in presence an absence, mind is proved as cause of perception. similar definition are 112 found in the mind Nyaya-Vaisesika. In language, Carakas definition looks closer to that of Vaisesika. 244

"cintyam vicaryamuhyam ca dhyeyam samkalpyameva ca | " 113 The objects and functions of mind are mentioned. Cintya, vicarya, uhya, dhyeya and sankalpa, these five are the objects of mind. Cintya is the consideration about worth doing or otherwise. Vicarya is that of critical analysis about rightness or otherwise. Uhya is conjecture or hypothesis is about emotional and thinking. Sankalpya is that about which merit or demerit it is consider.
