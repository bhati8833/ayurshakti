Study of Shringataka Marma

Shringataka Etymology—This is derived from the word Sring + ada + gatau. The word sringam is derived from the root Shr=himsayam. (Summit of a mountain.) A place where four roads meet. Description In the middle of the siras that irrigate the nose, ear, eye and tongue, there is a cross way or the sringhatakas They are four in number. According to Susruta they are sira marma and Vaghbata says that they are dhamani marma. They are sadya pranahara marma four anguli in extent. Facial artery and vein Lingual nerve and superior pharyngeal constrictor muscle Ramus of mandible Inferior alveolar artery vein and nerve Masseter muscle Styloglossus muscle Facial nerve External carotid artery Parotid gland Palatine tonsil Plate No. 12: The anatomical area of Sringataka 143 Medial pterygoid muscle

Surface Anatomy The anatomical situation of this marma is on the surface of the brain corresponding to the location of nerve centres which consist of nerve cells and fibres which are responsible for the functions of speech (Broca's centre) vision, hearing, taste and smell. Injury to any one of these four, injures the basal ganglia leading to death. Anatomical Structures corresponding to this marma Brocas centre Discussion ☐ ☐ Visio sensory centre ☐ Centre for hearing ☐ Centre for taste and smell. ☐ Soft palate The site of this marma is in Thalu (soft palate) where the cavities of the four Indriyas (The sense of taste, hearing, smell, and vision) meet. Any injury here will lead to injury to medulla oblongata which leads to bulbar paralysis and above said symptoms. The opening of all these in the thalu are termed as Shringataka marma which are four in numbers. Being a Sira marma and four in number this is also suggestive of the Circulus arteriosus of Willis. 144

Tamil varma sastra Patchi varma is situated 1.5 cms. below the centre of the eye joint above the sema kalam. This is termed as Pacci because just like various birds flocking will lead to the description. This also belongs to a peculiar group of varma disorders. Both the lower and upper jaws will be pulled to one side. Even if there is relief, after one month jaws will be pulled to one side. Kannaadi varmam is otherwise called Mookirani varmam. It is located on the bridge of the nose where the spectacles rest. In case of any impact sustained at Kannaadi varmam, the patient will becomes unconscious. The victim will lose strength. Blood will come out of the nose and ears. If the impact is of severe nature, the victim will die. Within one hour and thirty minutes, the victim should be retrieved. Bala varma is situated 1.5cms. below the Nema varmam which is 1.5cms., below the centre of the eye. Disturbance at this life-centre leads to convulsion (Udhira-sanni) and increase in phlegm 145

(Silerpanam) accompanied by active ulcer (Ashta gunmam), muscle pull (kolutthu) and epilepsy (valippu). If the disturbance is of mild nature, tuberculosis, anemias and asthma (chayam), paandu and Eelai may occur in later stages. This life-centre too belongs to peculiar type of life-centres (varmams). Kulayana marma In Kulayana marma this has close relation with Mookkadappan which is described as follows. The site of this marma is below the eyebrows just above the starting of the nasal cavity. If injured the opponent faints with profuse bleeding from the nostrils. He can be brought to normalcy by pressing the nasal pyramid (Nose).
