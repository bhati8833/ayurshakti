Study of Sthanamula Marma

Sthanamula Etymology—These two words Sthanamoola and sthanarohita are combination of two words Sthana + mula and sthana + rohita. Sthana dhatu is usually used as Sabdartha. Infants cry for this. In other context this signifies the beauty of a woman. Stan means hollow resonance of human breast. Description Situated two fingers below the breast on both sides. This is a sira marma and is kalantara pranahara. It is two anguli in extent. Injury to this marma endangers life by filling the koshtam with kapha and causes cough and dyspnoea. The fatal cause is kasa 170

and swasa with kapha poorna kosta. Its bhuta structure is Ap and Teja. Adhidevatha is Iswara. Surface Anatomy This marma can be palpated in sitting Internal thoracic artery and its perforating branches Axillary artery Medial mammary, branches Brachial plexus Subclavian artery Brachial artery Lateral thoracic artery and Lateral mammary branches Lateral mammary branches of lateral cutaneous branches of posterior intercostal arteries. Plate No. 18: The anatomical area of Sthanamula and Sthanarohita posture preferably on bare chest. The physician has to slightly role or press between the fingers to observe its tenderness. Anatomical structures corresponding to this marma ☐ The bases of lungs on both. 171

Discussion ☐ Left Side Pericardium, Heart Inferior margin of the heart, and the apex of the heart. ☐ Right Side Lower lobe of the lung. Kaphapoorna kosta invariably denotes collection of fluid in the pleural cavity that causes dyspnoeae and cough. Some interprets this as pleurisy and pneumonia. Pleurisy is a condition in which fluid is accumulated in the pleural cavity. Trauma is considered to be one of the major cause of the pleurisy. It may be due to some external cause like fall, accidents, stabbing etc., or due to internal cause like complications of surgeries etc. If not properly treated will lead to death. Next condition of kaphapoorna kostatha is suggestive as pneumonia. The cause of pneumonia is usually Pneumococci. Pneumococci due to traumatic condition is that the micro organism entering bronchi through the wound. This is kalanthara pranahara marma. The death may be due to inflammation caused by Pneumococci. This condition is said to have some similarity with Swasanka Jwara (Dr. Gananath Sen's). Blast lung is yet another condition in which this have some similarity. Here echymosis develop in the lung between the chest wall and 172

diaphragm. This may kill the patient by developing cardiac arrhythmias. If he survives, he coughs up blood stained sputum which gradually turns brown over a period of time. Dyspnoeae may be severe and broncopneumonia may rapidly develop. The capillary rupture and interstitial hemorrhages seen in the lower part of the chest is suggestive of sthanamula marmabhighata.
