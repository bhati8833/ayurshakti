Nila and Manya Marma1—Etymology—The word Nila is usually used to denote the blue colour dyed with indigo, dark blue. The word Manya is derived from the word Man = Jnane or Man = Avabodhane. This is also 102

defined as that structure which becomes prominent during death time. Manya = Honor probably due to its connection with the voice. Description According to Vaghbata, there are four siras on either sides of kanta nadi corresponding to hanu sandhi (Tempero mandibular joint) named as Nila and Manya. They are two in number each. If subjected to injury they cause absence of voice, alteration of sound, loss of sense of taste. Susruta says that there are four dhamani on either sides of Kantha Nadi. Two of them are known as Nila and other two as Manya. One Nila and one Manya are situated on either side of Larynx. An injury to any of these produces dumbness and change of voice (hoarseness). This is a Sira marma, and Vaikalyakara marma. Its bhuta structure is Jala. Adhidevata is Isvara. Surface Anatomy Enough care should be taken in palpating this marma as the structures adjoining the neck are very delicate. Use less pressure while palpating with fingers. So one should come to the conclusion that these sira and dhamanis are nothing but blood vessels. Susruta says 105

that they are all seen differently which means that two Nila and two Manya are seen separately. There are two veins and two arteries. Nila literally means blue and hence can be correlated as veins and other two 2 structures as arteries. Charaka says that if Manya does not pulsate in a person he dies. Commenting on this Chakrapani says that the Manya are two pulsating dhamanis seen on either side of the neck. Pulse is the characteristic feature of arteries, the veins will have a very feeble and weak pulse. From the above we can conclude that the Manya are Carotid Arteries and Nila are Jugular veins. The symptoms on damage of this marma are neurological. Hence the nerves passing through this area should also be taken into consideration. Another contradiction generally seen is that these are vaikalyakara marma. If they are told to be carotid artery and jugular veins, their injury will definitely lead to profuse bleeding and death. This varmam will be affected if hit at a distance of 41cms. The affected person will pass blood stained urine. The affected victim should be treated within two hours. Sanguthiri varmam, otherwise known as Poottu varmam is situated on the two sides of the glottis. If Sanguthiri varmam is affected. The victim will have the following signs and symptoms; Loss of breath, facial paralysis, swelling of abdomen, loss of voice, rolling of eyeball and chillness of the body. The patient who is affected with impact at Sanguthiri life-centre should be attended to immediately. In most of the cases the victim may become unconscious. Sumai varmam is situated 4 cms.
