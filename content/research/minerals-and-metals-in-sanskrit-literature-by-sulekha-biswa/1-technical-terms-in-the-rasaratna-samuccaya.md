1. Technical Terms in the Rasaratna-samuccaya

[Full title: Technical Terms related to Minerals and Metals in the Rasaratna-samuccaya]

The ancient or Hindu period in Indian history is supposed to have ended around twelfth century A.D. with the onset of Muslim influence in the sub-continent. However, the writing of Sanskrit texts continued even after this date. An early fourteenth century compilation—Rasa -Ratna -samuccaya (hereafter abbreviated as would be the terminal point in our discussion, even though some more iatrochemical texts appeared after Rasaratna-samuccaya (Ray, 1956:158-207) Rasaratna-samuccaya) Technical Terms Related to Mineral and Metal Processing Sri Vagbhata compiled in Rasaratna-samuccaya many details regarding gems, non-gem minerals, metals, alloys, metallic transformations (lohavedh and the transformations of body tissues (dehavedha). For the conversion of minerals to a pharmaceutical recipe, acceptable to the body, several steps had been envisaged: purification (sodhana) (marana), metallic extraction (satvapatana), liquefaction (druti) et incinerati Sodhana by actate (amla) and/or alkaline (Kaara) material (8.59-8.60) was designed to remove the toxic material. Satva was defined as the metallic extract: ksara amladravakayuktam dhyatam akarakostha ke | yastato nirgatah sarah satvam iti abhidhiyate ||  (Rasaratna-samuccaya 8.34)

White flame at the time metal is released is called suddhavarta: suddhavartastada jneya sa kalah satvanirgame |  (Rasaratna-samuccaya 8.56) IX-2 Bijavarta is the characteristic colour of flame when the metal produced is about to melt (8.57)= The metal could be cooled in the furnace (swanca sita) or out side, bahihsita (8.58). Marana means killing or oxidation of metal from which metal can be regenerated (utthapana) (8.39). If the metal is oxidised irreversibly, then it is called apunarbhava (8.29) Removal of a metallic impurity by blowing through a bent pipe (yankanala) in a way it is converted to an oxide scum is called tadana (8.32). easy Dhalana meant pouring of molten metal. Capala meant melting oxide of lead or naga, tin or vanga etc. (8.41-8.44) The concepts of alloy (misraloha) and heat-treatment were first put into literature by Somadeva in his Rasendra Cudamani, and Rasaratna-samuccaya followed Somadeva. Nirvapana or nirvahana was defined as the art of putting one metal into another through vankanala (8.24-8.25) This was probably practised by introducing Zinc vapour into molten copper to make brass. Addition of foreign substance to molten metal was also known as avapa or prativapa (8.52).

IX-3 Two different procedures of quenching were described Abhiseka meant pouring of water all around on a (molten) metal. Dipping of hot metal in liquid or water was known as nirvana or Snapana salilasya pariksepah sah abhisekah iti stutah | naprasya apsu viniksepo nirvanah svapanam ca tat  mercury. (8.53-54) Some of the technical terms were specifically related to This liquid metal was purified by patana or distillation, which involved 'movement of vapour upwards, downwards and obliquely from the distillation equipment and thus removal from impurities such as tin and lead': mardita paradasya yantrasthitasya urdhvam adhasca niryak niryatanam patanam samjna uttam vagahi samparka kamcukanjanam ||  (Rasaratna-samuccaya 8.64) Vedhana Or Mercury used to be converted to oxide or sulphide or solid amalgam 'in which fluidity was lost' (nasta pista). Vida was the treatment with ksara, amla, gandhaka, Lavana etc. prior to grasa or jarana, or amalgamation of metal with mercury (8.67, 8.84). transmutation of base metals to silver or gold was expected by pasting mercury (lepa vedha) on the base metal, or by putting mercury into the molten base metal (Ksepa vedha) Iatrochemistry had not yet snapped its extravagant alchemical roots.

IX-4 and Rasaratna-samuccaya contains a wide-ranging list of implements A equipments to be used in the alchemical laboratory (Chapters 5 and 7). Several kinds of crucibles and furnaces (musa, Kosthi, puta) were described (Chapters 5, 9 and 10). The author, Sri Vagbhata, openly acknowledged that his predecessor, Somadeva had collected most of the technical terms and their lucid descriptions from the earlier texts of Rasasastra, and thus prepared a garland of jewels to be worn in an assembly of scholars': sphutatara paribhasanam rakhani hutva  .... .... kanthamalam .... mandanartha sabhayama  1 Rasaratna-samuccaya 8.98
