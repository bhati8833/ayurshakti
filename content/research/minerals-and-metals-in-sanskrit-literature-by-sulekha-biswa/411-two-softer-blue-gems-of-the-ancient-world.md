Two Softer Blue Gems of the Ancient World VII-52 Turquoise (hardness 6) and Lapis Lazuli (5) are famous as two relatively soft blue gems. These were widely used in the ancient world, which had not yet invented the techniques to cut and process the harder gems such as blue sapphire (9). Turquoise is a hydrous phosphate of aluminium and copper. It may be considered to be an aggregate of orthophosphoric acid molecules hooked with (CuOH) and A 1 (OH)2 radicals, the tentative formula being H₂ (CuOH) [A 1 (OH)2]6 (PO 4) 4. The sky blue, dark blue or bluish colour is due to copper. 4 In the Harappan period, much of the supply of turquoise came from Persia, near Nishapur in Khorasan. The turquoise mines over there, existing in rocks consisting of porphyritic trachytes, are of great antiquity. The material was always in demand in India and imported, unless for some reason or other, the trade links. were snapped. The turquoise of Nishapur was considered to be as precious as ruby and sapphire of Sri Lanka and the emerald of Egypt. The name turquoise is the French word for Turkish; the mineral travelled to the West from Persia through Turkey. VII-55 The name Lapis Lazuli was most possibly derived from the Persian lazhward, meaning blue colour. In some Sanskrit text, this name was retained: : lajavartah alpasah raktah nilimamisrita prabha  'Lajavarta is coloured blue slightly reddish' (Agastiva Ratnapariksa 93). In another text, it was mentioned as rajavarta: 'nigaram asitam svaccham nilasvaccham samam guru sikhikantha drdham suddham rajavarte guna dasa -  'Rajavarta is not white (distinguished from white calcite which may be associated with it author) but blue or dark blue like neck of a peacock' (Laghu-Ratnapariksa 19). the Rasa-Ratna-Samuccaya (Rasaratna-samuccaya) mentioned in its chapter four, that rajavarta has a mixed shade of blue and slight reddish colour, and that it is heavy and smooth. All the Sanskrit texts referring to lapis lazuli mention that it is quru or heavy. Its specific gravity however is actually low (2.4), much lower than that of blue sapphire (~4.1) and on this point, the ancient scholars might have confused one kind with another. Rasaratna-samuccaya further mentioned sativapatana or metal extraction from rajavarta. We wonder what metal was extracted from this gem. Possibly the author of Rasaratna-samuccaya confused sasyaka, a blue copper mineral, from which copper metal was extracted (Rasaratna-samuccaya 2.125-2.133), with rajavarta Before we pass on to coral and pearl, the softest gem minera. we may briefly mention a few minor gem minerals such as vimalaka or the gem variety of pyrite. Rasaratna Samuccaya (Rasaratna-samuccaya) mentioned vimala or pyrite. occuring in several kinds: tara vimala (silver-like iron pyrite or vimalaka), hema vimala (golden chalcopyrite) and kamsya vimala or arsenopyrite (Rasaratna-samuccaya 2.95). These were mentioned as rasas or ores from which metal could be extracted. Only the first one, namely tara vimala or iron pyrite had been used as a minor gem in India and that too well before the thirteenth century A.D. Sasvaka or blue copper mineral (covellite, borite or coppe sulphate crystal?) was mentioned as a minor gem (AS. 2.11.35 and Brihat-samhita 80.5) in the early centuries, but not counted as a gem during the later years. Similarly anjan amulaka, the pigment grade dark blue mineral (carrolite, a cobalt compound? or an antimony compound?) was mentioned in AS. 2.11.35, but must have lost its reputation as a gem in the Post- Christian era. Conch or sankha had been widely used in India throughout the ages, even in pre-Harappan times. BS 80.4 mentioned this as a gem, but not other gemmologist mentioned it, although the material has always been used for other purposes, such as making bangles.

VII-57 A very striking fact is that Arthasastra, otherwise more or less complete in its list of gems, does not mention marakata or emerald which became very popular in the sub-continent during the post-Christian era. This also proves the antiquity of the said text, which is probably the oldest to provide a list of gem-minerals used in India.
