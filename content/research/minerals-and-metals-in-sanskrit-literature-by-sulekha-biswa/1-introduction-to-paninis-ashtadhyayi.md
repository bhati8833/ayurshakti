1. Introduction to Panini’s Ashtadhyayi

Panini's Astadhyavi is a veritable store-house of information (Vasu, 1891) on Sanskrit language and ancient India. We learn quite a lot about minerals and metals in ancient India from Panini, who illustrated his grammatical rules by referring to the words of a very great diversity including scientific and technical words. (Katre, 1968; Vide Appendix 'C'). It is widely accepted that Panini was born at Satatura which has been identified with Lahur, a small town four miles northwest of Ohind, in the angle of the river Kabul meeting the Indus, The question of Panini's date has been widely debated. Summarising the various arguments, Agrawala (1953: 455-475) has noted that the various dates assigned by scholars to Panini range from the seventh to the fourth century B.C., the majority of scholars being inclined towards the fifth and the fourth century B.C. Agrawala himself favours a date about the middle of the fifth century B.C. and suggests that he was a contemporary of the Nanda King named Mahananda. The scholars who prefer the later dates presume that Panini's work has not been touched or amended by his followers. This assumption may not be perfectly valid. At least in one case we find that the reference to the Kedara gold coin (Pan. 5.2.120) corresponds to the Post-Christian era and therefore could hardly have been made

IV-2 by Panini himself. We may conclude that Panini lived around 500 B.C. near the ancient city of Taksasila or Taxila after this was founded under the Achaemenid influence. The second urbanisation in India statted around the early part of the first millennium B.C. Settlements grew into small kingdoms or janapada about which an early reference is available in Chandogya Upanisad (5.11.5). There must have been a boost to mining and metallurgy in the north-western part of India after the partly successful intrusion of the Persians into India during the later half of the sixth century B.C. Panini's work reveals the contemporary awareness and activities in the fields of (a) mine and mineral engineering (b) extractive metallurgy (c) minerals and inorganic compounds (d) metals (e) coinage metals besides the development of several other technical and scientific concepts.
