2. Gem Minerals discussed in the Rasaratna-samuccaya

In one of our previous chapters (VII) we have mentioned. the references to ratna or gem minerals in Rasaratna-samuccaya (Chapter 2), which is indeed an unique compilation (samuccaya) of Rasa-as well as Ratna-sastra text materials. Here we would merely emphasize that Rasaratna-samuccaya believed the gem minerals to be potential sources of medicine. Hard minerals for example, when suitably administered, were believed to make the human tissues strong. Combined with mercury, precious stones could become medicines. This was another alchemical belief. Emerald was known as tarkava, gem was called vidurcaka Cat's eye tark sya, garudodgara etc. Diamond was known as vajra as well as bhidura. Diamond was classified under three 'genders' masculine, feminine and neutral as well as under four conventional castes. (Rasaratna-samuccaya 4.26-4.30).

IX-5 Vaikranta has been described (Rasaratna-samuccaya 2.53-2.60) as 'possessing eight edges, eight facets, six angles, smooth and heavy, associated with one or more of eight kinds of colours, occurring around Vindhy able to cut down or deform the metals': vikrntayati lohani tena vaikantakah smrtah  (Rasaratna-samuccaya 2.60) The above description reminds us of a similar one for diamond (4.27), and it is possible that vaikranta of Rasaratna-samuccaya was indeed diamond of different colours. However the reference to satvapatana of the gem or extraction of metal from it (2.69-2.71) makes us wonder whether the name was also applied to some other hard mineral containing iron (magnetite) or manganese. Many authors have trans™ -lated valkranta as tourmaline.
