6. Origin of Pearls: The Mythological Theory

We have indicated earlier that the ancient Sanskrit texts propagated the view that gems on the earth had been produced from different constituents of the body of the slain demon Bala. His teeth were converted to pearls. Ratnapariksa (Ratna-pariksha 72-74) and Garuda Purana (Garuda-purana 1.69.20-22) described that the seeds (Bala's teeth) fell from the sky along with water from the clouds, and both together entered the oyster shell and were converted into pearls.... Agastimata (Agastimata 107) provided the same description. Manimala quoted a Sanskrit text (Manimala 271) saying that twenty-seven kinds of pearls were generated in accordance with twenty-seven different stellar constellations, as Balas teeth fell over a long period of time. Nine of these kinds were heavy, nine intermediate in density and the remainder nine kinds were light.

VII-76 The best pearls which (originated and still) orginate in water drops falling upon oysters, when the sun rests on the Swati (Arcturus forming the fifteenth lunar asterism) star, are flawless and bright (Navaratnapariksa 69 and Manimala 286) : svatyam sthite khau medhairmukta ye jalabindavah te ginih suktimirmukta jayante nirmalalisa  Similar descriptions involving the beneficial influence of the Swati star were given in other texts: putita jaladhemadhye samutpata ca suktikah  || svati parjanya samyogat suktirgarbha vibharti sa ||  ( 109) sarvesu akaresu ca suktipudhe svati rkse jalayoge jayante bhauktikani sarvalamkara janakani ||  (Rayanaparikkha 50) Manimala further added (287) that the size of the pearls. varies in strict concomittance with the size of the water drops falling upon oysters i.e. a large drop producing a proportionally large pearl, and a small drop, a proportionately small one.
