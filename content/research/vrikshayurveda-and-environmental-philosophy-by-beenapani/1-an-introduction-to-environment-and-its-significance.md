CHAPTER-I The Purport of Environment and the Significance of Environmental Philosophy—I.0. An Introduction to Environment and its Significance:—The word environment is most commonly used in describing natural environment, which means the sum-total of all living creatures and non-living things that surround an organism or group of organisms. The dictionary meaning of the word environment is the act of environing or the state of being environed or that which environs or surrounds various objects, regions, circumstances, surrounding conditions, influence or forces that include animals, tree and insects etc. also along with the five mahabhutas namely, prthivi, ap, tejas, vayu and akasa. Which are being modified in the process of their growth and development? It is therefore the aggregate of surrounding things, conditions, or influences of different surrounding circumstances. Environment includes all the elements, biotic and abiotic factors and conditions that have some impact on growth and development of certain organisms. Out of, the two basic components, namely, biotic (living organisms) and

37 abiotic (non-living materials) factors, plant or vrksa belongs to the biotic component major constituent and the farming the environment. Abiotic factors such as light, temperature, water, atmospheric gases combine with biotic factors (all surrounding living species). The environmental conditions often changes after sometime and therefore many organisms have to enable themselves in adapting these changes. However, tolerance for such a change to be acceptable by every individual organism and their ranges are not the same with all species and as such exposure to environmental conditions at the limit of a certain organism's tolerance range represents the stress on environment. Thus, for the survival of any species, be it flora or fauna, the maintenance of the environment is essential. The organisms are totally dependent upon the environment and are intimately related to it. The surroundings in which an animal or plant lives have direct influence on the activities of the living organisms. Life and environment are interdependent to each other. The plant and animal life is thus affected by various environmental factors and in turn they modify their environment in various ways. Earth consists of land, water, air and many living organisms. Land, water and air remaining in a combination, form one group

38 called the physical environment. All the living organisms belonging to another group form the biological environment. Whereas, the physical environment is essential for the existence of life, the biological environment provides food and other basic needs to sustain life. However, as to the growth in time the aspects of environmental awareness in order to be brought to the surface line, a glimpse knowledge of ancient Indians towards nature and everything that surrounds life is thus to be understood. Therefore, the aim is to derive a deep insight out of such glimpse knowledge which would benefit the trends of modern society as well as the technologically advanced human being. Experts have therefore rightly judged that the development of science and technology should coincide with the balance of human values. Thus, an access to such ancient knowledge would definitely unveil the traditional ideas that might help the modern man in his discourse towards the advancements in scientific and technological developments. Now, to present such ancient knowledge on nature and its awareness, let us start with the basic literature review from Rigveda which provides the earliest picture of nature. The descriptions encrypted in Rigveda are rather in the form of poetry of nature
