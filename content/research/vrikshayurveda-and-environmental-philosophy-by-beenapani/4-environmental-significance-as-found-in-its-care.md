4. Environmental Significance as found in its Care

Our Ancient Sanskrit Literature is filled with various valuable principles. Ignoring all these values we are exploiting the

155 mother earth in order to satisfy our greed, lust and material urges. The mother earth has been plundered and has become unsafe place to live in now. The significance of the environmental values is well evident and clearly reflected in the part of the text where care for trees and plants is concerned. Different other texts of Vrikshayurveda also describes the importance of trees, the significant role they maintains in the eco-system and their care and protection are thus to be taken by man. Different texts of Vrikshayurveda deal with its chapter very clearly. Out of them we will discuss here the care, protection and affection of human beings towards trees and plants. Now, a glimpse review of the text by Surapala who narrates the aspects of protection by applying the water of carass (kunapa jala) nurture, care from diseases and treatment in its 6th, 7th, 8th and 9th chapters respectively. Surapala, the author of Vrikshayurveda describes that, on the earth trees gives us happiness and are cause of our all welfare. They save us from abject poverty and they are named as taravah (the savior). So, we should take care of them with labour (Vrikshayurveda, v.97). The text reveals in the verse (Vrikshayurveda, v.98) as follows:

156 ato dharmarthakamanam chayapuspa phaladibhih / prasadhakatama vrksa palaniyah prayatnatah // (Vrikshayurveda v.98) The trees through their shade, flowers and fruits help immensely for procuring duty (dharma), material prosperity (artha) and pleasure (Kama). Hence, they are to be nurtured carefully (v.98) An expert should protect the plants by coating with sesame oil cake, and krmisutra (vidanga), by sprinkling mixture of milk and water, the liquid fertilizer kunapa water, and by fumigating with ghee (Vrikshayurveda, v.100). The quintessence of v.121 144 taken from 7th ch. of the text signifying nourishment techniques lays down different procedures prescribing separately for trees and plants to be laden with fruits and flowers: Creepers bearing flowers and fruits (Vrikshayurveda, v.121), grape creepers bending down with flowers and fruits (Vrikshayurveda, v.122), mango tree with sweeter and bigger fruits (Vrkd, v.123), on coconut trees loaded with weights of huge fruit (Vrikshayurveda, v. 124 126), on breadfruit, date and kamala (Vrikshayurveda, v.127), pomegranate trees with juicier seeds of bigger variety (Vrikshayurveda, v.128 - 132), jackfruit tree bear many seedless and sweeter fruits of bigger size

157 (Vrikshayurveda, v.133), kola bear fruits being attractive, as big as that of the bilva fruit and taste like nectar (Vrk, v.134), karkandhu, lakuca, badari, dhatri, and jambu at blossoming time beat good fruits of big size with nectar like taste (Vrikshayurveda, v.135, 136), the bilva and kapittha trees bear plenty of juicy fruits (Vrikshayurveda, v.137), tinduka and paravata trees bend down with fruits (Vrikshayurveda, v.139), the matulungi bends down with fruits which are extremely sweet, soft, fleshy and big like a jar (Vrikshayurveda, v.140), the bijapuraka trees bear good quality fruits (Vrikshayurveda,v.141), madhuka trees bear flowers which are fragrant like camphor-dust (Vrikshayurveda, v.143), syama, kadamba, and karikesaraka bend down with branches of fragrant flowers (Vrikshayurveda, v.144). Newly planted trees should be protected from scorching heat of Sun, so long as the color of their foliage is not similar to that of the coral (Vrikshayurveda, v.108). The weed near the tree restricts the growth of the tree. Thus, everybody should weed out grasses from the areas of proximity to trees, and a wise man should dig it out with a spade near the roots of the tree (Vrikshayurveda, v.115).

158 After planting the trees, the field should be protected from rats, grasshoppers, worms etc. Surapala has mentioned an incantation (mantra) to protect the field from these creatures. The following mantra should be written on a plantation leaf with lace dye, should be kept at the centre of the field in a triangle or in the corner of the field. The mantra is as follows: yatha svasti! kiskindhatah paramabhattaraka paramesvaraparamavaisnava prakata parakrama Vijitarkamandalopavitah srimaddhanumadeva -carana vijayino amukaksetram musakagandhikasalabhadin samajnapayanti // (Vrikshayurveda,V.161.) yatha etad rajadesa pattakadarsanadeva ksetramidam vihayanyatra yasya tha/ no cet! hana radeha - rayacaravajra langulena hum phat svaha // (Vrikshayurveda , V.162.) The meaning of this inchantation is, "Thus may it be well: from kiskindha, the great Lord, the great devotee of Visnu, one who has revealed his valour, one who has conquered the orb of Sun and bears the sacred thread, the illustrious God Hanuman's victorious feet threatens the rats, locusts, mouths etc. That by seeing the document of royal order you will leave this field and go elsewhere.

159 If they do not obey the order, the (you Hanuman), kill them rend them here with your vast moving hard tail". Ham, phat, svaha".3 50 After writing this mantra properly on a leaf and after reciting it, it should be kept beneath the ground after digging a little. By this the keca (kencua), Worm (kita), grasshopper (patanga) rats, onto (Pipila) etc. then disappear (Vrikshayurveda, v.163).
