3.1. Introduction to Environmental Ethics

I.2. Division of Environmental Philosophy: I.2.1. Environmental Ethics: Environmental ethics as a branch of environmental philosophy is the study of normative issues and principles relating to human interactions with the natural environment. Though, human beings and all other living beings and creatures are part and partial of the society, their lives are guided by certain philosophical principles. They in turn form an integral part of the environment.

42 As such, the plants and animals too being guided with those principles have to abide with the moral and ethical values even. Thus, the study compares an increasingly significant field of applied ethics, crucial for the guidance of individuals in shaping the principles affecting their actions and policies across the entire range of environmental issues. I.2.1.1. Environmental Ethics as a branch of Philosophy: The discipline of philosophy can be divided into fields. Typically it is divided into three. In the simplest terms: metaphysics is the study of the fundamental nature of reality; epistemology is the study of knowledge, and how we acquire it; ethics is the study of goodness and rightness our reasons for acting in one way rather than another, or our reasons for trying to be one kind of person rather than another. 18 The study of ethics generally is guided by certain presuppositions. Among the main presuppositions are First we are more or less rational beings, capable of understanding the world around us. 18.Attfield R, The Ethics of Environmental Concern.

Second we can act on the basis of what we understand. Third our actions can serve a purpose - we can make a difference.
