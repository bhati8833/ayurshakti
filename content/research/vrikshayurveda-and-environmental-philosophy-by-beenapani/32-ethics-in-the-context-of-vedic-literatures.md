3.2. Ethics in the context of Vedic Literatures

The smrtikar's of ancient times have framed rules and code of ethics for those who hurts or causes wound to the trees, for those who cut them unnecessarily for their self benefits etc. The descriptions of certain offences among them which were covered under the grhavastukam adhyaya of vastu such as offences relating to house (grha), irrigation field (kheta), garden (upavana), dam (setubandha), border (sima), and garden laden with flowers and fruits (phala puspa udyana), pools, ponds (sarovara), etc. are entered onto the code of ethics of Arthasastra (38, 3, 9) and Gautama Grhyasutra (2, 3, 34-35; 2, 6, 19). Manusmrti, enjoins that man should protect the movable and immovable components of the environment by taking into consideration their usefulness for human health and welfare. Matsyapurana in its descriptions of the penal consequences as stated in the words of Manu reads that the offender one who cuts a tree being laden with sweet fruits shall be punished with gold as

If that tree is nearby to a border area, on any road side, or near a water body like pond, river, reservoir etc. such offender's punishment shall be doubled. vrksam tu saphalam cchittva suvarna danda marhati / dvigunam dandayeccainam pathi simri jalasaye || ( Matsyapurana, 227,91) If one conducts the offence of cutting a tree not bearing fruits, he shall be punished with a moderate punishment amounting to a penalty of 500 pana. chedanada phalasyapi madhyamam sahasamsmrtam // (Matsyapurana, 227, 91 ; Manusmrti, VIII, 285) Now a day the farmers are being provided with seeds of crop in a subsided rate by the Government for propagation of agricultural products. But, sometimes the seeds availed from the Government in such subsided rate are found unprocessed with proper nutrient value as such the farmers of today have to bear heavy loss. Adulteration of seeds is a much discussed corruption of the present day causing untold miseries to the farmers. Although, Kautilya's Arthasastra is a work on Indian polity, it lays down certain rules and principles of statecraft for the king to preserve and maintain the environment as a part of their machinery. Here, he refers to such principles as the code of ethics being treated to be superintendent of forests. This indicates that forests were protected by certain governing principles called the code of ethics. Thus, forest as an intrinsic part of the natural environment needs to be safeguarded from the evil forces by the application of such guided principles. Forest as such plays a significant role in his writings of Arthasastra. Kautilya prescribes many such rules and principles as follows: A king should construct such sahodakam (i.e. a river or a stream of water where water reserves continuously), reservoirs (Vapi), and dams or should preserve the water running out from any hill or mountain in the process of framing a sustainable system for irrigation.
