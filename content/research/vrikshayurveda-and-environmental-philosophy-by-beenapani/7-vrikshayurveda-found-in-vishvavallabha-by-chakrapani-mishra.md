7. Vrikshayurveda found in Vishvavallabha by Chakrapani Mishra

Like Vrksayurveda of Surapala, there is another treatise written by Chakrapani Mishra named as Visvavallabha (Dear to the World). The author here has named the book as "Visvavallabha" [Viswa-Vallabha] because the subject of plant life is dearer to all. Any book on the subject of plant life and tree plantation would directly or indirectly correlate to the core text of this present research work on "VrksA". However, independent treatise on this subject came to light only with the publication of Surapal's VrksA. The emergence of VrksA as a conspicuous and distinct discipline was thus established possibly by 550 B.C. "Visvavallabha"(Vishvavallabha ) is composed by Chakrapani Mishra who worked under the patronage of Maharana Pratap, the great ruler of Mewar region of Rajasthan in Western India belonging to the Guhilot dynasty.

132 The text 'Vishvavallabha ' closely resembles Surapal's Vrikshayurveda and dealing with subject of plant life. Chakrapani has composed this work in a systematic manner by neatly dividing the topics of the subject into nine chapters with direct and simple language without any style and striking expression. In the opening verse of 'Vishvavallabha ', salutations are offered at the feet of 'Raghavendra' and to those of the authors preceptors from whom he leant the secrets of the science handed down by traditions. In verse (ID-1) the author specifically mentioned the name of Varahamihira the author of Brihat-vamhita , Saramgadhara, and the author of SarangadharaPadhati. The text of Chakrapani contains information on the following aspects of plant life: 1. 2. 3. tam lam vam  A. 5. 1 Tracing ground water, Construction of water reservoirs, Classification of land, Propagation and plantation various trees, Water management, Protection and care of trees, 6. 7. 8. Nourishment and growth of trees, Diseases of trees and their treatment, 9. Botanical wonders etc.

133 The topics are described below: 1. The first chapter of the text gives us interesting information about availability of ground water, types of land, natural vegetation, rock structure and variety of rocks, condition of deserts and hilly lands. The authors have generally classified land into three categories i.e. arid, wet and ordinary. But in verse-3 of chapter-I, chakrapani has classified land in a different manner. He derives land into two categories i.e. Landlevel and Mountainous area. He subdivides level land further into arid, desert, wet and ordinary land. He subdivides mountain land into arid and wet. He has taken this division of land for tracing ground water. The author states that there are nine main ground water currents gushing into nine directions. The one that gushes upwards is the strongest. He also states that the origin of these currents is either in a mountain or at the root of a tree flows to a brook. The author has described indication of ground water on dry land from (1A-8-22) verses (15 verses). Again indication of ground water in the desert areas, on wetland, ordinary and mountain land are described clearly by the author Chakrapani.

134 The 2nd chapter of the text deals with construction of water reservoirs after finding out the ground water sources. Water reservoirs are of various shapes and sizes. They are of pools, ponds, wells, potholes, lakes, dams etc. the author has mentioned four types of ponds i.e. Nanda, Bhadra, Jaya and Vijaya (v.12-chII) and seven types of wells i.e. Bijaya, Dundubhi, Chudamani, Bhadra, Jaya, Nanda and sankar (v.-14-ch-II). The text gives many details about constructing the water reservoirs, on which Surapala is silent. The author also suggests how to cut the hard and soft rocks while digging the wells etc. in 14 verses (Ch-II-22-35). For purification of well water the author has prescribed different natural methods in (verse-II-36-38). If the water of a well is saline, khadira, chalk powder and coconut thrown onto the well, then the water turns into sweet tasting. By putting the powder of kakubha, loha and rajadana in wells or other water reservoirs, turbid, bitter and foul smelling water can be turned into tasty, clean and sweet smelling water. The Vrikshayurveda of Surapal has also prescribed the same things. Chapter-III, of the text deals with the examination of soil for plantation of trees, propagation of seed, trees near residence and plantation inside a fort.

135 The author describes soil three types such as arid, wet and ordinary. It is further divided into six types by taste and taste is determined by the colour of the soil. He denies not to plant trees on lands infested with ant hills, stones and holes, salty nature, which is inauspicious which is distantly located from water sources and which is sandy and poisonous for planting trees (v.-4-Ch-III). He has suggested lists of trees for wet lands, ordinary land and arid land. But like Surapalhe says that if divine power, rulers support and extraordinary effort on the part of the rich, trees and creepers grow even in unfavorable regions yielding fruits and flowers (ChIII-12). The author states that bushes, creepers and trees are propagated from seeds, stalks and bulbs. Some are stated to grow both from seeds and stalks. rambha etc. are grown from both stalks and bulbs. He has restricted to plant thorny trees, trees that ooze milky sap either in the garden or in the vicinity of the house (ChIII-22). Trees such as bijapuraka, jambu, haridra, badari, palasa, sleshmataka, cincha, arjuna, karanjaka, and aksta are in auspicious and should not be grown in the home gardens. Vata is auspicious if planted to the east of the house, udumbarika to the south; plaksa and pippala to the west are auspicious (Ch-III,v.25).

136 Gardens must not be laid to the south-east, south and northeast of a city. They are auspicious when planned in the east, west or north of the city (Ch-III,v.27). He states that depending on the space available an ordinary garden of desirable size should be planned inside a fort. He has also prescribed fruits and flower garden inside the fort. The chapter-IV of Vishvavallabha deals with propagation and plantation of trees. Before plantation pits should be kept ready heating them from inside and filling with soft and good soil. Seeds should be treated with dry cow dung and then with clarified butter and honey. Rain season is the best time for showing all types of seeds and planting trees. Autumn and spring are mediocre season for the purpose. According to the author all seasons are equally good for plantation when extra effort is put on watering. To maintain the distance between the trees, the old theory suggests big trees should be planted at a distance of 20, medium ones at a distance of 16, small varieties at a distance of 12 karas, but Chakrapani says ksirini, badama, lakucha, amalaka, bijapuraka and dadima should be planted leaving a distance of 5,7 and ten karas, champa and others of its class should be planted with 7,10 and 12 karas and chinch, rasala and rajadanaka at a distance of 16 and 12 karas. Considering their class and diameters trees should be planted. If

137 planted densely their branches are stunted and if planted too sparsely they are swept away by strong breezes. asoka, punnaga, sirisa, nimba, champaka cause happiness, mrdvi, jati, phalini etc. creepers are auspicious. They should be planted first in the garden. The Vrikshayurveda of Surapala deals with the same thing. The chapter-V of the text states about water management of trees. The author says that a newly planted tree should be sprinkled every morning and evening, if it does not rain. In rain season plants should be watered at mid-day, winter in the morning, spring in the afternoon and in summer in the evening. The chapter-VI of the text states the protection and care of plants from storm, frost, smoke, fire, insects, cobwebs and rats. By fixing sticks around the plant to support it, protected from strong wind. Trees planted in designs of mandap can be protected from fire. Destroying the rat holes, using medicines and by the help of mantras written on leaves as per Sarangadhar Padhati trees can be protected from rats and insects. The author states that young plants can be protected from frost by constructing temporary sheds covered with grass. The roots should be treated with powdered sesame and sprinkle ashes over it. Cobwebs can be removed by cloth or grass fastened to the end of a stick. The Vrikshayurveda of Surapala also deals with the same things.

138 The chapter-VII deals with nourishment and healthy growth of trees by special natural procedures and the chapter contains 46 verses. The author also states the preparation of kunapa liquid in his text (Ch-VII- v. 2, 3), excluding cows flesh and bones. As per the text kunapa liquid manner is prepared from fat, marrow, skin, blood of ram, sheep, deer, fish and so on should be mixed with water and cooked on fire. When properly cooked, milk and cold water should be added. Oilcake of sesame, honey and clarified butter should be added to the mixture and the pot should be removed from fire and kept in a warm place for a fortnight. This liquid manner is called kunapa. Sprinkling with kunapa is always beneficial to all the trees for increasing the produce of fruits and flowers. In verse 25 Chakrapani suggests a gruel prepared by fermenting karkandhuka, methi, tila, rodhra and curd/buttermilk to induce early flowering. Materials recommended for nourishment were similar to those stated by Surapala but with some modification. These includes milk, ash from chara, washings of rice, powdered mustard, white mustard, sesame, oilcakes, fish, ghee, triphala, decoction of horse gram (Kulatha) flour, decoction of cotton (Karpasi) seed, cattle dung, soaked black gram, mixture of several plants in ghee, honey, raw sugar, liquor, barley, bidanga

139 etc. the author Chakrapani has considered some of the recipes good for nourishment of trees and directs people to deduce the procedures whichever are beneficial for trees in their respective localities. Chapter-VIII of the text deals with different types of plant disorders and their treatment in 79 verses. Chakrapani follows Surapal in classifying internal disordered based on the imbalanced of humors such as vata, pitta and kafa. The symptoms of the three humors are described by Surapala, Sarangadhara, Chakrapani are similar with minor difference. The text of Chakrapani also states that external disorders like indigestion caused by excessive watering and excessive intake of food, over medication, internal and external insects disturbance, pruned trees, frost burn, fire born, burn by lightening, wrecked by wind, afflicted by friction with nearby trees, transplantation, trees ruined by animals, contact with in healthy trees, discharged of fluids, lapse in the mode of treatment etc. the author has also prescribed their preventive measures of each types of disorders after proper experiment of the materials applied to the trees and yield results. He suggests the experts to recommend medication after using their own discrimination. In this work he has paid attention not only to the theory but also to the practice of the science of plant life.

140 The chapter-IX of the text deals with Botanical wonders contains 52 verses, about wonders of seeds, wonders of trees, wonders with negative result, wonders of flowers, producing seedless fruits, naturally colored cotton, improving quality of produce etc. Enlarging fruit size and without seeds, by soaking seeds if kushmanda, vartaka, patolaka in hogs marrow and when shown and properly watered it gives the result (Ch-IX-6). Take a seed of caster and drench it with hog's blood and marrow, put it in ankola oil for a day, when shown it produces karavella creeper (Ch-IX-3). Enlarging vartaka fruit size by treating its seed with honey and ghee, insert this seed in a growing kusmandaka and close the hole. Apply cow-dung. Extract the seed when the kusmanda is ripe. When the seed sown it produces larger size (Ch-IX-7). Trees producing mango fruits more than once in a year by soaking its seeds in the blood of rabbit and tortoise for 21 days and then planting them with the sprinkling of milk and water (IX-9). Prepare a mixture of bidanga, yasti, honey, sugar water and milk. Sprinkling it on a tree initially yielding sour fruits. It is a wonder that the very same tree bears big sized sweet fruits (V-IX- 17).

141 White color cotton changes into sky blue, green, yellow and red by special treatment (V-IX-42-45). Producing a dwarf tree by cutting its trunk and then treating with kunapa, salt, honey, ghee, milk and iron nail (IX-20). Special process for keeping ripened fruits on trees longer is a great wonder (IX-21, 22). Similarly producing scented juice from sugar cane by sprinkling the plant with ushira, musta or ambu for a period of 20 days (IX-48, 49). A review of the subject matter of Vishvavallabha in the light of the subjects taken from VrksA of Surapala suggests that both the work corresponds to the same strategy: Plant life. The aspects of plant science are thus observed to be present both in terms of similarity of verses as well as in its senses starting from the IIIrd chapter of Vishvavallabha , examination and suitability of Ground (Soil and Propagation) onwards. However, a comparative study of the verses so observed is detailed as under. The IIIrd chapter of Vishvavallabha v. 1-3 correlates with that of the IIIrd chapter of Vrikshayurveda about ascertaining of soil (Bhuminirupana adhyaya) which speaks about the types and colour (varna) and on their tests (rasa) as well. Further, elaborating in details his writing he describes the various colours and tests on the basis of which

142 such discrimination has got accounted (v. 35, 36). The verses from Vishvavallabha like no. 8, 9 and 10 which correlates with the verse no. 41, 42 and 43 of Vrikshayurveda in a way details about plants to be grown on the moist (anupa) land, dry (jangala) land and moderate (sadharana) land. Verses 22, 23 mentions about the trees not to be planted in the garden or in the vicinity of the house, it correlates to that of verse 31, 30 of Vrikshayurveda chapter II, Subhasubha laksanam addhyaya (chapter on auspicious and inauspicious signs of trees planted near the abode) as to its original form. Verse 25 correlates with verse 24 of Vrikshayurveda which reads the auspicious signs of planting vata, udumbara, pippala and plaksa. Verse 27 which relate to verse 33 of Vrikshayurveda, laids the auspiciousness of planting gardens to the north of a city. The verse no. 14, 15 of the text has got its similarity with verse no. 48 of Surapala's text in its IVth chapter dealing with the method of propagation of seeds (Bijaotpatti viddhi). Verse 16 with verse no.49 and verse numbering from 17 to 19 are thus having similarity with verse no. 50, 51 of Vrikshayurveda which speaks about plants growing both from seeds, scions and bulbous roots. Chapter IV, verse no. 1, 2, 3 of Vishvavallabha which recommends the appropriate seasonal time for the growth of trees it connects with

143 Vrikshayurveda's chapter IV, Bijotpatti Vidhi addhyaya (the method of propagating seeds) verse 61, 62 that speaks about the agreeable time for plantation of trees. Again, verses 8, 10, 11 of Chakrapani's text describes the procedures through which the soil of a garden be made suitable. The relevance of the same is found with verses 63, 67, 68 of Surapala's vapanavidhi addhyaya (the method of plantation). Chapter V, verse 5 which narrates the watering of plants in accordance to the seasonal days, it corresponds with chapter VII of Vrikshayurveda, Posana addhyaya (chapter on nourishment), wherein watering is described as per the type of soil. Chapter VI, verse 3, 4 which says the remedies for protecting trees from the destruction of rats, ants, insects, moths, etc. has got its synchronization with that of verse 163, 162 of Vrikshayurveda, chapter VII, Posana addhyaya (chapter on nourishment). Vishvavallabha , however suggests some general ways of nourishing and growing of each crop. For nourishment of trees, it suggests a liquid called kunapa. "Fat, marrow, skin, blood along with the marrow secretion of ram, sheep, deer, and fish and so on should be mixed with water and cooked on fire. When properly cooked, milk and cold water should be added. Oilcake of sesame, honey, and

144 clarified butter should be added to the mixture and the pot should be removed from fire to be kept in a warm place for a fortnight. This liquid called kunapa is nourishing for trees" (Vishvavallabha , Ch. VII, v. '2' & '3'). It further adds that "sprinkling with kunapa is always beneficial to all the trees for increasing the produce of fruits and flowers." Vrikshayurveda by Surapala has got a specific chapter (VI) on the water of carass (kunapa jalam adhyayam). This chapter reflects on the preparation of the manure water (organic) kunapa in its verses from 101 - 106. However, apart from the general recommendation of kunapa, Vishvavallabha also makes specific suggestions for each plant. It also suggests treatment of the diseases of the plant. Chapter VIII, verse 1 which reads out about the acquired disorders of plants due to various types of decoctions, has got its relevance from verse 167, 169, 171 of Vrikshayurveda's chapter VIII, Roganirupana addhyaya (chapter on the knowledge of plant diseases). Verse 4 which describes about the breaking of branches of the trees, that affected by age, hurt due to rubbing of branches, etc. is that same found with verse 174 of Vrikshayurveda, chapter VIII. Verse 9 has got its similarity with verse 168 of Vrikshayurveda, chapter VIII, which speaks for the tree being affected by wind disorder either bears no or a very few flowers and fruits.

145 Verse 17, of chapter VIII gives the ingredients for the cure of bile related imbalance within the plants. It relates to verse 199, Vrikshayurveda, chapter IX Chikitsa addyaya (chapter on treatment of plant diseases). Verse 51, 52 reads out for the treatment measures to be taken for the growth of new leaves and sprouts when a tree is burnt by fire. It thus relates to verse 204, 205 of Vrikshayurveda, chapter IX. Verse 53 which suggests the treatment measures to be taken for the growth of new sprouts in no time if a trees struck is burnt by lightening, has got its similarity with verse 206 of Vrikshayurveda, chapter IX. Many of these prescriptions are certainly worth trying. The Vrksayurveda of 'Surapal' 'Upavanvinoda' of Sarangadhara and 'Vishavallabha' of Chakrapani offers us a wealth of prescriptions that could be researched further with the techniques available to us today. A small group of prescriptions could be a thesis topic for research students of agriculture. In the text the author has allowed freedom and flexibility to planters by advising them to learn from their own observation, to use their own reason and discretion as per changes in place and time, to consult local people and take the help of books. He suggests a path of future development of an applied science like

146 Vrikshayurveda But in the text he nowhere directs people or advice people to take plantation of trees by elaborating on the merits of trees and usefulness of trees to mankind. No portion of the text specially devoted to glorification of trees as we find in Vrikshayurveda of Surapala. From a comparative analysis of various texts on Vrikshayurveda it becomes evident that except only some of the verses all most all the texts have the common verses as their text. So it is significant to point out that the core text Vrikshayurveda was handed down from generation to generation and the texts were so, popular and widely accepted that many compendiums were developed on the more or less same texts except same specific verses.
