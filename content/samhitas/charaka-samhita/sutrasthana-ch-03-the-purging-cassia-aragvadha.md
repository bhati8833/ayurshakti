# Chapter 3 - The Purging Cassia (aragvadha)

## TL;DR — Executive Clinical Summary

**Sutrasthana Chapter 3** is a pharmacology chapter devoted to *aragvadha* (the purging cassia, *Cassia fistula*) and a companion group of herbs used mainly as **external applications**. The chapter presents thirty-two classic powders and unguents for dermatological conditions — obstinate skin diseases, leprosy-like lesions, ringworm, pruritus, keloids, papules and scrofula — as well as specific applications for **vata disorders**, rheumatic conditions, headache, pleurodynia, body odour and toxicosis. The dominant therapeutic idea is *samshamana chikitsa* through the skin: medicated pastes (*lepa*), dusting powders (*udvartana*) and unguents (*anjana*-style salves) prepared in ghee, oil, buttermilk or cow's urine, matched to the condition and the patient's humoral state.

---

## Introduction

After establishing the general principles of medicine in the first two chapters, the Sages — led by **Atreya Punarvasu** and compiled by **Agnivesha** — turn to a concrete therapeutic problem: the treatment of skin morbidity. The chapter is built around *aragvadha*, the purging cassia, a tree whose fruit, bark, leaves and flowers have a long and continuing use in South Asian medicine. Modern research continues to examine *Cassia fistula* extracts for antimicrobial and therapeutic applications.

The genius of this chapter lies not in a single "wonder drug" but in a **systematic method**: the same few dozen herbs — turmeric, Indian berberry, costus, embelia, neem, Indian oleander, sweet flag and others — are combined and recombined into pastes, powders, unguents and washes, each formulation aimed at a particular clinical picture. Reading it with a herb-dictionary at hand teaches more about classical pharmacy than any single recipe.

## The Fifteen Applications in Dermatosis

> Verses 3–17. Fifteen external preparations for the major group of skin diseases (*kushta*).

The opening series is the heart of the chapter. In **verses 3–5**, six groups of drugs are listed in distichs: purging cassia with fetid cassia, Indian beech, vasaka, giloy, emetic nut, turmeric and Indian berberry; then pine-resin, deodar, catechu, crane tree, neem, embelia and Indian oleander bark; the nodes of birch, garlic shirish, green vitriol, gum guggul and drumstick; sweet marjoram, kurchi, dita bark, Indian toothbrush, costus and sprouts of Spanish jasmine; sweet flag, fragrant piper, turpeth, red physic nut, marking nut, red ochre and black antimony; and red and yellow arsenics, kitchen soot, cardamom, green vitriol, lodh, arjuna, nut grass and sarja sal.

**Verse 6** gives the method: each group is soaked in ox-bile, pounded, and prescribed as an external application mixed with rape-seed oil. **Verse 7** states the indication — these quickly relieve obstinate skin diseases, recent leprous lesions, alopecia, keloids, ringworm, fistula-in-ano, piles, scrofula and papules.

**Verses 8–9** prescribe an equal-part powder of costus, turmeric, Indian berberry, holy basil, wild snake gourd, neem, winter cherry, deodar, drumstick, rape-seed, Indian toothache, coriander, rush nut and angelica, rubbed into a paste with buttermilk and applied over an oiled body — the classic *lepa* method — giving relief in pruritus, pimples, wheals, dermatosis and edema.

**Verses 10–11** turn to dusting powders: costus, blue vitriol, Indian berberry, green vitriol, kamala, nut grass, lodh, sulphur, yellow resin, embelia, the arsenics and oleander bark, pulverised and dusted over oiled parts to relieve ringworm, pruritus, keloids, papules and bullous eruptions. **Verses 12–13** continue with simpler pairs: the arsenics with black pepper, til oil and milk of mudar; and an unguent of Indian berberry extract and fetid-cassia seeds with wood-apple juice.

**Verses 14–16** give two celebrated formulations: an unguent of turmeric, Indian berberry, kurchi and beech seeds, Spanish jasmine sprouts and oleander bark used with til alkali; and a compound of red arsenic, kurchi bark, costus, green vitriol, fetid cassia, Indian beech, birch nodes and oleander root — one *tola* of each — boiled in 256 *tolas* of palash-root juice to a semi-solid consistency, declared most beneficial for eradicating skin diseases. **Verse 17** closes the series with simple leaf applications — purging cassia, black nightshade or Indian oleander with buttermilk — rubbed over oiled lesions.

## The Five Applications in Vata

> Verses 18–20. Five preparations for conditions of the *vata* humour.

**Verse 18** opens with a warming application: Indian jujube, horse gram, deodar, Indian groundsel, black gram, linseed, oil-seeds, costus, sweet flag, dill and barley flour, acidified and warmed — the classic *upalepa* for vata affliction. **Verse 19** extends the principle to the flesh of wetland animals and fishes, and to decoctions of roots and the fragrant group of drugs prepared in the tetrad of unctuous substances. **Verse 20** adds a stomachic variant: barley powder and alkali with buttermilk, given warm for stomach pain; and costus, dill, sweet flag and barley flour mixed with oil and acid articles.

## The Five in Rheumatic Conditions

> Verses 21–22. Five preparations for rheumatic complaints (*amavata*-type pain).

**Verse 21** lists dill, fennel, liquorice, mohwah, heart-leaved sida, buchanan's mango, luffa, ghee, white yam and sugar candy. **Verse 22** gives an ointment of Indian groundsel, giloy, liquorice, heart-leaved sida, country mallow, jivaka, rishabhaka, milk and ghee, mixed with bees' wax — a soft, emollient application for rheumatic pain.

## The Two in Cephalalgia

> Verses 23–24. Two applications for headache (*shirashula*).

**Verse 23** first notes that wheat flour with goat's milk and ghee also relieves rheumatic conditions, then gives an unguent of Indian valerian, blue water lily, sandalwood and costus prepared in ghee for headache. **Verse 24** widens the formula: root-stocks of white lotus, deodar, costus, liquorice, cardamom, sacred lotus, blue water lily, eagle-wood, elephant grass, Himalayan cherry and angelica, prepared in ghee.

## The Application in Pleurodynia

> Verse 25. One application for side-stitch and chest pain.

An unguent of Indian groundsel, turmeric, Indian berberry, nardus, dill, fennel, deodar, sugar candy and swallow-wort root, prepared in ghee and oil and applied lukewarm, relieves pleurodynia.

## The Two Refrigerant Applications

> Verses 26–27. Two cooling applications for burning conditions.

**Verse 26**: moss, lotus, blue water lily, country willow, fragrant peony, tubers of white lotus, cuscus grass, lodh, perfumed cherry, yellow sandalwood and sandalwood, prepared in ghee, are refrigerant. **Verse 27**: a salve of sugar, madder, country willow, Himalayan cherry, liquorice, aindri, nalin lotus, scutch grass, camel-thorn root, sacrificial grass, thatch grass, fragrant sticky mallow and elephant grass.

## One Application in the Algid Condition

> Verse 28. One application for coldness and rigour.

Lichen, cardamom, eagle-wood, costus, angelica, Indian valerian, cinnamon, deodar and Indian groundsel, applied externally, relieve the condition of algidity.

## One in Toxicosis

> Verse 28b (unnumbered in the text). One application against poisoning.

The application of shiris (*shirisha*, the lebbeck tree) with chaste tree counteracts the effects of toxicosis.

## One in Dermic Morbidity and One in Body Fetor

> Verse 29. Closing applications for skin morbidity, excessive sweating and body odour.

Rubbing the body with the powder of shiris, geranium grass, fragrant peony and lodh cures skin morbidity and hyperhidrosis; a salve of cinnamon leaves, fragrant sticky mallow, lodh, cuscus grass and sandalwood removes body fetor.

## Summary

**Verse 30** is the recapitulatory verse: in this chapter, the son of Atri, revered by the great sages, has described for the good of the world the **thirty-two most efficacious powders and unguents** that dispel manifold diseases. The colophon confirms the chapter's place: in the Section on General Principles, in the treatise compiled by Agnivesha and revised by Charaka, the third chapter, "The Purging Cassia (Aragvadha)", is completed.

## Key Points for Study

- **Method over materia medica**: the same core herbs recur across all thirty-two preparations — the art lies in the vehicle (ghee, oil, buttermilk, cow's urine, ox-bile) and the combination, each chosen for a specific clinical picture.
- **Dosha logic**: [vata](/glossary) disorders receive warm, unctuous, acidified applications; burning and inflammatory conditions receive refrigerant salves; skin morbidity is treated with dusting powders and pastes that are drying and antimicrobial in character.
- **Skin as a route of therapy**: the whole chapter is an early, systematic argument for transdermal therapy — the body is oiled first, then the medicament is applied, a protocol that survives in modern Ayurvedic *lepa* and *udvartana* practice ([dosha](/glossary), [kushta](/glossary)).
- **Conservation of resource**: most herbs are locally available in the Indian subcontinent — neem, deodar, drumstick, turmeric, Indian berberry — reflecting a deliberately sustainable pharmacy.

Modern phytochemical research continues to examine *Cassia fistula* flower extracts for bio-therapeutic applications — for example, in the development of plant-derived nanomaterials (Arland & Kumar, 2026, [PubMed 42227486](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42227486/)) — lending contemporary interest to this two-thousand-year-old chapter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is aragvadha?

Aragvadha is the purging cassia, *Cassia fistula*, a leguminous tree native to South Asia, known in English as the Indian laburnum or golden shower. In Ayurvedic pharmacy its fruit pulp is valued as a mild laxative, while the leaves, bark and flowers are used in external applications — the subject of this chapter.

### What diseases are treated in this chapter?

The chapter is primarily dermatological: obstinate skin diseases, leprous-like lesions, ringworm, pruritus, keloids, papules, alopecia, scrofula and body odour. It also includes applications for vata disorders, rheumatic conditions, headache, pleurodynia, algidity and toxicosis.

### How are the preparations applied?

In three ways: *lepa* (medicated paste rubbed over an oiled body), dusting powders applied after oiling, and unguents or salves prepared in ghee, oil or wax. The body is routinely oiled first, and the vehicle — buttermilk, cow's urine, ox-bile or plant juices — is chosen deliberately for each formulation.

### Can these preparations be used today?

These are classical formulations and should only be prepared and used under the supervision of a qualified Ayurvedic practitioner. Self-preparation from plant names alone is not advisable, as several ingredients (including the arsenics and marking nut) require expert handling, purification and dosage control.
