# Chapter 16 - The Fully Equipped Physician (Cikitsa-prabhrita)

## TL;DR — Executive Clinical Summary

**The Fully Equipped Physician (Cikitsa-prabhrita)** — Sutrasthana Chapter 16 completes the tetrad on pharmaceutical equipment (Chapters 13-16) and then rises from technique to philosophy. It opens with the clinical signs of successful, unsuccessful and excessive purgation and emesis, lists the conditions that indicate purification, and explains why purificatory therapy must precede sedative therapy — the root of the morbid humors must be destroyed, else disease sprouts again like shoots from a tree root. The chapter then turns to its philosophical heart: body-elements become discordant from the discordance of causes and concordant from concordance; cessation of existence is in the nature of things and needs no cause. Against this background, Agnivesha asks the question every student asks — *if cessation is natural, what is there for the physician to do?* — and Atreya answers: treatment is the operation that gives rise to the concordance of body-elements, and its object is that no discordance arises and concordance continues. The physician who effects this concordance becomes the giver of bodily happiness and long life, and thereby the donor of righteousness, wealth and the satisfaction of desires.

## Introduction

Chapter 15 closed with the armamentarium and the conduct of purification. Chapter 16 closes the pharmaceutical tetrad — oleation, sudation, emesis and purgation — with a verdict on *who* may administer them and *why* they work at all. The first half is pure clinical semiology: the signs by which the physician reads the outcome of purgation and emesis. The second half is pure philosophy: the concordance theory of health, the nature of cessation, and the definition of treatment itself. Charaka deliberately places the metaphysics at the end of the operational manual — the physician who knows only the procedures and not their purpose is only half a physician.

## The Merits of Purification Administered by an Expert

3. When a fully equipped physician, gifted, learned and skilled in practice, administers the procedure of purification to a person, that person, obtaining the right treatment, acquires good health thereby.

## The Evils of the Same Administered by a Quack

4. When a pretentious and ignorant physician administers the procedure of purification to a man, that man, getting either under-treatment or over-treatment, falls a victim to disease.

The two verses are deliberately parallel: the same procedure, in the hands of the skilled physician, confers health; in the hands of the pretentious quack, it becomes the cause of disease. Purification is powerful medicine — powerful in both directions.

## The Signs of Successful Purgation

5-6. Weakness, lightness, depression, attenuation of the disease, promotion of taste, clarification, purification of the stomach and complexion, feeling of hunger and thirst, regularity of natural urges, clearness of understanding, sense-organs and of mind, regular peristalsis and restoration of body-temperature are the signs of successful purgation.

## The Signs of Unsuccessful Purgation

7. Salivation, impurity of the stomach, precipitation of Kapha and Pitta, distension of the abdomen, anorexia, vomiting, absence of weakness and lightness,

8. flabbiness of the calf-muscle and the thigh, torpor, stiffness, onset of rhinitis and impediment to the function of Vata are the signs of unsuccessful purgation.

## The Signs of Excessive Purgation

9-10. The signs of over-purgation are — after the discharge of feces, bile, mucus and flatus successively, an excessive amount of blood of the appearance of fat and of flesh-washed water or fluid devoid of any mucus or bile flows out, or dark blood comes out, or the person gets afflicted with Vata or suffers from thirst and faints.

## The Signs of Excessive Emesis

11. The very same symptoms appear in the case of over-action of emesis too. In addition, there appear disorders of Vata affecting the supraclavicular parts of the body and impediment of speech.

12. A man, therefore, should resort to the shelter of one who is a fully equipped physician, who will confer on his client long life and happiness.

The three-fold reading of outcomes — success, failure, excess — is the same semiological discipline taught for emesis in Chapter 15, now extended to purgation. The signs are objective and observable: hunger and thirst, regular urges, peristalsis and restored body-temperature on the one side; salivation, distension, torpor and stiffness on the other; blood, thirst and fainting at the extreme.

## Conditions in Which the Purification Therapy Is Indicated

13-16. Indigestion, anorexia, corpulence, anemia, heaviness, exhaustion, pimples, wheals, pruritus, malaise, indolence, fatigue, debility, fetor, flabbiness, precipitation of Kapha and Pitta, insomnia, hypersomnia, torpor, impotency, dullness of understanding, evil dreams, and loss of vigor and color in spite of impletion through roborant therapy, are the signs of excessive morbidity. In such conditions are indicated the purificatory procedures of emesis and purgation, in accordance with the morbidity of the humors and vitality of the patient.

17. When a person is thus purified, his body-heat increases, his disorders disappear and his normal health returns.

18. His senses, mind, understanding and complexion become clear and he acquires vigor, plumpness, offspring and virility.

19. His aging is retarded and he lives a long life free of disease. Therefore, a person should take purificatory procedures at the proper time, skillfully administered.

The indications list is the clinical picture of *sama* (accumulated) morbidity: heaviness, torpor, dullness, fetor, flabbiness, evil dreams, and the telling item — loss of vigor and color *in spite of* roborant therapy. When building-up fails, the blockage must first be removed. Note also the outcome claims of proper purification: clarity of senses, mind and complexion, vigor, offspring, virility, and retarded aging.

## Reasons for Purification to Precede the Sedative Therapy

20. The morbid humors subdued by lightening therapy and digestives may sometimes be again provoked; but in the case of those which are subdued by purificatory procedures, there is no possibility of such recurrence.

21. When the root of the morbid humors, like that of trees, is not destroyed, there certainly occurs the reappearance of disease, like that of the sprouts from plant roots.

This is the classical argument for samshodhana over samshamana: palliation (lightening therapy, digestives) suppresses the humors, but suppression can relapse; elimination destroys the root. The tree-root metaphor is among the most cited images in Ayurvedic therapeutics — treat the root, not the sprout.

## Regimen of Diet After Purification

22-23. To those weakened by purificatory medication, roboration is to be done by wholesome food, viz., by a diet including ghee, meat-juice, milk and palatable soups, as well as by inunction, unctuous massage, bath and evacuative and unctuous enemata. In this way, he gets endowed with happiness and long life.

## Treatment of the Overdone and Underdone Conditions of Purificatory Therapy

24. To those suffering from the effects of overdone purificatory procedure, a potion of ghee is recommended, or unctuous enemata with the oil prepared with drugs of the sweet group.

25. But one who is under-purged should be oleated and purged again, with due regard to dose, season and strength of the patient, with the deficiencies of the previous procedure kept in view.

26. The treatment of the complications that arise from the wrong administration of oleation, sudation, purification and rehabilitation procedures will be described in the section on "Success in Treatment."

## The Cessation of Life Is the Nature of Things

27. The body-elements become discordant as a result of the discordance of the causative factors. Conversely, they become concordant following upon the concordance of the causative factors. Cessation of existence, however, is always in the course of nature.

28. As regards their rise, things stand in need of a cause, but no cause is needed for their cessation. Even in the latter case, some however hold the very non-continuance of their causative factors as the cause of their cessation.

With verse 27 the chapter pivots from procedure to principle. Health is *concordance* (samyatva) of the body-elements; disease is *discordance* (vikriti). Both have causes. But the end of the body — its dissolution — is not an effect requiring a cause: it is the nature of the existent to cease. Coming into being needs a cause; going out of being needs nothing. The verse quietly concedes that other thinkers disagree, holding non-continuance of causes as the cause of cessation — a rare glimpse of live philosophical debate inside the text.

## Agnivesha's Query Regarding the Purpose of Treatment

29. The master having thus spoken, Agnivesha said: "If cessation be in the very nature of things, what is there then to be done by the fully equipped physician?

30. What discordant body-elements does a physician harmonise by medicine? What is treatment and for what purpose is it given?"

## Atreya's Answer

31. Hearing these words, Punarvasu said: "Hear, O good one! what reason the great sages have seen in this respect.

32-32½. The cause of things ceasing to be is not known, for the simple reason that no such cause exists, as in the case of the passage of time. Being evanescent by nature, things cease to be, even as they are born.

33. Thus, there is no question of a cause for a thing ceasing to be, nor indeed of modifying this innate tendency to extinction."

The argument is a miniature of Vaisheshika-style causation: the rise of a pot needs a potter, but its fall needs no agent — it falls by its own evanescence, like time passing. If death were a caused disease, the physician could treat it; but it is uncaused, innate, and therefore *beyond the physician's object*. This clears the ground for the definition of treatment that follows.

## The Function of Treatment and of the Physician

34. The treatment of disorders, therefore, consists of such operations as give rise to the concordance of body-elements. This is regarded as the function of the physician.

## The Object of Treatment

35. Treatment is given with the object of ensuring that no discordance arises in the various body-elements and that there is continuance of their concordance.

36. By avoiding things that give rise to discordance and practising those that help concordance, the discordant elements do not persist and the concordant elements are continually brought into existence.

## The Merits of the Gifted Physician

37. Because he effects the concordance of the body-elements by using the concordant means, the expert in treatment becomes the giver of bodily happiness and longevity.

38. By the gift of bodily happiness and long life, the physician becomes the donor of righteousness, wealth and satisfaction of desires pertaining to both the worlds of man.

The definition is precise and bounded: treatment *is* the operation that brings about concordance; its object is the continuance of concordance against discordance. The physician does not fight death — death is uncaused and unmodifiable. The physician fights *discordance*, which is caused, and therefore curable. On this precise boundary rests the whole dignity of medicine: because the physician gives bodily happiness and long life, he is said to give righteousness, wealth and the satisfaction of desires — the three pursuits of man expounded in Chapter 11 — in both this world and the next.

## Summary

39. The benefits that accrue from the fully equipped physician as against the harm done by the quack; the signs of properly done, under-done and overdone purificatory procedures;

40. the signs of excessive morbidity; the advantages of purification; the general principles of treatment, basic principles of therapeutic procedures and successful methods in complicated conditions;

41. the rationale of therapeutics and the object with which a physician administers it — all this has been described by the sage in this chapter on "The Fully Equipped Physician."

Thus the tetrad of chapters dealing with the pharmaceutical equipment — oleation, sudation, emesis and purgation — is completed.

## Key Points for Study

- **The same procedure, two outcomes**: purification administered by the fully equipped physician confers health; administered by the quack, under- or over-treated, it causes disease.
- **The signs of purgation**: success — weakness, lightness, hunger, thirst, regular urges, clear senses and restored body-temperature; failure — salivation, distension, anorexia, torpor, stiffness; excess — blood, thirst, fainting, vata affliction.
- **The indications for purification**: indigestion, anorexia, corpulence, anemia, heaviness, pruritus, fetor, flabbiness, insomnia, hypersomnia, dullness of understanding, evil dreams, and failure of roborant therapy — the picture of excessive morbidity.
- **Elimination over palliation**: humors subdued by lightening therapy and digestives can be provoked again; those eliminated by purification cannot — the root of the tree must be destroyed.
- **After purification**: roboration by ghee, meat-juice, milk, soups, inunction, massage, bath and enemata; over-purification is answered by ghee and sweet-group unctuous enemata; under-purification by repeated oleation and purgation with dose, season and strength in view.
- **The philosophy**: discordance of body-elements arises from discordance of causes, concordance from concordance of causes — but cessation of existence is in the nature of things, needing no cause.
- **The definition of treatment**: the operation that gives rise to the concordance of body-elements; its object is that no discordance arises and concordance continues. On this foundation, the physician becomes the giver of happiness, long life, righteousness, wealth and the satisfaction of desires.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the signs of successful purgation?

Weakness, lightness, depression, attenuation of the disease, promotion of taste, clarification and purification of the stomach and complexion, hunger and thirst, regularity of natural urges, clearness of understanding, sense-organs and mind, regular peristalsis and restoration of body-temperature.

### What are the signs of unsuccessful and excessive purgation?

Unsuccessful purgation shows salivation, impurity of the stomach, precipitation of Kapha and Pitta, distension of the abdomen, anorexia, vomiting, absence of weakness and lightness, flabbiness of the calf-muscle and thigh, torpor, stiffness, rhinitis and impediment to the function of Vata. Excessive purgation shows, after the discharge of feces, bile, mucus and flatus, an excessive outflow of blood, or dark blood, or affliction with Vata, thirst and fainting.

### Why must purification precede sedative therapy?

Because humors subdued by lightening therapy and digestives can be provoked again, while humors removed by purificatory procedures do not recur. As Charaka puts it, when the root of the morbid humors is not destroyed, disease reappears like sprouts from a plant root — treatment must destroy the root, not merely suppress the symptom.

### In which conditions is purification indicated?

Indigestion, anorexia, corpulence, anemia, heaviness, exhaustion, pimples, wheals, pruritus, malaise, indolence, fatigue, debility, fetor, flabbiness, precipitation of Kapha and Pitta, insomnia, hypersomnia, torpor, impotency, dullness of understanding, evil dreams, and loss of vigor and color despite roborant therapy — the signs of excessive morbidity.

### What is the object of treatment according to this chapter?

Treatment consists of operations that give rise to the concordance of body-elements, given with the object that no discordance arises and concordance continues. Since cessation of existence is in the nature of things and needs no cause, the physician does not fight death but fights discordance, which is caused and curable. By effecting concordance, the physician becomes the giver of bodily happiness and longevity, and thereby the donor of righteousness, wealth and the satisfaction of desires.
