# Chapter 12 - The Salutary and the Unsalutary Influences of Vata

## TL;DR — Executive Clinical Summary

**The Salutary and Unsalutary Influences of Vata** — Sutrasthana Chapter 12 of the Charaka Samhita records a full assembly of sages debating the nature of vata: its six characteristics (dryness, lightness, coldness, hardness, roughness and clearness), the factors that excite and allay it, and its functions both within the body and throughout the universe. Varyovida's great eulogy describes vata as the upholder of structure and function — the impeller of movement, the stimulator of gastric fire, the eliminator of excreta and the source of all animation. When provoked, the same vata impairs strength, complexion, senses and the fetus, and obstructs the vital functions. The chapter closes with Atreya's decisive synthesis: all three doshas — vata, pitta and kapha — in their normal condition together make a man whole; no single dosha may claim exclusive credit for health.

## Introduction

The previous chapter dealt with the pursuits of man and the causes of disease. Chapter 12 begins the exposition of the three doshas themselves, starting with vata — the principle of movement, the subtlest and most pervasive of the three. The text takes the form of a symposium: sages of different schools assemble and speak in turn, each adding one element to the doctrine, until Atreya himself delivers the concluding synthesis. The structure mirrors the subject — vata is assembled from many voices, just as it marshals many functions.

The chapter is clinically foundational because it states, in one place, the complete physiology of vata: its qualities, its aggravators, its pacifiers, its normal functions and its morbid actions. Practically every vata disorder described later in the Samhita — from sciatica to insomnia, from constipation to tremors — is a consequence of the principles laid down here.

## The Assembly of the Sages

3-(1). Desirous of knowing one another's opinion concerning the salutary and unsalutary aspect of vata, the great sages, having assembled together, discussed among themselves thus—

3. "Of what character is vata? What is its provoking factor? What again are its allaying agents? How do the exciting or allaying factors, unable as they are to come into contact with vata, which is incorporeal and unstable, succeed either in exciting or allaying it? What again are the doings of vata moving within bodies and outside bodies, both when excited and when quiescent, both within the body and when at large in the universe?"

The questions of the sages define the agenda of the chapter — and, in effect, the agenda of dosha theory itself: character (qualities), provocation, pacification, the mechanism of action, and the distinction between normal and abnormal function.

## The Six Characteristics of Vata

4. Then spoke Kusha, the son of Sankritya: "The characteristics of vata are six, viz., dryness, lightness, coldness, hardness, roughness and clearness."

The six gunas of vata — ruksha (dry), laghu (light), shita (cold), khara (hard/rough), parusha (rough) and vishada (clear/non-slimy) — are the master-key to vata pathology. Whatever possesses these qualities increases vata; whatever possesses their opposites decreases it. This single verse, applied consistently, generates the entire vata diet and regimen.

### The Exciting Factors of Vata

5-(1). Hearing this statement, Bharadvaja, known as Kumarashira, said: "It is even as your honour has said; such, to be sure, are the characteristics of vata.

5. It is by the repeated use of such-like qualities, such-like substances and actions of such-like potencies that vata becomes excited. For, verily, the increasing factor of the body-elements is the repeated use of homologatory things."

### The Sedating Factors of Vata

6-(1). Hearing this observation, Kankayana, the physician from the Bahlika country, said: "It is even as your honour has observed. Such, indeed, are the exciting factors of vata.

6. The opposite of these are the allaying agents of vata. For, surely, the alleviative factors in relation to the body-elements are just the opposite of the exciting factors."

## The Working Process of These Factors

7-(1). Hearing this remark, Badisha Dhamargava said: "It is, indeed, as your honour has declared. Such, of a truth, are the exciting and allaying agents of vata.

7-(2). As regards how these provocative and sedative agents, while unable to come into contact with vata, which is incorporeal and unstable, succeed in provoking or allaying it, we shall now explain.

7-(3). The exciting agents of vata, in human bodies, are of course those that induce dryness, lightness, coldness, hardness and porousness.

7-(4). Vata, having effected lodgement in bodies of such description and having gained strength, gets provoked.

7-(5). The sedative agents are, conversely, those that promote unctuousness, heaviness, heat, smoothness, softness, sliminess and compactness.

7. In bodies of such description, the vata, wandering about without obtaining lodgement, drifts into tranquility.

The mechanism is subtle and worth stating carefully: vata itself is incorporeal and unstable — it cannot be "touched" by food or regimen directly. Instead, the factors act on the **body**: qualities homologous to vata give it a lodging place and strength; qualities antagonistic to vata leave it nowhere to settle, and it drifts into tranquility. Modern terms might say that vata pathology is a function of the tissue state, not of the wind itself.

## The Functions of Normal Vata in the Body

8-(1). Having heard this pronouncement of Badisha, conforming to the truth and approved by the assembly of the sages, the royal sage Varyovida said: "It is all, as your honour has propounded, unexceptionable. Now, as regards the doings of vata moving within bodies and outside bodies, both when excited and when not excited, both when confined in the human body and when at large in the universe, we shall, so far as we are able, give a detailed exposition of them in the light of observation, inference and authoritative instruction, having made our obeisance to vata. The vata is the upholder of both structure and function of the body. It is the very self of the five forms of vata in the body, viz., prana, udana, samana, vyana and apana. It is the impeller of upward and downward movements, the controller and conductor of the mind, the inspirer of all the senses, the conveyer of all the sense-stimuli, the marshaller of the body-elements, the synthesizing principle in the body, the impeller of speech, the cause of feeling and audition, the source of the auditory and tactile senses, the origin of all excitement and animation, the stimulator of the gastric fire, the desiccator of the morbid humors, the eliminator of excrement and deobstruent of the gross and subtle body channels, the modeller of the fetal form, the sustaining principle of life — all these are the functions of the normal vata in the body."

This is the classic enumeration of vata's physiology, and it names the five sub-forms — prana, udana, samana, vyana and apana — that later chapters will elaborate. Note how the list spans the whole organism: nervous (impeller, inspirer, conveyer of stimuli), motor (speech, movement), metabolic (gastric fire), eliminative (excrement, channels), embryological (fetal form) and cognitive (mind, senses). Vata is the principle of *everything that moves*.

## The Actions of the Provoked Vata in the Body

8-(2). When, however, the vata becomes abnormal in the body, it afflicts the organism with diverse kinds of disorders, tending to impair its strength, complexion, well-being and life; it depresses the mind, impairs all the senses, destroys the fetus in the uterus, produces deformity in it, or unduly prolongs the period of gestation, gives rise to fear, grief, stupefaction, depression of the spirits and delirium, and obstructs the vital functions.

Every normal function of vata has a morbid mirror: the inspirer of senses becomes their impairer; the sustainer of life becomes the destroyer of the fetus; the impeller of movement becomes the producer of fear, grief and delirium. The paired list is the template for all vata pathology.

## The Actions of Normal Vata in Nature

8-(3). Of the vata which is at large in the universe and is in its normal condition, the following are the works: upholding the earth, setting the fire ablaze, the governance of the procession and orbits of the sun, the moon, the constellations and planetary systems, formation of the clouds, the release of the atmospheric water, the propulsion of the streams, the production of flowers and fruits, the germination of seeds, the variation of seasons, the evolution into different elements, their differentiation in weight and shape, the fertilization of seeds, the growth of crops, and absorption and evaporation of moisture in plants — and all processes of change that are normal.

## The Actions of Abnormal Vata in Nature

8-(4). Of the vata moving about in the universe in an enraged condition, the following are the works: the grinding down of the peaks of the mountains, the up-rooting of trees, the churning up of the oceans, the upsurging of the lakes, the reversing of the courses of the rivers, the quaking of the earth, the inflation of the rain-clouds, the releasing of snow, thunder, dust, sand, fish, frogs, snakes, alkaline substances, blood, stones and lightning; the un-settlement of the six seasons, the retardation of the crops, the visitation of the pestilences, the destruction of all things created, the releasing of the wrath of the clouds, sun, fire and winds, which marks the end of a world cycle of four ages.

The cosmic parallel is deliberate and pedagogical: the same vata that moves the streams and germinates the seed, when enraged, reverses rivers and uproots trees. Microcosm and macrocosm obey the same law — this is the principle of homology (samanya) that underlies Ayurvedic reasoning from nature to the body.

## In Praise of Vata

8. Vata is God, the author and everlasting one, the maker and unmaker of creatures, the dispenser of happiness and misery, the death, the ruler of the underworld, the controller, the lord of creatures, the undivided one, the universal artificer, the omniform, the omnipervading, the disposer of all processes, the subtlest of all things, the omnipresent, the immanent, the pervader of all the worlds; vata alone is God.

## Marichi's Question

9. After hearing this dissertation of Varyovida, Marici observed: "No doubt this is so; but what is the purpose of either retaining or acquiring such information in the course of a medical discussion? For it is in connection with the science of medicine that this discussion has arisen."

## Varyovida's Explanation

10-(1). Said Varyovida: "If the physician is unable to predict the destructive vata, exceedingly strong, exceedingly fierce and exceedingly swift-moving, how can he, withstanding all his vigilance, forestall its sudden outburst, with a view to safeguarding the people from the danger of destruction?

10. Besides, a truthful panegyric of vata, in itself, tends towards freedom from disease, increase of strength and complexion, body-lustre, growth, attainment of knowledge and the blessing of maximum longevity."

Marici's question is the physician's own voice of economy: why study cosmic vata? Varyovida's answer is twofold — first, the physician who cannot foresee the destructive outbreak of vata cannot protect his patients from it; second, a truthful appreciation of vata is itself therapeutic, tending to freedom from disease, strength and longevity. In other words: *understanding the principle of movement is the first therapy of movement disorders*.

## The Effects of Pitta

11-(1). Marici said: "It is Fire alone that, located in the pitta, gives rise to good and evil consequences according as it is in a normal or an abnormal condition.

11. These consequences are digestion and indigestion; vision and loss of vision; the normality and abnormality of temperature; the healthy and the diseased look; intrepidity and fear, anger and delight, confusion and lucidity, and such other pairs of opposite qualities."

## The Effects of Kapha

12-(1). Hearing this statement of Marici, Kapya said: "It is Soma, the water-element alone, located in the kapha of the body, that gives rise to good and evil consequences, according as it is in a normal or in an abnormal condition.

12. These are: compactness and flabbiness, plumpness and emaciation, zest and lassitude, virility and impotence, knowledge and ignorance, understanding and stupefaction, and such other pairs of opposite qualities."

## Atreya's Conclusion — The Doctrine of the Three Doshas

13-(1). Hearing this statement of Kapya, the worshipful Punarvasu, the son of Atri, said: "All of you have spoken rightly except as to your making exclusive claims.

13-(2). To be correct, it is all the three — vata, pitta and kapha — in their normal condition that combine to make a man whole of his senses, possessed of strength, good complexion and ease, and assured of great longevity, even as the triad of dharma, artha and kama, when properly cherished, secure for him the highest good both here and in the other world.

13. The same three, if rendered morbid, bring upon the man great tribulation, just as the three seasons grown abnormal afflict the world with evil at the time of destruction."

14. All the sages approved and applauded the words of the worshipful Atreya.

15. Hearing the award of Atreya, all the sages received and applauded it, just as the gods receive and applaud the words of Indra.

Atreya's verdict is the philosophical crown of the chapter. Each sage had claimed his own element as the whole truth — vata for Varyovida, fire for Marici, water for Kapya. Atreya corrects the exclusivity without discarding the insights: all three doshas, in their normal condition, *together* make a man whole, just as dharma, artha and kama together secure the highest good. The tridosha doctrine is born here as a synthesis of competing one-sided truths — a model of how Ayurveda handles partial claims.

## Key Points for Study

- **The six characteristics of vata**: dryness, lightness, coldness, hardness, roughness and clearness — the master-key to vata diet and regimen.
- **Exciting and allaying factors**: homologous qualities lodge vata and provoke it; antagonistic qualities leave it no lodging and pacify it.
- **The mechanism**: factors act on the body, not on the incorporeal vata directly — tissue state governs vata pathology.
- **Normal vata**: upholder of structure and function, inspirer of the senses, stimulator of gastric fire, eliminator of excreta, sustainer of life; the five sub-forms prana, udana, samana, vyana and apana.
- **Provoked vata**: impairs strength, complexion, senses and the fetus; produces fear, grief, stupefaction and delirium; obstructs vital functions.
- **Atreya's synthesis**: vata, pitta and kapha in normal condition together confer health; morbidity of any of them brings tribulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the six characteristics of vata in Ayurveda?

Vata is characterized by six qualities: dryness (ruksha), lightness (laghu), coldness (shita), hardness (khara), roughness (parusha) and clearness (vishada). Whatever repeatedly introduces these qualities into the body increases vata; whatever introduces their opposites — unctuousness, heaviness, heat, smoothness, softness, sliminess, compactness — pacifies it.

### How does vata get provoked and pacified?

Vata itself is incorporeal and unstable, so exciting and allaying factors act through the body: substances and actions homologous to vata's qualities give it a lodging place where it gains strength and becomes provoked; antagonistic qualities leave it wandering without lodgement, so it drifts into tranquility. This is why vata disorders are treated by changing the tissue state — with warmth, oil, nourishment and routine.

### What are the functions of normal vata in the body?

Normal vata upholds the structure and function of the body as the five sub-forms prana, udana, samana, vyana and apana. It impels upward and downward movements, controls the mind, inspires the senses, conveys sense-stimuli, marshals the body-elements, impels speech, stimulates gastric fire, desiccates morbid humors, eliminates excreta, opens the channels, models the fetal form and sustains life itself.

### What happens when vata is provoked?

Provoked vata impairs strength, complexion, well-being and life; depresses the mind; impairs all the senses; destroys the fetus or produces deformity and prolonged gestation; gives rise to fear, grief, stupefaction, depression and delirium; and obstructs the vital functions. In nature, provoked vata reverses rivers, uproots trees and unsettles the seasons.

### Why does Charaka Samhita praise vata as God?

Varyovida's panegyric identifies vata with the cosmic principle of movement — the maker and unmaker of creatures, the pervader of all worlds. Marici asks why a medical text needs this. The answer: the physician who cannot foresee the destructive, swift-moving outbreak of vata cannot protect patients from it, and a truthful appreciation of vata itself tends to freedom from disease, strength, complexion and long life. Atreya then corrects the exclusive claims of all the sages: vata, pitta and kapha together, in normal condition, make a man whole.
