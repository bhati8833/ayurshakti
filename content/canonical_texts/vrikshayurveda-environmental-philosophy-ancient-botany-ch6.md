3.2. Ethics in the context of Vedic Literatures

The smrtikar's of ancient times have framed rules and code of ethics for those who hurts or causes wound to the trees, for those who cut them unnecessarily for their self benefits etc. The descriptions of certain offences among them which were covered under the grhavastukam adhyaya of vastu such as offences relating to house (grha), irrigation field (kheta), garden (upavana), dam (setubandha), border (sima), and garden laden with flowers and fruits (phala puspa udyana), pools, ponds (sarovara), etc. are entered onto the code of ethics of Arthasastra (38, 3, 9) and Gautama Grhyasutra (2, 3, 34-35; 2, 6, 19). Manusmrti, enjoins that man should protect the movable and immovable components of the environment by taking into consideration their usefulness for human health and welfare. Matsyapurana in its descriptions of the penal consequences as stated in the words of Manu reads that the offender one who cuts a tree being laden with sweet fruits shall be punished with gold as

Adulteration of seeds is a much discussed corruption of the present day causing untold miseries to the farmers. 3.3. Environmentalism and the Appreciation of Nature

I.2.2. Environmental Aesthetics: Environmental aesthetics relates to the theory or philosophy of taste or the science of the beautiful aspect and concerns in nature and art; especially that which treats the expression and embodiment of beauty by art. It applies naturally to the ensuring wave of investigations of the natural bounty that are conducted under the influence of environmental concerns. I.2.2.1. Environmentalism and the Appreciation of Nature: The fundamental part to be accounted in the appreciation of nature is that, whether nature can be appropriately appreciated with the same methods and assumptions with which we appreciate art. 24. Yajnavalkya smrti, 2, 227-228

50 The model of appreciation at the heart of standard art aesthetics is roughly an interpretive judgment of a demarcated object based on a conventionally circumscribed perception of it. The environmental tradition gives rise to a preference for a more active relationship with and within with and within a natural world of interconnected elements. The contemporary positions in environmental aesthetics have developed from different point of views concerning the aesthetics appreciations of the natural environment. These are frequently divided into two fields cognitive and non-cognitive. The cognitive (conceptual or narrative) views in environmental aesthetics are united by the thought that knowledge and information about the nature of the object of appreciation is central to its aesthetics appreciations. Thus, they support the idea that nature must be appreciated. But appreciation of nature requires knowledge of natural science and natural history. The non-cognitive (non-conceptual or ambience) approach only means to arouse emotions. It is often called as the aesthetics of enjoyment, rejects many of the traditional ideas about aesthetics appreciations not only for nature but also for art.

I.2.2.2. Environmental Art: 51 Nature art or the aesthetic formation and decorative arrangement of the natural aspects have obviously been a key factor in general adducing for the increase of growth of environmentalism witness the importance of natural aesthetics. Therefore, within the generic category of art about nature, it can be defined that, the familiar genre of 'nature art' as representations of nature in any art medium - principally, literary and in the form of visual arts subject. that have only nature and not human as their main The art have always been one way to explore the world and our feelings and ideas about it. Environmental art explores our ideas about nature and our changing relations with it. Further, the environmental artworks in the form of various pleasure gardens, orchids, different artistic outlays of natural environment that do adopt environmentally enlightened perspectives can be viewed as that addressing in a unique way which gives answer to the question about how interaction with nature is possible aesthetically while at the same time respecting nature for what it is.

52 I.2.2.3. Aesthetics in the Sanskrit Literature: The ancient Vedic literature is filled with enormous examples depicting the natural beauty being described, ascribed and so also formulated in various prescriptive manners for its artistic creation in way of recreational places of public gathering like pleasure gardens, decorative arrangement of trees, plants, creepers, bushes, etc. nearby to ponds, pools, water reservoirs, hermitage garden, places surrounding an abode adorned with greenery and many more. The classical Sanskrit poet Kalidasa in his dramas has treated the hermitages as the natural habitats where one find the dominion of peace and tranquility. Here violence is prohibited in all its possible forms. Kalidasa is also treated as a perfect environmentalist who has proved himself through his melodious poetic descriptions of ponds, lakes, and gardens. In his poetic descriptions he has also specified for the manner of their maintenance as well. Rtusamhara, one of the poetic masterpieces of Kalidasa narrates the natural aspects of all seasons in such a way that one's eye catches all the blissful sceneries and loveliness spread over in the nature. One may became overwhelmed with

53 passion in the seasons like spring, rain, and summer in awakening the sensuous feelings of human beings. Banabhatta, another eminent Sanskrit poet of the ancient times who is also treated as a widely travelled man, had minutely observed the beauties of nature, the flora and fauna in its various aspects and admired them through his poetic reflection. He keenly felt for the magnetic attraction of nature, its various colours, its sweet, soft and melodious music all drawn him towards it. The poet describes both the soft and the grim side of nature. He has given an elaborate description of forests, hermitages, rivers, trees, birds, animals, morning and evening, dawn and dusk sceneries which give much more importance to ecology and more specifically to environmental settings. I.2.2.4. Aesthetics in the context of Modern Nature: While discussing the various ways of environmental aesthetics, we cannot ignore the beauties of nature. Undoubtedly, natural wilderness has got tremendous aesthetics values. We worship Mother Nature, who is kind, benevolent and generous enough to sustain all life forms on earth. The 'Wild nature' is considered the most beautiful gift of 'mother nature' for the mankind. The vastness of ocean, dense forest, beautiful landscape,

54 high hills and mountains with ever flowing streams, flying clouds, and sweet breeze's carrying the fragrance of flowers, flying clouds, chirping of birds, roaming of animals; amazing snowfalls etc. are the beauties of nature. Nature is kind, beautiful and sweet. Her language is subtle and metaphorical. She always gives signs which can be read by philosophers, literature experts or poets only. In the wild nature we hear the rustling of leaves when the gentle breeze carries the fragrance of forests. The dances of roses, periwinkle and lotus, the twittering of birds, different sounds of insects and wild animals amidst music of flowing springs and streams, running of rabbits in far off bushes, chattering of monkeys in the canopy of forests, sudden jump of a black buck are some of the attractive and thrilling expressions of wild nature. It can be best understood with a poetic and philosophical vision or those who are sensitive and romantic by nature. It may nevertheless be true that the appreciation of natural will not be shared by many people. But, if wilderness can be the source of such deep joy and satisfaction, its destruction would be a great loss. Dense forests do have links with the past, since they are virgin forest. Virgin forests are those forests which are yet intact since the beginning of creation. But, it is hard to say

55 whether we have any virgin forest left till date. In fact, we are living in a post natural world. True wilderness of the earth becomes scarce. Wilderness is valued as something of immense beauty, as a reservoir of scientific knowledge still to be gained and for recreational opportunities. It is a matter of regret that any portion of wilderness is untouched by modern civilization. We simply hope that coming generation will appreciate and conserve wilderness. We can teach to the younger generation that a walk in the forest is more rewarding than playing computer games. So, we should preserve the amount of wilderness that exists now, for coming generation, to see a world that has not been created by human beings. Otherwise, the coming generation will curse us for having deprived them of the beauty of natural forests. True wilderness means the unspoiled part of nature. Environmentalist doubt whether any true wilderness is left on this planet. But, human beings have so far changed the face of this world in such a way that almost every spot on the earth appears to be manmade. Man has destroyed the creator's natural forest to satisfy his lust, greed and self. Now, it is right time to protect the remaining forests throughout the world by creating awareness.
