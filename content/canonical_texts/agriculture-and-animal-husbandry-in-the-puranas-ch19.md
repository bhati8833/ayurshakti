## 79. Part 15 - Management and Treatment of Elephants

Agriculture and Animal husbandry in the Puranas

This page relates ‘Management and Treatment of Elephants’ of the study on agriculture and animal husbandry in the Puranas—a category of Sanskrit literature describing the culture and heritage of ancient Indian society. Besides detailing animal care, the six chapters of this essay also documents various Puranic agricultural science: ploughing, sowing, soil-types/fertility tests, transplantation, manuring, irrigation/water-management, tanks, rainfall–Sun dependence, horticulture, plus crop lists (e.g., Vrihi, Yava, Godhuma) and festivals/rites.

Part 15 - Management and Treatment of Elephants

The Matsya Purāṇa mentions about Hastisikṣā (the science of elephants).[1] Rājaputra or Budha, the son of Tārā, knew well the science of medicines for the elephants.He was the originator of science of elephantalogy according to the Matsya Purāṇa. Hence he was considered Gajavaidyaka.The śāstra in which he was well-versed was known as Rājaputrīya, a work on elephantalogy by Rājaputra. The treatments of the elephants have been thoroughly discussed in this Hastiśāstra.[2] The superintendent of elephants was known as Gajādhyakṣa[3] in the Matsya Purāṇa. It is suggested in the Matsya Purāṇa that one who knows the art of training elephants and is aware of the races of wild beasts, and is accustomed to bear hardships should be made the superintendent of royal elephants.[4] The mahout (elephant driver) of the state elephant was known as Gajārohī.[5] The elephant driver should also be similar who should be of the above qualifications and firm in his seat and clever all around.[6]

The Agni Purāṇa mentions the following diseases and their remedies in case of elephants:

Headache (śirasaḥ śūla): Elephants head should be lubricated with oil when it suffers from headache.[7]

Infection of legs (pādaroga): The parts of legs which get affected should be smeared with oil and then should be washed with a concentrated liquid resulting from boiling the smooth paste of sesamum powder as an antiseptic.[8]

Shivering (vepathu): An elephant suffering from shivering, should be administered the essence of peacock, lava, mixed with pepper and pippalī.[9]

Dysentery (atīsāra): When it suffers from dysentery, a morsel composed of bilva, balā, lodhra, dhātaki and śīta, should be given.[10]

Paralysis (karagraha): In case of paralysis, the animal should use a composition of salt and clarified butter as snuff.[11]

Erect of the ears (utkarṇa): In this disease, compound of māgadhī, nāgara and ājajī mixed with yavāgu should be given.[12]

Roughness of voice (galagraha): In case of roughness of voice, juice of vārāha, daśamūla and kulattha treated with āmla and kākamacī, boiled with oil should be given.[13]

Retention of urine (mūtrabhaṅga): When it suffers from retention of urine, medicated liquid composed of clarified butter saturated with eight sorts of salt or decoction of trapuṣa seeds should be given.[14]

Skin diseases (tvagdoṣa): When it suffers from skin diseases, a refined compound of nimba tree should be given for good result.[15]

Worms in intestines (kṛmikoṣṭha): If in intestines worms are formed, then urine of cow and pulverized viḍanga must be given.[16]

Problems in intestines: In all kind of wasting diseases, liquid consists of extract of śṛṅgavera, kaṇā and drākṣā mixed with sugar and milk would be favourable.Moreover, the juice of meat and food consist of mudga, vyoṣa and tamaruca would also be served. Oil prepared with the concentrate of trivṛḍ, vyoṣa, agni, danta, arka, śyāmā, kṣīra and pippalī would also be a good remedy for all kinds of intestinal problems.[17]

Kaṭa roga: Liquid consists of soup of yaṣṭika, mudga and sarada or a mixture of bāla and bilva would proof good for healing kaṭa roga.

Pain in the abdomen (sarvaśūla): Food composed of viḍanga, indrayava (Holarrhena antidysenrica), hiṅgu, sarala and two varieties of rajanī should be given in the morning if the elephant suffers from any kind of severe pain in the abdomen caused by wind.[18]

In any diseases: Decoction of triphalā, pañcakola, daśamūla, viḍanga, śatāvarī, guḍucī, nimba, vāsaka and kiṃśuka is useful for curing the diseases in elephant.[19]

Jaundice (pāṇḍuroga): An elephant suffering from jaundice, should be treated with clarified butter, prepared with the urine of a cow, and the essence of the two sorts of rajanī.[20]

Emptying the intestines (ānāha): In case there is any difficulty in emptying the intestines, then lubrication with oil and bath of the part with medicated clarified butter would be beneficial.[21]

Sudden loss of consciousness (mūrcchā): In the case of a sudden loss of consciousness, vāruṇī wine thoroughly soaked with the five sorts of salt, as well as small piece of food which is composed of viḍanga, triphalā, vyoṣa and saindhava should be prescribed. In such case, washings of honey should be used for its normal drink.[22]

Feeding and Nourishment:

The food which consists of ṣaṣṭi, vrīhi and śālī rice, is considered the best food for an elephant.[23] The food consists of wheat and barley should be ranked as second best, while the other sorts of food should be considered inferior sort.[24] A course of barley and sugarcane would form the best strength-giving diet for an elephant.[25] A course of dried barley would deranged the seven cardinal principles of its organism.Abundant milk should be served to an elephant for physical strength and good health.Juice of meat mixed with substance with stimulant effects would also be administered for the same purpose.[26] In famine and in war, the essence of the flesh of crows, owls or dogs mixed with honey can be given as food to an elephant.[27]

Preservation of Elephant-Life:

For keeping the elephant healthy the following is enjoined by the Garuḍa Purāṇa that the administrator, being without food, should fasten in between the tusks of the elephant, a garland, vacā and mustard, duly consecrated with the mantras by the learned. The images of Sūrya, Śiva, Durgā and Viṣṇu are bound to protect the elephant. The elephant should be bathed with four jars full of water. He should be given food consecrated with mantras and ashes should be strewn over him.[28] The Agni Purāṇa also suggests that the grooms should be smeared with oil and clarified butter.[29] Oil and clarified butter should be applied to the shoulders of the beasts too.[30]

Regarding the bed of an elephant it is stated there in the Agni Purāṇa that the bed of an elephant should be strewed with ashes and powdered cow dung and its body should be fomented with oil in summer and autumn—

śayyāsthānaṃ bhaveccāsya karīṣaiḥ pāṃsubhistathā/
śarannidāghayoḥ sekaḥ sapirṣā ca tatheṣyate// [31]

Care Taken for War-Elephants:

For victory in battle the body of the war-elephants should be fumigated with the vapour of kaṭu matsya, viḍaṅga, kṣāra(alkali),milk and haridrā.[32] A paste made of the waste matter of a sparrow or a pigeon and the sticky substance discharged from a kṣīra tree should be applied along the eyelids of elephants.This would make the elephant happy in a battle.[33]

A liniment composed of pippala, oil, rice, madhvikā and makṣikā applied along the eyelids of an elephant, sharpens its eyesight.[34] The petals of blue lotus, muṣṭa and tagara crushed into a paste, mixing the water which is used to wash rice to it, would be beneficial for elephant’s eyes.Anointing with oil is the best remedy for a broken nail.[35]

Part 23 - Kingdoms Noted for Horses

The Purāṇas refer to some kingdoms, which were noted for horses. Yavana was a northern kingdom, noted for horses. It was on the west of Bhāratavarṣa.[1] Saindhava, a kingdom watered by the stream Sindhu, was noted for horse.[2] The horses produced in the Aratta region of Gāndhāra country were known as the best of the horses.

Gāndhāra was the son of Śaradvat and the grandson of Druhyu. After him the country Gandhara was named.[3] Moreover, Gandharava (a kingdom)[4], Kāmboja (a northern kingdom)[5] and Darada (a northern kingdom)[6] were also noted for horses.

In the Purāṇas, names of Kings are found, who kept horses in their kingdom. Pratarddana, the son of Divodāsa, who was from the race of Dhanvantari, had a horse (aśva) named Kuvalaya; therefore, Pratarddana was famous as Kuvalayāśca.[7] The Viṣṇu Purāṇa refers to the demon Narakāsura, the ruller of Prāgjyotiṣa, who beheld six thousand elephants having four tusks and twenty one lakhs horses of Kamboja and other excellent breeds.[8] Dhṛti, the son of Āhuka, had ten lakh and eighty youthful horses.[9] Candraprabhā was the name of Ilā’s horse which became a mare in the Śaravaṇa forest.[10] References of horse-riding are also found in the Purāṇas. Riding on a horse of Sindhu breed, Sudyumna, the foremost among men, rode to the northern forests.[11]

Part 22 - Rites Performed for the Welfare of Horses

The pacification of the horse (aśva-śānti) is described in the Agni Purāṇa. The aśvaśānti is for the health and welfare of the horses.

Aśvaśānti is of three types.

Nitya,

Naimittika and

Kāmya.

On an auspicious day one should worship Śrīdhara (The God Hari), Śrī (The Goddess Lakṣmī) and a royal horse thinking him to be the son of Uccaiḥśravā and one should offer ghee with the mantras invoking Savitṛ. Then daksiṇās are to be paid to the Brāhmaṇas.The performance of this pacificatory rite increases one’s stud.[1]

Again on the fifteenth day of the bright half of the month Āśvina, the Aśvins and Varuṇa (the water god) are to be worshipped; then the image of the goddess should be surrounded by the branches of the sacrificial trees. On all sides, jars full of juices should be placed covered with linen cloth. Barley and ghee should be offered in the sacrificial fire; after that the horses and the Āśvin gods should be worshipped;the Brāhmaṇas should be given dakṣiṇā at last.[2]

In the Naimittika form of pacificatory rite, the God Viṣṇu and the Goddess Lakṣmī is worshipped, when the sun enters the sign of the Capricorn. Brahmā, Śaṃkara, Soma, Āditya, Aśvina, Revanta, Uccaiḥśravā and the guardian deities (Dikpālaḥ) of the different quarters of the sky should also be worshipped. Jars full of water should be ceremonially assigned to the aforesaid deities.Oblations consist of sesamum, sun-dried rice and clarified butter should be poured on the sacrificial fire. Oblations consist of the same should also be offered hundred times in the honour of each deities. The performer should observe a fast that day.[3]

Part 21 - Treatment and Care of Horses

About the health care of horses the Garuḍa Purāṇa advises as follows-the rite of protection of horses should be done by worshipping the God Revanta and by feeding Brāhmaṇas. To prevent from diseases, the essence of the sarala tree, margosa (nimba) leaves, guggula, sarṣapa, ghṛta, tila, vacā, and hiṅgu should be tied to the neck of the horse.[1] The Garuḍa Purāṇa recommends the following points that are to be observed while taking care of horses—

1) For curing the wounds a ball prepared from the garlic, rock salt, butter milk, gruel, oil, malt, margosa leaves should be used.[2]

2) For curing germs in the stomach, compound of paṭola, nimba-patra, vacā, citraka, pippalī, triphalā and śṛṅgavera should be used.[3]

3) Decoction of nimba leaves, paṭola, triphalā and khadira is prescribed to be given for three days in the disease called haya-kuṣṭha(diseases that affects the skin).[4]

4) In any kind of hurt or injury, the horse may be given guggula, to drink and should be made to drink pāyasa.Then he would be hale in loss of time.[5]

5) In case of any diseases one palaṃ weight of dūrvā grass should be given to a horse in the first day. The dose should be increased by measure of one karṣa each day, till it reaches five palaṃ. Eighty palaṃ is the highest dose, sixty is the middle and fourty palaṃ is considered the lowest.[6]

6) If he suffers from any eating trouble, he should be given milk and boiled rice as food. In pitta the same with flesh juice, honey, soup of green gram and ghee is to be given. For cough green gram and kullattha should be administered[7]

7) In diseases that affect the skin, in lameness and in swellings, triphalā boiled on mild fire along with cow’s urine is to be administered.[8]

8) In vāta and pitta together or in vraṇa(ulcer) ghee mixed with cow milk should be given; and to faten the unsteady horses food with flesh should be given.[9]

9) As a general tonic five palas of guḍucī, well powdered, should be given mixed with ghee in the morning, in the autumn and in the hot season; also śatāvarī and aśvagandhā should be prepared with guḍucī. Four palas and three palas should be given respectively to the moderate horse and the horse of a lesser quality.[10]

In the Agni Purāṇa, Śālihotra talks about the warning marks that are usually found in the body of a horse and the diseases it generally suffers from.[11] The diseases of horses and their remedies according to the Agni Purāṇa are as follows:

Bloody urine(raktameha): Goat’s milk mixed with sugar and cooled after being boiled with mañjīṣṭhā, madhuka, drākṣā, bṛhatī, raktacandana, roots and seeds of trapuṣa, śṛṅgāṭaka and kaserukā, should be given to the horse suffering from bloody urine.[12]

Dysentery(atīsāra): When it suffers from dysentery (atīsāra), a small piece of food consist of vṛṣa, nimba, bṛhatī, guḍūcī, siṃhī and gandhakarī should be given. A fomentation of the head with hiṅgu, roots of puṣkara, nāgara, amlavetasa, pippalī with saindhava salt and warm water would also be beneficial. Moreover, a decoction of nāgara, ativiṣa, ananta, musta and vilva leaves may also be a good remedy.[13]

Extreme tiredness(śrama): A drink composed of goat’s milk, priyaṅgu, sārivā and plentiful sugar would take away the extreme tiredness of a horse.[14]

Swelling of limbs(śotha): In case of swelling of limbs, composition of lodhra and karañja roots, mātuluṅga, agni, nāgara, kuṣṭha, hiṅgu, vacā, rāsnā would be favourable.[15]

Swelling of throat (manyāhanunigālasthaśirāśotha): In all cases of lock-jaw and in case of swelling that affects the throat, the affected part should be anointed with the medicated kaṭu taila.[16]

Cough (kāsa): When it suffers from a sudden attack of cough, food composed of dāḍima, triphalā, vyoṣa and guḍa would be beneficial. A decoction of priyaṅgu, lodhra, juice of vṛṣa with honey would also be favourable. A decoction of kṣīra and pañcakola may also be administered.[17]

Infection of throat (galagrahagadaḥ śothaḥ): When it suffers from diseases which affect the throat, then decoction composed of the flowers of pratyaka, vahni, saindhava salt, juice of saurabha, kṛṣṇā and hiṅgu would be beneficial.[18]

Paralysis of the tongue(jihvāstambha): In paralysis of the tongue, paste composed of niśa, jyotiṣmatī, pāṭhā, kṛṣṇā,kuṣṭha and vacā with madhu should be administered.[19]

Pain in abdomen(basti): To relieve a horse of any abdominal pain, he should be given the insertion of oil from a tub or the veins of his abdomen should be cut.[20]

Ulcer (vraṇa): In case of ulcer, clarified butter prepared with sesamum, yaṣṭi, rajanī, nimba leaves or paste composed of the aforesaid drugs mixed with honey would be a good remedy.[21]

Wound (vraṇa): For washing a wound and for antiseptic purposes, decoction composed of the barks of aśvattha, udumbara, plakṣa, madhūka, vilva and vaṭa boiled with abundant water should be used as a remedy.[22]

Bleeding (raktasrāva) and infection of eyes (netraroga): In cases of bleeding or in diseases which affect the end of the eyes of a horse,the affected area can be washed with a decoction composed of udumbara, aśvattha, dhātrī, tikta and priyaṅgu.[23]

Itching of skin (kaṇḍu): When it suffers from itching of the skin, a paste composed of gośakṛn, mañjikā, kuṣṭha, rajanī, tila, sarṣapa, with the urine of a cow should be administered.[24]

Healing snuffing(mrakṣaṇe bastinasye ca): For the purpose of healing snuffing, decoction prepared from śatāhvā, nāgara, rāsnā, mañjiṣṭhā, kuṣṭha, saindhava salt, devadāru, vacā, two sorts of rajanī, raktacandana, juice of guḍucī should be used.[25]

Excess of blood (puṣṭaśoṇitāḥ): If a horse suffers from excess of blood in autumn season or from constipation in the rainy season,then clarified butter should be given to drink. In case of increase of kapha or vāyu, a horse should be given oil to drink.[26]

Disorder of vāyu, pitta and śleṣma: Dūrvā grass destroys pitta. Again yava would be favourable for cough. When a horse suffer from respiratory problem, arjuna would be beneficial. Moreover, diseases that are caused by disorder of vāyu, pitta and śleṣma would never attack a horse, who lives on a meal of dūrvā grass.[27]

Feeding and Nourishment:

Each meal of a horse should consist of four āḍhaka measures of yava, free of husks and mixed with caṇaka, vrīhi, mudga and kalāya pulse.[28] In case of Hoemoptysis (the coughing up of blood) in a horse, decoction of aśvakarṇa mixed with honey and sugar should be applied through its nostrils.On each seventh day salt should be given and vāruṇī wine should be given.[29] In autumn season, decoction of jīvanīya, mṛdvikā, sugar, honey, lotus petals, and pippalī should be given.[30] In winter season, liquid consists of viḍaṅga, pippalī, dhānya, śatāhvā, lodhra, saindhava salt and citraka should be given to drink.[31] In spring season, decoction of lodhra, priyaṅgu, musta, pippalī and viśva mixed with honey should be given to drink.This would also destroy kapha (phelgm).[32] In summer season, wine mixed with molasses and the juice of priyaṅgu, pippalī, lodhra and yaṣṭi should be given to drink and in rainy season, liquid consists of the essence of lodhra, kāṣṭha, pippalī, viśva and salt mixed with oil should be served.[33]

Preservation of Horse-Life:

References of shelters of elephants, stables of horses and references of chariots (hastyaśvarathaśālā) are met with in the Purāṇas.[34] While enumerating the ways of protecting the fort and the city of a king, the Matsya Purāṇa attests that the stables and cowsheds should be to the left side of the king’s palace. The stables should face to the north or to the south. If they face to other directions then it was considered bad. Moreover, there should be lamp burning in the stables through the whole night. The horses should stay in the stables.[35] It was customary to keep in the stables the following beasts for the welfare of the horses—the cocks, the monkeys, especially the apes, cows with their calves and she-goats. Dung should always be cleared away from the stables or elephant yards or cow sheds, before sunset. The quarters of the charioteers (sārathī) should be near the stables.[36] As diseases become more powerful in the fortress therefore, Veterinary doctors (govaidya, aśvavaidya and gajavaidya) should be provided lodging within the fortress.[37] It is suggested there in the Agni Purāṇa that, horses should be stabled in a well-ventilated house and dried bay should be strewn over its floors. The stable should be guarded well and lamps should be lighted there. Goats(aja), monkeys(kapaya), kṛkavākas, deers(mṛgas) should be kept in the stable.[38]
