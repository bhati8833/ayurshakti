5. Samanya (Generality) and Visesa (Particularly) in Ayurveda

"sarvada sarvabhavanam samanyam vrddhikaranam | hrasahetuvisesasya pravrttirubhayasya tu || " Similarity of all substances is always the cause of increase and dissimilarity the cause of decrease, Both effect by their application. "samanyamekatvakaram, visesastu prthakatvakrt | tulyartha hi samanyam, visesastu viparyayah | |  959 i.e., similarity brings unity while dissimilarity causes diversity. Again, similarity purpose (or action) while dissimilarity has opposite one. The concept of Samanya and Visesa is of great importance in Ayurveda and that is why list of six padarthas starts with 212

60 Samanya and Visesa. In the first verse, Caraka gives this applied definition "Samanya invariably causes increase while Visesa causes decrease in all substances". Interpreting the word 'Sarvada Cakrapani says that it means in all times in relation both ever-moving time as well as 61 conditional states interpreting the word 'sarvabhavanam' Cakrapani says that here 'Sarva' means 'all' and 'Bhava means those which are existing such as Dravya, Guna and Karma 62 and not those which are created. Thus the products increased by 'parthiva dvyanuka' with the similarity of eternal atoms of prthvi etc would not be covered by this. Cakrapani emphasizes that 'causation of increase' is the feature of Samanya and not only the generic similarity. For instance, both the flesh to be eaten and that of the body are generically similar but it wouldn't do because in that case the flesh should increase even in those who do not eat meat. Hence, 213

63 'causation of increase' is the feature of Samanya. This is actually not the definition but statement of the feature applicable in Ayurveda. Actual definition is he declare that the second definition mentioned above. 64 Cakrapani further emphasizes that here 'Samanya' should be taken in technical sense as one of the six padarthas and not loosely in sense of similarity because in that case the present 65 statement would be superfluous. But, as will be seen later, in applicant sense, Samanya is reduced to Tulyatva (similarity) in Ayurveda. Cakrapani says that is a general statement of dravyas. But in his view, the present case is quite different because it is not enumeration or statement but an applied definition. In fact, 'causation of increase' is the specific character and as such definition Samanya. 214

According to Cakrapani, Samanya causes increase but it is not only the cause of increase because it may be effected by similar entities as well such as increase of agni and intellect by ghee. On the same basis, some divide Samanya in two types Ubayavrtti (bilateral) and ekavrtti (unilateral). The former is 66 applicable in similar entities while the latter in dissimilar ones. But, really speaking, unilateral similarity is only a form of Visesa and cannot be taken as Samanya. Cakrapani's view that Samanya causes increase but increase is not always caused by Samanya can't be acceptable because it would create chaos and the purpose of Caraka's statement would not be served. In scientific point of view, causal relation between two entities should be established in such a way that cause is inferred from the effect and vice versa. Thus there must be invariable concomitance of Samanya and vrdhi. Accordingly, the definition can be put in both ways, Samanyam 215

vrdhikaram' and 'Vrdhikaranam Samanyam'. In this way one would not be Samanya to effect the increase but would also infer the causation of Samanya by observing increase. Cakrapani further says that Samanya causes increase only when there is no contradicting factor. For instance, the properties like sour etc. in amalaka fruit do not increase similar properties of pitta because of being contradicted by the inherent cooling effect of the fruit. In the like manner, the cases of drugs pacifying all the three dosas may by explained. The increase by dissimilar entities like that of intellect and digestive fire by ghee, and also aggravation of vata caused by anxiety, increase of semen by emotional determination and application of aphrodisiac pastes on sole of feet etc. may be explained with prabhava. The increase caused by Samanya would be effective in case of the similar cases such as flesh would cause increase of flesh and not 216

of blood because it is Visesa and not Samanya in respect of flesh. Commencing on the feature of Visesa. Cakrapani says that as the features of Visesa defined in Vaisesika are not of much use in medicines, Cakra has given causation of disease as distinguishing feature of Visesa. The word 'Visesa means that 67 which is distinguished. For instance, though gavedhuka is Samanya in respect of other gavedhuka grains, it is Visesa in respect of flesh because in the latter there is no generic attribute of gavedhuka. In the same way, flesh in Samanya for flesh, but Visesa in increase should also be understood as in absence of contradictory factor like that of Samanya such as the cases of mandaka etc. Contrary to vata etc. do not pacify vata etc. because of their inherent unwholesome effect. Like two types of cause (similar and dissimilar) of increase in Samanya, Visesa may also be grouped into two - 217

Viruddhavisesa (antagonistic dissimilar) and aviruddhavisesa (non-antagonistic dissimilar). The frame acts directly whereas the latter does indirectly. Here the former is meant by Caraka through the latter also exerts the same effect eventually. Though the non-antagonistic dissimilar does not replenish the decaying tissue elements due to dissimilarity. The decrease in such case is indirect because of non-replenishment of tissues undergoing katabolism such as by constructing dam on a river, there is natural fall in water level upward course. Interpreting the last quarter of the verse, Cakrapani says that the both (Samanya and Visesa) exert their effects only on 68 contact with the body,00 otherwise they would remain ineffective. Secondly, to effect equilibrium of dhatus, both of them act simultaneously otherwise by one-sided action of Samanya there may be increase on one side leading to disequilibrium.' 69 For instance, sweet and unctuous dravyas pacify vata, on one side, 218

they also increase kapha by Samanya so as to maintain the balance otherwise there may arise pathological conditions of vataksaya and kaphavrdhi. "samanyamekatvakaram visesastu prthakatvakrt " - This is taken by Cakrapani as definition of Samanya and Visesa. Samanya is that which produces idea of oneness. For instance, the idea which causes oneness in several cows in different places and times is 70 Samanya. The same is applicable in respect of action (cook etc.) and qualities (white etc.). On the contrary, Visesa in respect 71 of horses because of making distinction." Similarly, the generic attribute of flesh increase the same due to similarity but it behaves as a decreasing factor for vata due to Visesa. However, the same being non-antagonistic dissimilar to blood etc. does not cause such decrease rather it may increase them due to similarity in quantity. 219

The second half of the verse is taken by Cakrapani as supporting argument for the first one. He interprets that Samanya means similarity and Visesa as the reverse. E.g., dissimilarity." 72 Cakrapani conclude this topic with remark that in order to avoid details the subject has been dealt with only in respect of its application to Ayurveda. Those who are interested in (philosophical) details may go through the Vaisesika Philosophy. According to Vaisesika Philosophy, Samanya and Visesa 73 are relative terms and they are limited to dravya, guna and 74 karma only. Prasastapada has defines Samanya as that which produces idea of oneness whereas Visesa is that which discriminates." According to its field of jurisdiction Samanya is said to be of two types para (superior) and apara (inferior). Satta (existence) itself is para while dravyatva, gunatva and karmatva are parasamanya. The latter three are at the same time both Samanya in respect of the members in Samanya for prthvi 220

etc but is Visesa to gunatva and karmatva. Technically speaking, Visesa is used only to denote the discrimination between atoms which stands at the terminal point and are eternal but secondarily it is used to denote discrimination in dravya-guna etc. too and 76 such is called as Bhakta (secondary). The later authors have also accepted a third type of Samanya as 'parapara' which is both para as well as apara such as dravyatva is para in relation of 77 prthvi etc. but is apara in that to satta. Para is that having wider extent while apara is that having narrower one. 78 In Siddhantamuktavali, Samanya is defined as that which, being eternal, is inherently related to more than one. 79 In pratibimbatva commentary on the Tarkasamgraha, Samanya is said as generic attribute and Visesa as self- 80 discriminating. Bhasaparicheda also takes Samanya as Jati but Vatsyayana while interpreting the aphorism of Gautama (samanaprasavatmika jatih ) distinguishes Samanya from jati though in 221

the aphorism there is no such indication. Vatsyayana says that which produces idea of sameness in different individuals but does not discriminate them mutually is samanya whereas jati is a type of samanya which produces non- difference in some but difference from some others." 81 According to this definition, samanya is purely samanya whereas jati performs functions of both samanya and visesa. Thus the statement 'samanya viseso jatih  ' is very meaningful. Now if we look at the statement of Caraka on this topic, it is evident that 'samanyamekatvakaram ', 'visesastu prthaktvakrt ', is the definition tallying with that given in Vaisesika philosophy but Caraka extended it further and said that as samanya and visesa cause oneness and discrimination respectively the exhibit consequent effects of additions and subtraction on body tissues when applied. Thus Caraka has remarkably been able to apply the philosophical concept to the realm of human body in relation 222

to its physiology and medicine. It is to be noted that in the definition propounded in philosophical texts there is emphasis only on the idea of sameness and discrimination whereas Caraka has made it as background for evolving his own concept of Samanya as applicable to medicine.
