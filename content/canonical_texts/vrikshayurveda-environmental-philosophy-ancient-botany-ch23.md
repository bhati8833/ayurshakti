3. Philosophy behind the planting of Trees near the Abode

VI.2. Environmental Philosophy behind the determination of Auspicious & Inauspicious directions for planting of Trees near the Abode:—In an environment, full of danger to life, the trees indeed are the friends to human life and animal life. That is why people regarded them as the beneficent Devata. Trees provide us their pleasant shade against the scorching heat of the Sun. The tree bears the rich foliage of blooming flowers and sweet and Juicy fruits for the welfare of human beings. The birth and life of a tree is very useful. It makes the life of all creatures comfortable. Thus, trees are quite righteous, generous, self sacrificing. As such, planting or sowing a seed and growing it into a tree are compared with giving birth to a son and bringing him up as a man. There is a possibility of a son going way ward, become harmful and dangerous in the society, but there is no such possibility of such hazards in the case of a tree. So, everyone bows down to tree, the supreme preceptor, who preaches to the world the lesson of self-sacrifice for the sake of serving others. However, for one's own benefit one should plant trees and take care of them. The directions for planting of trees have been vividly prescribed in the Puranas, Dharmagranthes and Vrikshayurveda texts, particularly in the text of Surapala.

235 Vrikshayurveda of Surapala guides as to what types of plant should be planted near the abode to enjoy their positive results. Trees are considered auspicious or in auspicious, according to the direction in which they are planted. If one plants Nyagrodha to the east of the house it fulfills all wishes. Similarly, Udumbara to the South and Pippala to the west are auspicious. (Vrikshayurveda v.24) If one plants plaksa to the North it is also held auspicious (Vrikshayurveda v.25) One should avoid planting asvattha to the east of the house, Plaksa to the South, Nyagrodha in the West and Udumbara in the North as they are in auspicious and gives as negative result. (Vrikshayurveda v.26) A benevolent tree is the shelter place for birds, snakes, beasts and human beings. Gods, demons, gandharvas, ghosts, rakshasas also take resot to the trees (Vrikshayurveda v.27). Surapala prohibits the planting of trees in front if the house, because the shade of the tree should not fall on the house. Even gold-yielding tree should not be planted at the entrance of the house (Vrikshayurveda v.28). The author names the trees which are not to be planted in a house. The house where badari, Kadali, dadimi and bijapurak are grown does not prosper well. He also guides as not to plant eranda,

236 Kancanara, slesmataka, arjuna, and karanja in the vicinity of the house of a happy man. (Vrikshayurveda v. 29, 30) According to the philosophical transcription a person should not plant Nili and haridra (turmeric), because they cause loss of progeny and prosperity. Even when these are sprouted of their own accord, they should be up rooted by the wise man following the advice of the sages (Vrikshayurveda v.32). Planting of a garden is prohibited in some specific directions as rest rained to be inauspicious leading to quarrels, distress, and adversity. As such one should not plant a garden to the south, southwest, or northeast of the house (Vrikshayurveda v.33). Thus, a king he who avoids these directions and plans for a garden in proper advised directions viz. west, north, and east in the vicinity of a dwelling house is rewarded with progeny (Vrikshayurveda v.34). A wise-man should plant Bhillota in all directions surrounding all other trees carefully without fail as they keep the tree free from diseases ensuring good health (Vrikshayurveda v.91).

6. Environmental Philosophy behind the Nourishment of Trees

Surapala recommends the philosophy beyond the growth of the young trees to be administered every seventh or eighth day with small quantity of fish, flesh and sesamum cocked together (v.107). Young trees are to be protected from heat, strong wind flow, smoke, fire, and spider (v.108). He also mentions the rules for watering plants (v.109, 110, 111, 112) according to the types of land. The writer here beautifully justifies the extreme satisfaction of the trees they get from their fruits and flowers when they are set free from the suspicion of any impurity and are subjected to treatment (v.116). He recommends several methods for protecting the plants concerning their nourishment techniques. These include besmearing, watering, sprinkling, and fumigation and sometimes combining them even. Surapala lays down separate prescriptions for separate plants individually (v.122 -157).

240 VI.5. Environmental Philosophy behind the Care and Treatment of Trees against Diseases: VI.5.1. Plant Disorders: Surapala had given a vivid exposition on the knowledge of plant diseases with very originality and the sense of interesting expressions. He classifies the diseases broadly into two categories; those arising from outside and the one from their body within (v.165). The internal diseases are caused by the disorders of wind (vata), phlegm (kapha), and bile (pitta) whereas, those affecting from outside are caused by lacking in vermin's, frosts, etc. (v.166). VI.5.2. The Treatment of Plant Diseases: The scribe of the text has prescribed very systematically and. methodically the treatment for all plant diseases. In comparison just like human body plants to suffer from problems such as displacement or dislocation of flower, trunk, leaves, barks and the author has also mentioned the for its care and treatment as well. The diseases due to phlegm can be cured by the application of oil cakes of white mustard to the roots of the tree and irrigate thereof with water in which sesamum has been dissolved (v. 188). The diseases due to wind can be cured by digging out the earth

241 near their roots (v. 189) and the diseases due to bile can be removed by sprinkling water mixed with milk honey and the decoction of madhuka (v.191) and further the trees are relived of diseases caused by bile when irrigated with the decocted water of the fruits of three myrobalan (triphala) (v. 192). The author however has mentioned systematically different treatments for various other types of plant disorders too.
