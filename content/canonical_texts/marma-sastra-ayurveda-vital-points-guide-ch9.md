Study of Krikatika Marma

Krikatika Etymology—This has the root word Krik = Kandha (neck). Krik + Ad (Gathau) this is the area where the neck joins the head. In tamil it is termed as Pidari. Description These marmas are seen at the junction of the head and upper part of the neck. When these are injured, the balance of the head in erect posture is lost. The sirogreeva sandhi should be the Atlanto occipital joint. It is this joint that keeps the head in erect position. When this joint gets injured it loses its stability and the head cannot be retained in its erect position. These are sandhi marmas. Vaikalyakara marma and half anguli in extent. They are two in numbers. Its bhuta structure is Jala. Adhidevata is Isvara. Gananatha Sen in his book Prathyaksha Sareeram says that krkatika is in the shape of anguri and is a tharunasthi which controls the voice. 5 Surface Anatomy Slightly bend the head of the patient in vertical flexion. The site of this marma is seen at the back of the head near the small pit at the junction of the head and neck. If injured the person might become insane. If we stimulate the back of the neck the patient can be retrieved from this injury. 115

Study of Matruka Marma

Matruka Marma Etymology—Derived from the root word Mathru meaning maternal. However the word Matruka has the following meanings Coming or inherited from mother, □ Maternal, Nurse, □ A source/ origin, A necklace which is worn around neck. Description On either side of the neck there are four Sira matrukas. Injury to any one of these marmas cause severe haemorrhage leading to collapse and death immediately. Vaghbata opines that there are four marmas on each side of the neck running superiorly to the jihva (tongue) and nasa (nose). They are Matrukas and if injured can cause sudden death.` They are eight in numbers. Four on each side of the neck. This is a Sira marma and Sadya pranahara marma. The pramana is swapanithala, four angula in pramana (Dalhana). Its bhuta structure is Jala. Adhidevata is Isvara. Surface Anatomy The details for identifying the exact area is very difficult. Two opinions regarding this is - 1. It exist collectively like a cluster. 2. The are seen vertically in the side of the neck. 108

Dr. K.H. Krishnamoorthy is his book The Wealth of Susruta narrates the four dhamanis. As the word Matruka has the meaning of necklace, this could mean some structure similar to that. While discussing about the word Matruka and its meaning it is told in Sabda Kalpadruma that this means that somebody who is just like a mother. She need not necessarily be the mother. Thinking the same in this context one may tend to doubt that, this is something like a mother for the rest two adjacent marma namely the Manya and Nila. Hence this is termed as Common Carotid and the Nila and Manya as external and internal carotid arteries. 110

As this is a sira marma and causes sudden death, this marma will invariably be blood vessels. This is because death occurs due to haemorrhage. Among this, the subclavian artery supplies the upper limb and subclavian veins drain the blood from the upper limb. This does not correspond to the description of Vaghbata. The external and internal carotid arteries forming the branch of common carotid runs superiorly to the above mentioned structures and corresponds to this marma. Similarly the external and internal jugular veins too drain blood from the corresponding areas and could therefore be taken into consideration for this marma. The only other structure that could cause sudden death after an injury is the vagus nerve. Pressure over carotid sinus may cause vagus irritation thereby leading to death due to cardiac arrest. But this being a sira marma and also eight in number, the internal and external carotid and jugular veins could be taken into consideration. 111

Study of Vidhura Marma

Vidhura Etymology—The word Vidhura is derived from the root Dhura = weight. This word is formed by Vigatha Dhura = Chesta nashta, or imbalance of the body. Description The situation of this marma is behind the pinna of the ear. It is seen near the pit like structure seen behind the ear. They are two in number. Its prognosis is Vaikalyakara. They are half anguli in parimana. If injured will lead to total deafness. Its bhuta structure is Jala. Adhidevata is Isvara. 6,7,8 Vaghbata consider this as Dhamani marma and Susruta consider this as Snayu marma. As this lead to deafness Susruta's opinion of this marma as a snayu marma stands good. Surface Anatomy It is easy to palpate marma behind the pinna of ear just below the mastoid bone where the ligament of strenocliedo mastoid muscle joins it.
