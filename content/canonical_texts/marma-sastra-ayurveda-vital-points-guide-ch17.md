Study of Parsvasandhi Marma

Etymology Parsvasandhi 6,7 This too is a combination of two words, B Parsva = lateral side, □ Sandhi = joint. They are self explanatory. Description Situation of these marmas are between costal cartilage and the pelvis, corresponding to the vessels of kidney seen medially and upwards. Injury to this marma causes collection of blood in the abdominal cavity and causes danger of life. Injury to this marma (Coeliac axes) which are situated medially below the extremities of the side (Parsva), and which lies attached at the middle between the loins fills the koshta with blood and results in death. They are sira marma two in number and half anguli in extent. Its bhuta structure is Agni and Jala. Adhidevatha is Rudra. Surface Anatomy This point falls between the two parsva, urah and koshta parsva (Parshavanta rapratibadhau), and also the point falls above the pelvic girdle (Jaghana). The vertical area drawn from the region of axilla on the iliac spine, passes through the region of Parsva. The sandhi is the junctional area of the two 201

Deltoid muscle Infraspinatus fascia Triangle of auscultation Trapezius muscle Rhomboideus minor muscle Rhomboideus major muscle Acromion Supraspinatus muscle Spine of scapula Infraspinatus muscle Teres minor muscle Teres major muscle Plate No. 24: The anatomical area of Parsvasandhi parsva. Thus parsva sandhi denotes the space immediately after twelfth rib on both sides. Anatomical Structures corresponding to this marma Renal artery and Renal vein. Discussion ☐ ☐ Subcostal nerve and artery ☐ Ilio hypogastric nerve ☐ Ilio inguinal nerve ☐ Kidney as a whole Right side Liver ☐ Left Side Spleen. This is interesting to note that the acharyas of the yore were able to identify this area as very vital and protected this renal angle area It has relation to swadhishtana chakra of yoga. 202

Tamil varma sastra Cheriya atti surukki is situated half angula distance from the pelvis towards the rib. If a hit is received in a slanting hand at a distance of 70 cms the patient will fall shivering. Frantically he will beat the floor with his legs and hand. He will also have intense thirst. Yenthi varmam is positioned 5 cms. below the Thivalai varmam on either side of the rib cage. The Yenthi varmam located on the left side is known as left Yenthi varmam and the one on the right side Yenthi varmam. The victim of this varmam disorder will suffer form tetanus. The patient will becomes weak and lose his hearing power. Within 3 hours the patient should be attended to, for cure. The location of Kondakolly marma is in between the axilla and the hip inside the rib cage. If injured he should be immediately manipulated by pressing the Orkarathu marma in the lower limb. Or this injury could be manipulated by pressing the opposite side. 203

Appendix 3: Various other Varmam

Sadappira varmam is otherwise known as Ananda Sadappira varmam. Below the navel adjacent to the nadusurukki lies the Sadappira. Sadappira varmam is situated on the back exactly opposite to the kaikkaatti varmam on either sides. Sada means the dead body. Persons affected with varma resemble like a dead body. The victim will be dazed and his eyes will be open once Sadappira life centre is disturbed. Tiredness and pain all over the body are other symptoms. This disorder should be attended to within six hours of its occurrence. Kilippura varmam are two, varmas situated 2.5 cms. above the sadappira life-centre. Kilippira is described as Kilimegha in Varma Kannadi. The face will be distorted on one side. Motion will be blocked. The patient will laugh, and not be able to speak. 214

It is preferably be treated within seven hours of the on set of the disorder for cure. Kilimega varmam life centre is found 5 cms. above the Kilippura varmam, which is 5 cms. below the shoulder blade on the back According to Varma Suttiram this is in the hip region 4 fingers above the sadappira varma. If the Kilimega life-centre is affected the weak person will lose his consciousness within one and half hours. The patient will make sound like a parrot. Tears in the eyes will also be seen. The victim should be treated within four hours. Kilippura varmam and kilimega varmam are located on either side of the body. These two lifecentres are together called as poonool varmam. Poonool life centre is situated on both sides of the shoulders bone on which the holy thread (poonool) is normally worn. If Poonool varmam life-centre is affected, the patient will lose his consciousness, and the limbs will lose activities. Continuous coughing and tetanus are also seen. The patient will have nightmares. 215

There are difference of opinion regarding the position of the Nettellu varmas. Some say that these are two fingers above the Pallavarma which is located at the depression of the mundellu. There are 32 vertebrae in the backbone. In this column, there are eight life centres and a collective name for these eight life centres is Nettellu varmam. They are situated one below the other. Nettellu varmam I (Mel Suzhiyaadi varmam) is situated 11cm. from top of the head. That is positioned at the beginning of the spinal column. The affliction of Mel suzhiyaadi life centre leads to loss of appetite, tuberculosis, fever, shivering and itching at the top of the spinal column. The controlling nerve will lose its strength. If the impact at the life centre is serious, the person will become insane. It should be treated at the earliest. Nettellu varmam II resides 7 cms. below the nettellu varmam - I If this varmam sustains damage, the symptoms will be worse than the previous varma disorder. The patient vomit blood. Nettellu varmam III is situated 7 cms. below the Nettellu varmam II. - - 216

If affected, the patient will have fever and cough. His testicles will ascend into the abdomen. After two hours there will be heavy breathing and the patient faint. Frothing at mouth will also occur. Nettellu varmam - IV is situated 7 cms. below nettellu varmam III which is more or less in the centre of the back. varmam In case disorder occurs to Nettellu IV the patient will have hunch back and burning micturition. The patient will become weak and have tuberculosis. The senses will function below normal and heavy coughing will obstruct speech. Nettellu varmam V is situated 4 cms. below the centre of the back. Damage to the life centre reveals symptoms similar to poisoning. Further giddiness, tiredness, sweating, dryness of the mouth, stomach pain, painful urination and constipation will appear. The patient should be rubbed with the special medicated oil (Sarvaanga thailam). Still the patient is likely to expire on the 648th day. Nettellu varmam VI is situated 7 cms. below the Nettellu varmam V - 217

If Nettellu varmam - VI is affected the patient will feel tired, giddiness and blurring of vision. Nettellu varmam - VII is located on the two depressions found just over the buttocks. Excretion of urine and motion, as well as dislocation of hip bones are the symptoms which reveal the affliction of this life-centre. Nettellu varma - VIII is otherwise called Kaikatti varmam or Keezh suzhiyadi varmam. Nettellu varmam VIII is positioned at the coccyx bone. In case of its disorder, the patient will faint and froth at the mouth. He will be totally disabled. Even if treated he will have abdominal disturbance. Pain in the chest, dysentery (kirani), vomiting, tuberculosis etc. will also occur. The patient will become a mental wreck. seven hours. The patient is to be attended to within Kachchai varmam is situated slightly below the Nettellu varmam. If affected the patient will have pain in the legs. He will have retention of urine and motion. If the symptoms do not appear initially, the same will be 218

seen later. Irritation at the thoracic region will arise due to gastritis and chest pain. Kaikkootti varmam is located 7 cms. below the adappa varmam. The patient will faint and have breathing difficulties once this life-centre is affected with impact. Pain in the ribs and limbs will be felt. There will be blood stain in the saliva and stomach ache with the stretching of body. The urine will drip continuously. The patient should be treated within seven hours of the disorder to this life-centre; otherwise it is difficult to cure. Vasaukkala. Vaasikkala is otherwise named Vaasikkala varmam resides on the sharp points of the vertebrae. There are sixteen such points situated on all the vertebrae. These are on the reverse sides also. 219

Study of Amsa Marma

Amsa Etymology—The word Amsa has a various range of meaning starting from Shoulder, a A marma, □ Amsha, □ Yudha. Description There are two marmas seen situated on either side midway between the neck and the head of the arms and connects the amsa peetha and skandha (Shoulder). An injury to any of these marma can lead to incapacity of moving the hands. They are two in number and are snayu in origin and they are vaikalyakaram also. It is of half anguli in extent. Its bhuta structure is Jala. Adhidevatha is Iswara. Surface Anatomy This marma is located on the amsa which is formed by the union of amsa peetha (glenoid) and the Skanda (acromioclavicular). This is found on either side of the shoulder. Probe the index finger on acromion protuberance and glide the finger slowly towards the base of the cervical triangle. Few centimeters immediately, after the bony structures, it is possible to feel tender. 210

Anatomical Structures corresponding to this marma Coraco clavicular ligament. Trapezius Acromion Deltopectoral triangle Deltoid muscle Deltoid branch of thoracoacromial artery Cephalic vein Pectoralis major muscle Plate No. 27: The anatomical area of Amsa Discussion ☐ Conoid ligament. ☐ Trapezoid ligament. ☐ Superior acromioclavicular ligament. ☐ Coraco - acromial ligament. ☐ Sub acromial bursa ☐ Brachial Plexus ☐ Shoulder joint as a whole Clavicle Sternum Shoulder joint is the only joint having a wide range of movements. Any injury to this part will lead to restriction of movements there by incapacitating the limb. Tamil varma sastra Kaakkattai varmam is positioned on both the shoulders where the collar bones join on either side. 211

The patient will faint and the head will sink once this life centre is disturbed. The neck will be affected and the patient will at times vomit blood. The body will jerk and the patient will have muscle cramps on the arms and legs. The pulse will be at low ebb. The treatment should be given within four hours of impact at the life centre. Otherwise the damage will be permanent and loss of life may occur. Some times the patient's head will sink permanently and the affected side will also be paralyzed. Bhuja varmam rests at the tips of the collar bones on either side. In other words, each upper arm has a Bhuja varmam. The victim of this disorder, will not be able to lift the hands freely. The area will become numb and swelling will set in. The patient will not have a wink of sleep and will become faint. The patient should be attended to within one and half hours. Vilangadi varmam (vilangu varmam) which is otherwise called Vilangu varmam is situated at the point where the collar bone and the shoulder bone meet on the front side of the body. 212

In case the Vilangu life-centre is affected the victim cannot lift his arms. Fainting giddiness and fever will arise. Sulukku varmam is otherwise called Kaaraikkuzhi varmam. Sulukku varmam is located at the meeting of collar bone and the shoulder bone at the sides. If disorder occurs to Sulukku life-centre, the middle finger will be affected because the nerve running down the arm will be affected. Hence the name Sulukku varmam. This varmam is found on either side of the shoulders. Sulukku varma disorder will make the patient lose consciousness, will produce protruding of tongue and twisting of the neck. The impact to sulukku varmam (nerve centre) should be attended to within seven hours of disorder. Kulayana marma Kulayana marma adds one more marma in this position. Phanam is a marma seen inside the armpit. If injured the patient faints. He could be 213

brought to consciousness by manipulating the same area just two angulas below the arm pit and in and around the breast.
