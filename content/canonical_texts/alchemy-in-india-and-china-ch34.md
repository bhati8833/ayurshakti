2. Case of Naga (i.e., Dragon or Long)

Naga was the name for lead in Sanskrit since ancient times. It meant a snake, cobra or a semidivine serpent-dragon which inhabits patala (the nether world). Similarly we find in the Chinese language also the name of dragon, i.e., "long" was sometimes attributed to the metal lead. Long long dragon Naga Further, in Buddhist texts, translated from Sanskrit to Chinese, in the early centuries of the 132

Christian era, the name of the author "Nagarjuna"l translated into Chinese as "long shu" or "long mu", was meaning dragon-tree or dragon-wood, which is parallel to Naga-arjuna (name of a tree) = Nagarjuna. shu shu tree, mu mu wood or tree 133 Obviously the translater knew that "Naga" and "long" are the words for "dragon" in Sanskrit and the Chinese languages, respectively. This fact suggests that there is a great probability that the usage of the word dragon (long or Naga) for the metal lead was borrowed from one culture by the other. 1 Needham, Joseph (1), Vol. 5, Pt. Needham tells us that Nagarjuna's name was translated as long shu or long mu and also as long mu Wang (prince Long mu, where ancient Bodhisattva was transformed into a spiritual prince Long mu wang). Further, in the Chinese Tripitaka we find the name translated as "long meng" where "meng" means cruel, fierce or courageous and "long sheng" where "sheng" is to excel or to be superior to.
