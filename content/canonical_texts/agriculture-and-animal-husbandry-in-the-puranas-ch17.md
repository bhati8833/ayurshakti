## Part 4 - Classification of Animals

Part 4 - Classification of Animals

Prajāpati created birds and beasts;he created goats from the mouth, sheep from the chest, from the stomach and from the two flanks and feet, the horses along with the elephants,the oxen, the camels, the mules etc.[1] The Vāyu Purāṇa again speaks that Dakṣa created two-legged and four–legged creatures, in the Vaivasvata epoch.[2] The four-fold classification of creatures, viz. aṇḍaja (oviparous), udbhijja (germinating ones), svedaja (those generated by warm vapours and sweat) and jarāyuja(viviparous) is frequently mentioned in the Purāṇas.[3] Another system of classification is indicated by the grāmya and āraṇya paśus. At the commencement of Tretā Yuga, grāmya paśus and āraṇyakas were created by Prajāpati. The grāmya (i.e. the domesticated) animals are: go, aja, meṣa, aśva, aśvatārā and gardabha. The āraṇya (i.e. wild) beasts are: svāpadas, dvi-khuras (i.e. having two hoofs), hastī, vānara, pakṣis and sarī-sṛpas (sapta grāmya paśava, sapta āraṇyakas).[4]

The Bhāgavaṭa Purāṇa[5] mentions twenty-eight kinds of creatures.They are—

(i) Six kinds that have single hoofs: the horse, the mule, the ass, the yak, the śarabha and the gaura or white deer comes under this group.[6]

(ii) Nine kinds which have double or cloven hoofs: the cow, the goat, the buffalo, the hog, the gayal, the black deer, the antelope, the camel and the sheep comes under this group.[7]

(iii) Thirteen kinds which have five claws or nails instead of hoofs: the dog, jackal, wolf, tiger, cat, hare, porcupine, lion, monkey, elephant, tortoise, lizard and alligator come under this group.[8]

## Part 13 - The Bull in the Purāṇas

Part 13 - The Bull in the Purāṇas

The bull figures in the Purāṇas mostly as a vehicle of Śiva[1] who is also mentioned as Vṛṣabha-dhvaja or Vṛṣavāhana (i.e. having the Bull standard).[2] Bullock carts (śakaṭa) were used for travelling.[3]

The bull is believed to be auspicious. The Agni Purāṇa records bull-fights for the general weal of cattle (go-śānti) on the first day of the bright half of the Āśvina, along with band and music.[4] The word govṛśāṅka in the Vāyu Purāṇa, refers to the bull.[5] The Bull (Govṛśa) is the overlord of four-footed animals (quadrupeds).[6] Saurabheya refers to the bull from which Aśija learnt the Godharma (the law of cattle). One day it ate all the grass kept for ceremonial occasion and was forcibly retained by Dīrghatamas who on hearing the Godharma from it i.e., from Saurabheya, the son of Surabhi, the mythical cow, left it go. Surabhi got him rid of all curses including blindness and old age.[7]

Qualities of Bull:

The Matsya Purāṇa[8] gives the various qualities of the bull, and also mentions its types. According to it, the bull having the following qualities were considered auspicious:[9]

1) The bull should be endowed with a prominent hump and should be strong.
2) It should have red neck and tongue,
3) It should be of nice colour,
4) It must have beautiful hoofs and horns,
5) It should be of middle height,
6) It should give good milk,
7) It should have high shoulders,
8) It should have spacious shoulder sides and waist, and
9) His eyes should be like the vaidūrya gem.

Before keeping in the household the bulls should be well-examined. The forepart of the horns and eyes of a bull should be red and its hoofs should be like coral. These types of bull with white body were considered the best. These can be kept in the house. These increase grains and wealth.[10] The bulls which are well-built, roar like the thunder-clouds, high in size, walking like an angry elephant, having broad chest and those who are very powerful can be kept in the household.[11] In case of cow, chest, back, head, belly and loins-these parts of a cow’s body should be raised to a more impressive level. Eyes, ears and forehead-these parts should be at the same height and tail, dewlap, udders, thighs-these parts should be capacious i.e. having a lot of space inside. These types of cow with large head and neck are of high quality.[12]

Breeds of Bull:

Various types of bulls are mentioned in the Purāṇas. They are—

1) Nandīmukha—The bull which is especially of red colour and whose face is white up to the ears is known as Nandīmukha.[13]

2) Samudra—The bull whose stomach and back are white is known as Samudra. These types of bull increase the offspring of the race.[14] and

3) Nīla Vṛṣabha—The bull whose colour of the four feet, face and tail are white and whose colour is like the juice of lac is known as Nīla Vṛṣabha.[15] These Nīla Vṛṣabhas are said to be excellent. The tuft of hair at the end of the tail of such type of bull is so long that it touches the ground.[16]

The bulls having the signs of a pearl, a banner, etc., are also excellent.They are the givers of great siddhi and jaya(success).[17] Again, one who desires to have many sons, he should dedicate a Nīla Vṛṣabha. Then his desire would be fulfilled.[18]

Different coloured bulls and their significance are also mentioned in the Purāṇas. They are—

1) white coloured,
2) tawny coloured,
3) red coloured and 
4) black coloured etc.[19]

The white coloured bull with black head, ears, forehead, tuft of hair at the end of the tail, feet and eyes are excellent. Likewise the black bulls with all those aforesaid things white in colour are considered the same.[20] The bull with the colour of Jasmin (Jarminum sambae) flower increases wealth. Those which have circles like lotus flower, increase the fortune. Again, the bull with the colour of Atasī (Linum usitatissimum) flower increases prosperity. These types of bulls are considered good.[21]

Gift of Bull:

The gift of a bull, has been recommended. According to the Agni Purāṇa, if a person releases a bull on a day of the month of Kārttika and fasts at night, he gets to the region of Śiva.[22] The term Vṛṣotsarga in the Purāṇas signifies the rules for the dedication of a bull. Bull and cow with its calf were intended for gift for all castes.Gift of Nīla bull at Gayā, was considered best for pitṛs.[23] Vārṣavratam leads to the world of Śiva; consists of dedication of a bull in the month of Kārttika.[24]

## Part 14 - The Elephant in the Purāṇas

Part 14 - The Elephant in the Purāṇas

The Vāyu Purāṇa shows elephants as objects of gifts to Brāhmaṇas in śrāddha[1] and mentions it as a Yajñīya animal. The elephants were thought to be born from water.[2] The elephant was purely a wild animal and was not domesticated. Irāvati’s son Airāvata, the vehicle of Indra, was the first king of elephants.[3] The elephants are the best ornamentation of a royal camp and a king can always achieve success with armed force alone by means of his elephant.[4] Making of gifts under the shadow of an elephant was considered to be meritorious.[5] The Agni Purāṇa[6] enjoins that an elephant passing by the right side is a good omen.

Breeds of Elephants:

The varieties of elephants named as Kumuda, Añjana, Vāmana and Airāvata are met with in the Matsya Purāṇa.[7] The Vāyu Purāṇa too mentions the varieties of elephants such as Supratiṣṭha, Rathāntara, Puṇḍarīka (an elephant born of Rathantara group), Pramardana (an elephant born of Sāma),[8] Vibhāvasu,[9] Bṛhatsāman, Puṣpadanta (the elephant of Sāma fold with six tusks), Ṣaḍdanta, Dantapuṣpavān and Tāmravarṇa etc.[10] Ṛṣabha, Puṣkaracuḍa, Vāmana and Aparājit were the elephants at the four cardinal points to maintain the balance of the worlds.[11] The word Diggaja in the Purāṇas refers to the aforesaid four elephants at the four quarters with clouds.[12] Parjanya and the quarter tuskers take birth in the winter season. Born of cold, they pour out showers of snow for the overgrowth of all the corns or crops.[13]

Characteristics of Elephants:

Pālakāpya described to Lomapāda, the king of Aṅga, the various characteristics that are exceptionally well in case of elephants and the remedies of the diseases they suffer from, when they are kept as pets.[14]

The characteristics of good elephants are:

1) those who have a long trunk (dīrghahastā) and deep breath (mahocchvāsa = mahocchvāsāḥ),
2) having the ability of tolerating (sahiṣṇava),
3) having twenty or eighteen nails (viṃśatyaṣṭādaśanakhāḥ) and emit rut in the winter season (śītakālamadāḥ ca),
4) whose right tusks are raised to be of more impressive level than left tusks (dakṣiṇaśconnato danto),
5) have the roar like the cloud-thunder (bṛṃhita),
6) which in every case attain to a large size (jaladopama),
7) with large and well-developed ears which move up and down or to and fro (karṇau ca vipulau), and
8) have tiny dots on the skin (sūkṣmavindvanvitāstvaci).

The elephants with these characteristics should only be kept in the stables, on the other hand, the elephants which are of short height and continuously require use of the goading mace, should not be so kept as they are of bad characteristics.[15] Pālakāpya states the seven points of shine that are to be observed in purchasing an elephant.A man who is possessed of such a noble animal, can conquer his enemy in battle.[16]

They are:

the colour (varṣa),

the strength (sattva),

the general build (vala),

the speed (rūpa),

the roundness of figures (kānti),

the eager to fight (saṃhanana),

the power of tolerance (java).

## Part 10 - Expiation for Killing a Cow

Part 10 - Expiation for Killing a Cow

The penance of killing a cow is as follows: The cow killer should drink barley soup for a month; he should stay in the cow-stall for that time, wearing the hide of the same cow. He should also eat once in four times without salt. Moreover, he should take bath with cow-urine for two months with full control over senses.[1] Rodha is a kind of hell; here falls slayers (killers) of cow.[2] Viśasana is one of the twenty-eight hells. It is a slaughter house for hypocritical sacrificers of animals.[3] Śūdrahatyā vratam is the vow for injuring bony animals. Killing animals having bones was considered a sin and to purify himself of such a sin one should observe the Śūdrahatyā vratam and also make some gift to a Brāhmaṇa.[4] Suvarṇam is a golden coin; a fine of hundred Suvarṇas for one who having been paid a wage, but does not protect the cow or milks it or uses it; besides he is liable to be bound by iron chains and made to work for his master.[5]
