Study of Shringataka Marma

Shringataka Etymology—This is derived from the word Sring + ada + gatau. The word sringam is derived from the root Shr=himsayam. (Summit of a mountain.) A place where four roads meet. Description In the middle of the siras that irrigate the nose, ear, eye and tongue, there is a cross way or the sringhatakas They are four in number. According to Susruta they are sira marma and Vaghbata says that they are dhamani marma. They are sadya pranahara marma four anguli in extent. Surface Anatomy The anatomical situation of this marma is on the surface of the brain corresponding to the location of nerve centres which consist of nerve cells and fibres which are responsible for the functions of speech (Broca's centre) vision, hearing, taste and smell. Injury to any one of these four, injures the basal ganglia leading to death. Anatomical Structures corresponding to this marma Brocas centre Discussion ☐ ☐ Visio sensory centre ☐ Centre for hearing ☐ Centre for taste and smell. ☐ Soft palate The site of this marma is in Thalu (soft palate) where the cavities of the four Indriyas (The sense of taste, hearing, smell, and vision) meet. Any injury here will lead to injury to medulla oblongata which leads to bulbar paralysis and above said symptoms. The opening of all these in the thalu are termed as Shringataka marma which are four in numbers. Being a Sira marma and four in number this is also suggestive of the Circulus arteriosus of Willis. 144

Tamil varma sastra Patchi varma is situated 1.5 cms. below the centre of the eye joint above the sema kalam. This is termed as Pacci because just like various birds flocking will lead to the description. This also belongs to a peculiar group of varma disorders. Both the lower and upper jaws will be pulled to one side. Even if there is relief, after one month jaws will be pulled to one side. Kannaadi varmam is otherwise called Mookirani varmam. It is located on the bridge of the nose where the spectacles rest. In case of any impact sustained at Kannaadi varmam, the patient will becomes unconscious. The victim will lose strength. Blood will come out of the nose and ears. If the impact is of severe nature, the victim will die. Within one hour and thirty minutes, the victim should be retrieved. Bala varma is situated 1.5cms. below the Nema varmam which is 1.5cms., below the centre of the eye. Disturbance at this life-centre leads to convulsion (Udhira-sanni) and increase in phlegm 145

(Silerpanam) accompanied by active ulcer (Ashta gunmam), muscle pull (kolutthu) and epilepsy (valippu). If the disturbance is of mild nature, tuberculosis, anemias and asthma (chayam), paandu and Eelai may occur in later stages. This life-centre too belongs to peculiar type of life-centres (varmams). Kulayana marma In Kulayana marma this has close relation with Mookkadappan which is described as follows. The site of this marma is below the eyebrows just above the starting of the nasal cavity. If injured the opponent faints with profuse bleeding from the nostrils. He can be brought to normalcy by pressing the nasal pyramid (Nose).

Study of Simanta Marma

Seemantha [Simanta] Etymology—Seemantah [Simantah] Kesa vesha that area where the ladies divide the hair for tying. Description They are five in number and is included in the kalanthara pranahara marma. The situation of this marma is on the scalp. They correspond to the five important sutures of the skull. Injury to these marmas lead to insanity, fear and intellectual disturbances from trauma. They comes under the sandhi marma category. Prognosis is kalanthara pranahara. Its parimana is four anguli. 139

Surface Anatomy These marma are located on the all four sides of head. One needs sufficient knowledge of anatomy of skull to located them. Discussion The Simanta marma can be correlated to sutures, which are seen around the scalp. Hence two Tamil varmas falling into its anatomical vicinity are considered. Tamil varma sastra As the marma Simanta is corelated as sutures of the bone Seerum kolli could be cosidered in this area. Kolli means that which kills. depending upon the injury - the patient may even die, hence the name. Seeru Koli life-centre is located six inches below (back side) of the centre of the head. If Seeru kolli life-centre (varma) is affected, the victim will faint and also rotate the head sideways. The tongue folds up inwards and the saliva comes out of the mouth. Within a few days, the patient will have terrible cough and there will be spermatorrhoeae. Varmacuttiram says he grows a hunch on one side and chews with nothing in the mouth. This varma disorder should be treated within six hours of the affection. The eighth and twelfth day of injury is very crucial, if the patient survives he may be medically treated with success. Study of Utkshepa Marma

Utkshepa Marmam—This is derived from Kship dhatu which is prefixed by Ud = Urdhvam Ksipathi. That which lifts up. Description The situation of this marma is above the Sankha and near the hairy margin on the scalp on both sides of head. Injury to this marma by a foreign body keeps the person alive as long as it is kept within the wound or it falls down on its own accord, after the formation of the tissue which acts as a barrier between the foreign body and the injured tissue of the body. But if it is drawn out immediately after the injury, the person dies at once due to bleeding. (sudden loss of nervous energy prana.) They are two in number and its prognosis is Vishalyaghna in nature. Its ashraya is snayu Its bhuta structure is Vatha. Adhidevata is Brahma 132
