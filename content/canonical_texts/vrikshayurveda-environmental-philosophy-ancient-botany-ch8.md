3.6. Environment and Humanity: In the context of Vedic View

61 I.2.3.3 Environment Verses Human Being: In the context of Vedic view: Since time long the ancient Indian thinkers had a predominant idea about the relationship between man and nature. All living creatures including human beings are the product of nature. Unlike today, the ancient man was even happier being in nature. Thus, the relation between man and nature from time immemorial stood to be interdependent. The human-nature relationship, their interdependence and association with each other in the context of Vedic version therefore gets articulated through the divine embodiments and have been pronounced in the guise of rituals being sermonized at every step of human life repeatedly reminding us the need to sustain and foster the ecological balance in nature. Man has forever expressed his familiar feelings towards nature, as such always lived in close proximity to the natural environment. A reference can be drawn from Atharva-veda's Prthivi Sukta whereby man calls prthivi as mother. He says - 27 visvasvam mataram ausdhinam dhruvam bhumi prthivi dharmanadhrtam// 27. He further eulogies as mata bhumih putro aham prthivyah// - (Atharva-veda, XII, I, 12) >> 28 This means, "Earth is mother and I am the son of earth". However, Prthivi Sukta the part of Atharva-veda also named as Bhumisukta determines the inter relationship of man with his environment and his liability in preserving it. 3. Vrksayurveda texts found in Brihatsamhita by Varahamihira

Brhatsamhita has written by Varahamihira (C. 505 to 580 AD) deals with the topics of Vrikshayurveda in chapters 29, 53, 54, and 55. The texts on the aspect of Vrikshayurveda found in this book are not arranged in the same sequential form as that of Vrikshayurveda of Surapala, but are scattered in four different chapters as sated above. Chapter 53 of Brihat-vamhita mentions about the vastu vidya (on house building) consisting of 125 verses, out of which four verses (85 to 88) deals with the merits and demerits of trees planted around the house. The text advises to plant plaksa to the north, banyan to the east, udumbara to the south and asvattha to the west are auspicious and will bring prosperity (V.85). The text warns, not to grow thorny trees near the houses, as there will be fear for enemies. If you grow milky trees, there will be waste of wealth and if fruit trees, the children will die. The wood of these trees shall also be rejected (V.86).

95 If it be felt inconvenient to remove the several trees mentioned above, a number of superior trees shall be grown between them. These are punnaga, asoka, arista, bakula, panasa, sami and sala (V-87). Any spot where the ground is smooth and without holes, where the earth is sweet, fragrant and of bright appearance, and where there grow superior crops, trees and creepers that afford comfort to weary travelers, that plot is suitable to built a dwelling place (V-88). This chapter advices us to plant suitable trees near the building for environmental point of view. Chapter 54 consists of 125 verses deals very exhaustively with the subject of ground water (on under-currents) named as udakargal. But, the other two texts Vrksayurveda and Upavanvinoda treat the subject briefly. We find only 19 verses about ground water in Vrikshayurveda of Surapala and 53 verses in Upavanvinoda. The first five verses of Brihat-vamhita is about the eight under-currents and the mahasira. From verse 6 to 96 it deals with several indications leading to the discovery of under-currents. These under-currents are to be ascertained by the presence of ant hills and snake holes near trees of vetasa, jambu, nirgundi, badari, vibhitaka, udumbara, arjuna, kampillaka, sonaka, kusa, saptaparna, karanja, madhuka, tilaka, kadamba, kapittha, haridra, pilu, sami, palasa, durva, etc. and also

96 the depth, at which water is to be available is mentioned in the verses. While digging the earth the indicators are noted in the form of the nature of clay, gravel stone, sand, white soil, frog, snake, scorpion (V. 73), tortoise (V. 34 to 44), mongoose(nevala) (V. 32), fish (V- 10), red fish (V. 15) and other aquatic animals. At the time of digging, the colour of the soil also indicates the quantity of water underneath the ground. Brihat-vamhita also mentions that the wells must be situated in the north-east, which will lead to prosperity (V. 97-98). The other verses numbering from (100 to 110) the view of Manu are all mentioned about the under-currents and their indications. Verses from (111 to 117) are about the construction of wells with stones and the types of stones and their uses are described. The other verses are giving from (118 to 125) are about plantation of trees on the bank of the ponds for their safety, how to clean the muddy, bitter and brackish water into clear, sweet, fragrant with the help of arjuna, musta, usira, amalaka, the right time (lagna) the work of sinking wells to be started and the last two verses are about the worship of God Varuna at the time of construction of the wells. There is another chapter in Brihat-vamhita No. 55, on gardening which deals with science of plant life briefly. It contains 32 verses about

97 different aspects of plant life. The text advices to set-up pleasure gardens and plant shady trees on the sides of rivers, lacks and other water banks (V. 1). For garden soft soil is congenial to the growth of trees. Such a soil should be selected for the garden. So the sesamum plant should be grown in the soil, and when the plant begins to bear blossoms, it has to be crushed and mixed with the soil (V. 2). This verse teaches us how to improve the fertility of the soil. The trees arista, asoka, punnaga and sirisa are (mangalamaya) auspicious and there to be grown in the garden first or near the house (V. 3). The trees viz. panasa, plantain, jambu, lakuca, dadima, draksa, palivata, bijapuraka and atimuktaka shall be grown either by planting their roots after clipping them or their branches, smearing cow dung over the parts cut (V. 4 - 5). The text also mentions about the transplantation of trees in different seasons according to the stage of growth of seasons. Tree to be transplanted be smeared from the root to the steam with ghee, usira, sesamum, honey, vidanga, milk and cow dung, is known as samkramana growth (v. 6-7). The text also suggests watering of plants according to the requirement in different seasons (v. 9), and list out the trees thus to be planted on wet lands (v. 10, 11). The text mentions to maintain the best possible distance between two trees as 20 cubits also suggestively describes for one of 16 and 12 cubits as best,

98 passable and injurious respectively for the growth of trees (v. 12 - 13). There is a brief narrative of dangers, diseases and elements of plants and their treatments in verses 14 to 16. Diseases (roga) of searing of leaves, arrest of growth, drying of the branches, and oozing of sap are caused by exposure to cold, wind and heat. Their treatment is also prescribed in the text, as to scrape off or removed the effected parts dead from the tree with knife, smearing them with a paste of vidanga and ghee and nurturing them with water mixed with milk (v. 15). For increasing fruits and flowers (phala puspa samrddhi), it recommends sprinkling with a mixture of horse gram, black gram, sesamum seed, barley, stool of sheep's and goat kept for seven days (v. 16, 17, 18). The materials are to be mixed in a specified quantity as mentioned in verse 18. The seed before sowing is laid down in verse 19 to 20. The text of Brihat-vamhita also deals with horticultural wonders from verse 21 to 30. The special care for sowing and nurturing the seeds of tamarind, kapittha and some other seeds will grow as a creeper is the wonder for anyone. The special treatment and application of different fertilizers in different ways are to be followed (v. 21 - 26). The special method for plantation with a rapid growth and fruit bearing in the case of the seed of slesmataka says, soak any

99 seed in the water of ripe fruit of ankola and dry it in shade, repeat the operations seven times, rub the seed over with the dung of the buffalo, and keep it buried in the same dung for some time, then sow it in the soil drenched with the water of the coconut. The seed will grow and bear fruits in a day (v. 29 - 30). The last verse 31 prescribes the auspicious time for planting of trees and sowing of seed according to the movement of the moon through the asterisms as suggested by the sages with their inherent knowledge (v. 31). The text of Brihat-vamhita only deals with some of the important practices and methods of plant life. Chapter-XXIX of Brihat-vamhita contains 14 verses, known as kusumalata (on flowers and plants). Out of 14 verses, five verses are found to be similar with Vrikshayurveda of Surapala (v. 320 -324, Vrikshayurveda). It's upon an analysis it come to the light that the verses of Brihat-vamhita (chapter XXIX) and Vrksayurveda of Surapala, in its last chapter XIII the similar verses are articulated as follows:Brhatsamhita (Ch-XXIX) Verse 3 Verse - 4 Verse - 7 Vrksayurveda (Ch-XIII) Verse - 320 Verse - 321 Verse - 322 Verse - 11 Verse - 323

100 Verse 12 Verse - 324 From the above four chapters (29, 53, 54, 55) of Brihat-vamhita , we find that Varahamihira presents a summarized account of plants life with some important practices and methods. Only chapter 55 of Brihat-vamhita deals briefly with planting, soil, nourishment, diseases and elements, treatment and a few horticultural wonders in 31 verses.
