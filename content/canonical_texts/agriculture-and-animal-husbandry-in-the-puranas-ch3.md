## Part 8.2 - Contents of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa

Part 8.2 - Contents of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa

According to the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Brahmā described the Bhāgavata Purāṇa to his son Nārada, which had been transmitted to him by the glorious God. Nārada, who then illustrated it to Vyāsa.[1] The glorification of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa has been stated elaborately in the Bhāgavata-Māhātmya[2] section of the Padma Purāṇa. It is stated there that in the Kali-age, the holy text of Śrīmad Bhāgavata, would bring safety of the world. There is nothing higher to this Purāṇa that would cleanse the mind. It is acquired by good men through religious merit earned in many (earlier) existences.[3] Knowing the salvation of king Parīkṣit, the lotus-born one (kamalasaṃbhavaḥ) compared the holy texts and the great Purāṇas.

Among them Śrīmad Bhāgavata came to be superior on the earth.[4] By reciting and listening to it, a man goes to Viṣṇu's position. Listening to it for a year gives great happiness, dedication to it for a month brings luxury, and listening to it for a week gives salvation at all times.[5] The Śrīmadbhāgavata Purāṇa takes pleasure in itself.

It is manifested for the happiness of Bhakti, Jñāna and Vairāgya to Brahmā, by Kṛṣṇa. The four-versed (summary) of it appears to be the entire Brahman. That account was narrated to Nārada by Brahmā. Nārada narrated it to Vyāsadeva, to take away torment. By remembering it Bādarāyaṇa (Vyāsa) instantly became depressed; and in a manner delighting the soul, composed the work of the great name.[6] According to the Padma Purāṇa, the treatise called Śrīmad Bhāgavata comprises eighteen thousand verses and twelve skandhas(sections), and is in the form of a dialogue between Parīkṣit and Śuka.[7]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa itself too mentions that it contains eighteen thousands ślokas.[8] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa was revealed by Viṣṇu to god Brahmā at first.[9] It contains discussion on the value of detachment at the beginning, in the middle and at the end. Moreover, it contains several episodes exalting the sportive activities of Lord Hari.[10] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa is said to be the essence of all the Upaniṣads which is distinguished by uniqueness between the supreme Brahman and the Paramātman. Its only purpose is to secure Mokṣa.[11] One, who drinks the sweet nectar of the teachings of this Purāṇa, does not find pleasure in any other thing.[12] The Bhāgavata Purāṇa occupies the foremost position among all the other Purāṇas.The position is compared with that of the holiest river Gaṅgā among the other rivers, Lord Viṣṇu among the gods or god Śiva among the devotees of Viṣṇu.[13] Among the Purāṇas there is no Purāṇa which can be equal to the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.[14] It is a Purāṇa without any fault. It is dear to the devotees of Śrī Viṣṇu.[15] He who, on the full moon day of Bhādrapada, offers the Bhāgavata Purāṇa as a gift, attains to the highest abode.[16]

In the Bhāgavata Purāṇa the god has been glorified at every step, who abides in the entire universe.[17] Lord Hari who is the destroyer of all sins, who is Nārāyaṇa, the regulator of sense-organs and who is the defender of his devotees, has been glorified here, in this Purāṇa.[18] It has described the Supreme Brahma, who is the cause of creation. The topic of spiritual knowledge has also been discussed.[19] In its twelfth Skandha, the Bhāgavata Purāṇa itself mentions the merits of studying it.

According to statement mentioned therein, a Brāhmaṇa acquires spiritual knowledge, a Kṣatriya conquers sovereignty over the whole world, a Vaiśya becomes the lord of possessions and a Śūdra is freed of sins, on studying this Purāṇa.[20] He who narrates this Purāṇa every day or for a time of three hours or for a minute and he who listens this Purāṇa with devotion, purifies his inner soul.[21] Moreover, it added that one, who hears it on the twelfth or eleventh day of every fortnight of the lunar month, will be blessed with long life. One who reads it with dedication frees himself of all sins.[22] Reading this Purāṇa at the holy places like Puṣkara, Mathurā and Dvārakā, is said to be freed from the fear of Saṃsāra.[23] One who, with a pure and controlled mind, learns this Purāṇa, attains the supreme abode i.e. Mokṣa.[24]

In this regard Professor Wilson’s remark is worthy to mention. He thus views: The Bhāgavata is a work of great celebrity in India and exercises a more direct and powerful influence upon the opinions and feelings of the people than perhaps any other of the Purāṇas. It is placed in the fifth position in all the lists but the Padma-Purāṇa ranks it as the eighteenth, as the extracted substance of all the rest. According to the usual specification, it consists of eighteen thousand ślokas, distributed amongst three hundred and thirty-two chapters divided into twelve Skandhas or books.[25]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa[26] has ten topics in it. They are as follows—

1. Sarga (subtle creation)—It consists of the creation of the Bhūtas, Tanmātras, Indriyas, Ahaṃkāra and Mahat, and the appearance of Virāṭ Puruṣa.[27]
2 Visarga (gross creation)—the individual creation by Brahmā is called Visarga.[28]
3 Sthāna [law and order (ensured by God)]—it is the triumph of the lord in the maintenance of (the divine) law and order(in everything).[29]
4 Poṣaṇa (protection-welfare of all)—the grace of God is known as Poṣaṇa.[30]
5 Manvantara (the periods of Manu)—the duties of the rulers of Manvantaras.[31]
6 Ūti (material lust from Karmas)—the desire for karma(action) is called Ūti.[32]
7 Īśānukathā (accounts of God’s deeds)—the stories of the various incarnations and of the followers of Hari are known as Īśānukathā.[33]
8 Nirodha (physical annihilation)—the sleep of Hari and all individual souls in Pralaya.[34]
9 Mukti (liberation)—the continued perception of the identity of self and of Brahman.[35]
10 Āśraya (the last resort of the universe, the ultimate reality)—The Final Resort, Para-Brahman or Paramātman from creation or dissolution both proceed.[36]

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa contains twelve Skandhas. The first Skandha presents a discourse between Vyāsa and Nārada,[37] as well as accounts of Nārada.[38] The second Skandha describes what men are to do and what they are forbidden to do,[39] about the creation of the Universe-the Bhāgavata Purāṇa as related by Brahmā to Nārada,[40] description of the Virāṭ Puruṣa,[41] preservation by Līlā-Avatāras[42].The third Skandha describes about the creation of Bhāgavata as related by Maitreya to Vidura,[43] the ten-fold creation by Brahmā,[44] divisions of Kāla,[45] the descriptions of the various creations of Brahmā,[46] the first or the Svāyambhuva–Manvantara,[47] Deva and Deva-yoni creation.[48] Kapila, the founder of Sāṃkhya school is mentioned in the third Skandha as an incarnation of Viṣṇu, who instructs his mother Devāhuti about the yoga philosophy of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.[49] The Fourth Skandha describes about the genealogy of Manu and the Ṛṣis,[50] the story of Dhruva,[51] the story of Pṛthu, etc.[52] The fifth and the sixth Skandha describes about the Bhuvanakoṣa, Earth-chain,[53] story of Ajāmila,[54] the progeny of Dakṣa,[55] the story of Vṛtra,[56] etc. The seventh Skandha includes the account of Hiraṇyakaśipu and Prahlāda,[57] description of Varṇa and Āśrama,[58] etc. The eight Skandha gives description about the second to fourteenth Manvantaras. The ninth Skandha narrates about the Vaivasvata-Manvantara,[59] the dynasty of Ikṣvāku,[60] the Lunar dynasty,[61] etc. The tenth Skandha includes the description of the life of Kṛṣṇa. The love-scenes with the Gopīs have been described elaborately. Referring to the tenth Skandha Winternitz says that the tenth book is the most popular and most widely read by all. This book is translated into almost all popular Indian languages of India and is a favourite book of all classes of the Indian folk.[62] The eleventh Skandha describes about the destruction of the Yādavas and the death of Kṛṣṇa, and the twelfth Skandha encloses the usual predictions on the Kaliyuga and the destruction of the world.

## Part 8.3 - Contents of the Matsya Purāṇa

Part 8.3 - Contents of the Matsya Purāṇa

The Matsya Purāṇa is one of the 18 Mahā-Purāṇas consisting of two hundred and ninety one chapters as printed in the Ānanda Āśrama Edition, Poona.[1] The Matsya Purāṇa is a voluminous work which begins with the story of the great flood.Here Viṣṇu, in the form of a fish(Matsya) saves Manu alone. The conversation which took place between Manu and Viṣṇu, incarnated as a fish, forms the substance of the Purāṇa.[2] It is a conglomeration of chapters taken from various sources, especially the Vāyu Purāṇa and Viṣṇu Dharmottara Purāṇa.[3]

The Purāṇa, in which the lord speaks the glory of Narasiṃha to Vaivasvata Manu at the beginning of the Kalpa with a view to spread the Śrutis and in which the lord has described about the seven kalpas, is known as the Matsya Purāṇa. The Matsya Purāṇa contains fourteen thousand ślokas.[4] But according to the Agni Purāṇa the Matsya Purāṇa contains thirteen thousand verses.[5] All the characteristics of the Purāṇa occur in the Matsya Purāṇa. The sarga section of this Purāṇa describes about production of the grass, trees, creepers, shrubs, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas,[6] Apsaras, kinnaras and Gandharvas[7] etc. The pratisarga section describes about the various manvantaras, the stories of all the Manus and the rule of each one of them.[8] The Vaṃśa section is found to be dealt with the descriptions of the family and the sect of Sūrya, Soma and Pitṛs.[9] The features of the age of manvantaras has been described in the manvantara section.[10] The Vaṃśānucarita part of the Matsya Purāṇa includes descriptions about the ruling of different dynasties of Ikṣvākus and Bṛhadrathas,[11] Pradyuta, Śiśunāka, the Nanda, the Maurya, the Śuṅgas and the Kanvāyana dynasties.[12] The Matsya Purāṇa also gives the description of Bhāratavarṣa, Kiṃpuruṣa and Harivarṣa.[13] Regarding the name Bhāratavarṣa it is stated that the place in which one can achieves the svarga (heaven), mokṣa(emancipation) and the position between the two, is known as Bhāratavarṣa.[14] This Purāṇa again describes the Earth and its boundary with the seven Dvīpas and their extent and other measurements.[15] It also describes various incarnations of Viṣṇu such as Nṛsiṃha,[16] Vāmana,[17] Varāha,[18] etc. Description of Rājadharma and its inflicting punishments[19], the reigns of the Andhras, Yavanas and the Mlecchas are also described in this Purāṇa.[20]

There are descriptions about glorification of the sacred places of Allahabad (Prayāga Māhātmya),[21] Benares (Vārānasī and Avimukta-Māhātmya),[22] of the river Narmadā,[23] the duties of a king,[24] the ceremonies at the building of a house,[25] the excretion and dedication of statues of deities, temples and palaces,[26] the sixteen kinds of donations,[27] etc.

As far as religious content is concerned, the Matsya Purāṇa might be called Śaivite with just as much reason as it is classed as Viṣṇuite. Religious festivals of the Vaiṣṇavas are described side by side with those of the Śaivas, and both Viṣṇu and Śiva-legends are related.[28]

## Part 8.4 - Contents of the Vāyu Purāṇa

Part 8.4 - Contents of the Vāyu Purāṇa

The Vāyu Purāṇa obtains its name from Vāyu who is its promulgator. M.Winternitz in his A History of Indian Literature says that this Purāṇa appears in some lists, under the name of Śaiva or Śiva Purāṇa, a title which is given to the work because it is dedicated to the worship of god Śiva. As proclaimed by the Wind-god, Vāyu Purāṇa is quoted in the Mahābhārata as well as by the Harivaṃśa. It has been mentioned that the poet Bāṇa (about 625 A.D.) had read the Vāyu Purāṇa and that in this Purāṇa, the rule of the Guptas is described as it was in the 4th century A.D., making the work not later than 5th century A.D.[1]

The Vāyu Purāṇa itself declares that it was illustrated by Vāyu (Mātariśvā).[2] It was recited to the Brāhmaṇas by Vāyu.[3] It was expanded by Vāyu himself, who was employed in the beneficence of the people. He described it to the sages in the Naimiṣa forest.[4] In another verse, the Vāyu Purāṇa states that this Purāṇa was spoken by Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, who was the expounder of Brahman.[5] Again it is stated that previously this Purāṇa had been stated by Brahmā.[6] This sacred text was presented by Brahmā to Mātariśvā(Vāyu). From him it was acquired by Uśanas, from Uśanas it was acquired by Bṛhaspati, Bṛhaspati then spoke it to Savitṛ. Savitṛ passed it to Mṛtyu(Yama), Mṛtyu passed it to Indra, Indra passed it to Vasiṣṭha and Vasiṣṭha passed it to Sārasvata. Sārasvata passed it to Tridhāmā, Tridhāmā passed it to Śaradvata, Śaradvata passed it to Triviṣṭa and Triviṣṭa passed it to Antarikṣa.

Antarikṣa passed it to Varṣiṇ, Varṣiṇ passed it to Trayyāruṇa, Trayyāruṇa passed it to Dhanañjaya and he gave it to Kṛtañjaya. From Kṛtañjaya it was received by Tṛṇañjaya, he passed it to Bharadvāja, Bharadvāja passed it on to Gautama and Gautama gave it to Niryantara. Niryantara handed it over to Vājaśravas, Vājaśravas passed it on to Somaśuṣma, who gave it to Tṛṇabindu. Tṛṇabindu gave it to Dakṣa, Dakṣa passed it to Śakti, from Śakti it was heard by Parāśara, who was even in embryo then. From Parāśara it was received by Jātukarṇa, he passed it to Dvaipāyana and from Dvaipāyana, it was expanded to Śāṃśapāyana.[7]

The Vāyu Purāṇa elaborates the spirits of manes (Pitṛs) and their cults (Śrāddhas).[8] It’s one chapter is devoted to the art of song.[9] Although it is a Śaiva Purāṇa, but we find two chapters in this Purāṇa, even on Viṣṇu too.[10] Moreover, in the Gayā Māhātmya section, a great tribute is paid to Viṣṇu.[11]

The whole Vāyu Purāṇa is divided into two parts(Kāṇḍa).The second Kāṇḍa begins with the 62nd chapter.[12] There are one hundred and twelve chapters in this Purāṇa. It has twenty-three thousand Ślokas.[13]

This Purāṇa is divided into four Pādas—

Prakriyā Pāda,

Ānuṣaṅga Pāda,

Upodghāta Pāda, and

Upasaṃhāra Pāda.[14]

Leaving out the two opening chapters, the first section (Prakriyā Pāda) occupies only four chapters, with the general title of sṛṣṭiprakaraṇaṃ or the creation of world.[15]

The second section (Ānusaṅga Pāda) comprises 58 chapters of the book, beginning with the 7th chapter and ending with the 64th chapter.[16] It is the longest among the four sections. Creation of gods and men, cycles of Manus, practice of Yoga, descriptions of thirty-three Kalpas of the duration of the world(the last one of these kalpas i.e., Śveta Kalpa, in which we are now said to have), incarnations of Mahādeva, and the curse of Dakṣa, descendants of Ṛṣis, of Agni and other Devas, of Svāyambhuva Manu, of Prajāpati, of Pṛthu, the descriptions of Jambudvīpa, the four yugas, the sacred tīrthas, the continents of Ketumāla, Bhāratavarṣa, Kiṃpuruṣa and Plakṣa are also met with in this section.

The third section (Upodghāta Pāda) contains 34 chapters (65-99).[17] This section gives the description of the races of Prajāpati, Kāśyapa, Varuṇa, Vaivasvata Manu, Viṣṇu, and the Gandharvas. Moreover, there are accounts of Śrāddhas to which more than ten chapters are devoted. Descendants of Soma and Yadu race and the dynasties of kali age are also met with there.

The last section (Upasaṃhāra Pāda) consists of chapters 100 to the end.[18] Periods of seven Manus[19] are described here. Moreover, descriptions of periodical destructions and regenerations[20] have also been elaborated. The concluding chapters on the greatness of Gayā seem to be out of place and indicate a definite interpolation.[21] The Vāyu Purāṇa states that, a Brāhmaṇa, who may understand the four Vedas along with the six Vedāṅgas and Upaniṣads, but if he does not understand this Purāṇa then he may be called as an inexpert.[22]

## Part 9 - Justification of Studying the Five Purāṇas

Part 9 - Justification of Studying the Five Purāṇas

The title of the thesis Agriculture and Animal Husbandry in the Purāṇas-A Study itself indicates that the study should have included the whole eighteen Purāṇas. But among the Eighteen Mahāpurāṇas the study of only five Purāṇas is included in this dissertation. They are the Agni Purāṇa, the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, the Matsya Purāṇa, the Vāyu Purāṇa and the Viṣṇu Purāṇa. The reason of taking these aforesaid five Purāṇas is mainly lack of time and space. Due to the limitation of time during my research period, the study of the aforesaid five Purāṇas is considered.

Because, it would not have been possible for me to complete the study of the whole eighteen Purāṇas in just five years. The contents of the whole eighteen Purāṇas are so vast, so it would have taken more than five years to have a thorough look into the whole eighteen Purāṇas. Furthermore, these five Purāṇas were thought to be containing more data on agriculture and animal husbandry than in the remaining thirteen Purāṇas, comparatively. In this matter my Preceptors guided me. So following their suggestions I selected to do research covering these five Purāṇas.

Moreover, the limitation of the length of the thesis also compelled me to take these five Purāṇas mainly. The length of the thesis should be 300 pages according to the Ph.D. guidelines. A candidate must not cross this limit. I was also bound to keep this rule in view.
